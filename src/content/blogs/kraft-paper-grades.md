# Virgin vs Recycled Kraft Paper in Corrugated Boxes

"100% recycled" sounds like the responsible choice, and for many boxes it is. But paper made from recovered fibre behaves differently from paper made from fresh pulp, and the difference shows up in humid godowns, heavy stacks and food packaging.

This guide explains the two kinds of paper, where each belongs in a box, and what to put in a spec.

## What kraft paper is

Kraft paper takes its name from the German word for strength. It is made by cooking wood chips in chemicals so the lignin that binds the fibres is dissolved, leaving long cellulose fibres that are then formed into paper. The result is strong, tear-resistant and naturally brown.

Corrugated boxes are built from layers of paper: **liners** on the faces and a **fluting medium** in the wave. Each layer can be a different grade.

## Virgin kraft

Virgin kraft is made from fresh pulp that has not been used in paper before.

- **Long, strong fibres**, so it generally gives higher strength for the same weight.
- **More consistent quality** from roll to roll.
- **Better in humidity.** Strength is lost in damp conditions by every paper, but long fibres and the sizing used on kraft liners usually hold up better.
- **Cleaner, lighter surface** in bleached or white-top grades, which suits printing.
- **Usually costs more.**

## Recycled kraft

Recycled paper is made from recovered paper, such as old corrugated boxes, which is pulped, cleaned and re-formed.

- **Shorter fibres**, because fibres get shorter each time paper is recycled. Fibres can go round several times (figures such as four to seven are often quoted) before they are too short to use.
- **More variation** in strength and colour.
- **Lower strength for the same weight**, which is made up for by heavier paper or more layers.
- **Usually costs less** and keeps paper out of waste streams.

Recycled content is a real environmental advantage, and a recycled box can be the right box. The point is to match it to the job.

## Names you will hear

Terms are not fully standardised, so ask your supplier what exactly they mean. These are common meanings.

| Term | Usually means |
|---|---|
| **Kraft liner** | Liner made mostly from virgin kraft pulp, strongest of the common grades |
| **Semi-kraft** | A blend of virgin and recycled fibre |
| **Test liner or recycled liner** | Liner made from recovered paper |
| **White top** | A liner with a white surface for better print, over a standard base |
| **Fluting medium** | The wavy layer, often semi-chemical or recycled paper |

Brown colour does not tell you whether a paper is virgin or recycled.

## Where each belongs in a box

Many boxes mix grades, spending money where it matters:

- **Outer liner:** strength and print surface count most. Virgin kraft or white top for printed or heavy-duty boxes.
- **Fluting:** hidden, so it is often a lower-cost grade.
- **Inner liner:** often a lower-cost grade, unless the box needs it to be strong or clean.

A spec that lists each layer, its GSM and BF, and its grade is easy to compare. See [GSM and BF in corrugated boxes](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength).

## When recycled is fine, and when to ask for virgin

| Situation | Leans towards |
|---|---|
| Light goods, dry storage, short journey | Recycled or mixed grades are usually fine |
| Heavy or tall-stacked boxes, long storage | Stronger grades, virgin on the liners |
| Monsoon or cold-room storage | Virgin liners and a test sample |
| Printed retail boxes | White top or virgin kraft for print quality |
| Export | Whatever the buyer specifies; ask for it in writing |
| Food contact | Discuss with your supplier and food-safety adviser |

Always back the choice with a sample test: stack a real box with a real load.

## Food contact

Recycled paper can carry traces of materials from its earlier life, so direct food contact needs care, and virgin or specially made papers are often preferred. Food-contact packaging in India falls under FSSAI's packaging rules, and the food business has to use suitable material. Tell your supplier what the food is and whether it touches the board, and ask what documentation they can provide. Our [food-grade boxes](/products/food-grade) page lists what we offer.

## Sustainability: the honest picture

- **Recycled paper** saves fresh pulp and diverts waste, but needs de-inking and cleaning, and the fibre cannot be recycled for ever.
- **Virgin kraft** comes from managed or farmed wood, which is renewable if sourced responsibly. Look for forest certification (such as FSC or PEFC) if it matters to you, and ask for proof. We do not claim certified paper unless we can show it.
- **The lightest box that works** is usually the most sustainable one: a box that fails creates waste of the product, the box and the reshipment.
- **Coatings and laminates** can make recycling harder, so print and finish choices matter too.

## Common myths

- **"Recycled is always weaker."** Heavier or better-graded recycled paper can do many jobs. Test it.
- **"Virgin is always better."** Only where its strength or cleanliness is needed.
- **"Brown means recycled."** Colour says nothing about source.
- **"A paper can be recycled for ever."** Fibres shorten each cycle.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is virgin kraft stronger than recycled?
Generally, for the same weight, yes. Strength varies by grade, so compare BF and board test figures.

### Can I use recycled paper for food boxes?
It depends on the food, how it touches the board and the grade. Ask your supplier and food-safety adviser before you decide.

### Does recycled paper cost less?
Usually. The gap depends on grade and market, so ask for quotes on both.

### What should I write in a spec?
List each layer with GSM, BF and grade, for example "top liner 150 GSM, BF 18, kraft".
