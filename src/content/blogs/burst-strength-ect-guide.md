# Burst Strength vs ECT: Which Box Test Should You Ask For?

Your supplier quotes "14 kg/cm² burst". Your pallets still crush at the bottom. Both facts can be true, because burst strength and edge crush (ECT) are different tests, and stacking depends mainly on ECT.

This guide explains what each test measures, the units people quote, and which figures to ask for in a spec.

## The two tests in one minute

| | Burst strength | Edge crush test (ECT) |
|---|---|---|
| **Asks** | How much pressure does it take to rupture the board? | How much load can a strip of board carry on its edge before it buckles? |
| **Think of it as** | Resistance to bursting and puncture | The strength of the board when used as a wall |
| **Matters most for** | Rough handling, sharp corners, internal pressure | Stacking and storage |
| **Units** | kg/cm² (or psi) | kN/m, kg/cm or lb/in |

Neither test replaces the other. Many buyers need a sensible level of both.

## Burst strength

A circle of board is clamped over a rubber diaphragm. Pressure rises until the board ruptures, and the peak pressure is the burst strength. It is also called the **Mullen test** after the machine.

- **Units:** kg/cm² in India. 1 kg/cm² is about 14 psi, so a "200 psi test" board is about 14 kg/cm².
- **Why it matters:** it reflects how the board resists puncture and bulging. It is also tied to paper BF: see [GSM and BF](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength).
- **What it does not tell you:** how a box performs under a stack. Two boards with the same burst can have different edge crush.

## Edge crush test (ECT)

A narrow strip of board is stood on its edge between two plates, with the flutes vertical, and compressed until it fails. The load per unit of edge length is the ECT.

- **Units:** kN/m or N/mm are the same number. Many Indian reports quote kg/cm, which is almost the same value: 1 kg/cm is about 0.98 kN/m. US boxes carry ratings such as "32 ECT", which are in **lb/in**: 32 lb/in is about 5.6 kN/m. Always check which unit a figure is in before comparing.
- **Why it matters:** ECT is the board's strength as a wall, and it is the main input in the usual formula for how much a whole box can carry.

## From ECT to stacking strength

A whole box's compression strength (BCT) is estimated with the McKee formula:

> BCT ≈ 5.87 × ECT × √(board thickness × box perimeter)

Notice what is in it: edge crush, thickness and perimeter. Burst strength is not there. That is why a high-burst, low-ECT board can disappoint at the bottom of a pallet.

The result is a laboratory number for a new, dry box under a short load. Real stacks are hotter, damper, longer and rougher, so engineers divide by a safety factor. [Will my box stack?](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide) works through an example, and the designer's strength guidance applies the same method to your size.

## Which should you ask for?

| Situation | Ask for |
|---|---|
| Stacking in a godown, pallets, containers | **ECT**, plus ply and flute |
| Courier parcels, rough handling, sharp products | **Burst**, plus ECT |
| Printed retail cartons that are not stacked tall | Burst or the paper spec (GSM and BF) |
| Anything critical or high-value | **Both**, on the actual board you will be sent |

A simple spec line looks like this: "5-ply, B + C flute, liners 150 GSM / BF 18, ECT not less than X kN/m, burst not less than Y kg/cm²". Ask your supplier what values they can reliably meet for your ply and paper grades.

## Test methods and standards

Both tests are standardised, so figures from different labs can be compared if the method is the same. ISO 2759 covers burst strength of board, and ISO 3037 covers edge crush. Indian labs and buyers also use national and trade test methods, and some buyers set their own limits. When you receive a figure, ask which method and which unit it was measured in, and on which sample: a paper layer, a board strip or a finished box.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Higher is always better."** Past the point you need, extra strength is cost and weight.
- **"Burst and ECT convert to each other."** There are rough correlations, but no exact conversion: they measure different failures.
- **"The box says 14 kg/cm², so it is 14."** A printed rating is only as good as the testing behind it. Ask for a test figure or a sample.
- **"One test means every batch is the same."** Paper varies. For important jobs, ask how batches are checked.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good ECT for a shipping box?
It depends on box size, load and stack. Use the designer's strength guidance for a starting point and confirm with test data for your board. Single wall boards are often in the range of a few kN/m, and double wall boards are higher.

### Is burst strength or ECT more important?
For stacking, ECT. For puncture and rough handling, burst. For most buyers, a sensible level of both.

### What does "200 psi test" mean?
It is an older US way to rate board by burst strength, about 14 kg/cm². Newer US ratings use ECT in lb/in.

### Can I test boxes myself?
A simple trial is useful: pack the real product, load the stack for the real time, and watch for bulging or leaning. For measured values, use a lab.
