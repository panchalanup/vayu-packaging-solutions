# GSM and BF in Corrugated Boxes: What the Numbers Mean

You ask three suppliers for a "3-ply box" and get three prices. Often the boxes are not the same: the papers inside have different **GSM** (weight) and **BF** (bursting factor, a strength rating). Until you compare those, you are comparing prices, not boxes.

This guide explains both terms, how the weight of the finished board is worked out, and what to ask for so quotes can be compared fairly.

**Next step:** see how paper weight feeds into price in the [3D Box Designer's estimate](/box-designer), or [send us your spec for a quote](/quote).

## GSM: grams per square metre

GSM is the weight of one square metre of paper. A sheet of paper that is 1 m by 1 m and weighs 150 g is 150 GSM. Heavier paper has more fibre, which generally means a stiffer, stronger sheet and a higher cost.

You can check a sample at home: cut a piece exactly 10 cm × 10 cm (0.01 m²), weigh it, and multiply the weight in grams by 100. That is only a rough check on a single layer, since a finished board has several layers glued together.

## BF: bursting factor

GSM tells you how heavy the paper is. **BF** tells you how strong it is for its weight. It is the paper's burst strength divided by its GSM, scaled by 1,000:

> BF = burst strength (kg/cm²) × 1,000 ÷ GSM

So a 150 GSM paper that bursts at 2.7 kg/cm² has a BF of 18. Two papers of the same GSM can have different BF, and the higher-BF one is the stronger paper. This is why a quote that gives only "150 GSM" is incomplete. Ask for GSM **and** BF for each layer, for example "150 GSM / BF 18".

## How the board weight is worked out

A finished board has a **top liner**, a **fluting medium** and a **bottom liner** (more for 5-ply and 7-ply). Do not simply add the three GSM numbers, because the fluting medium is **corrugated into waves**, so it uses more paper than a flat sheet of the same width. The extra is called the **take-up factor**. It depends on the flute, and is roughly 1.3 to 1.5 for common flutes.

For a 3-ply board with a B flute:

| Layer | Paper GSM | Take-up | Weight in the board |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top liner | 150 | 1.0 | 150 |
| Fluting (B flute) | 120 | about 1.3 | about 156 |
| Bottom liner | 150 | 1.0 | 150 |
| **Board weight** | | | **about 456 GSM** |

If a quote lists "150 / 120 / 150", the board weighs more than 420 GSM. When you compare suppliers, compare paper specs layer by layer, not one total.

## What GSM changes, and what it does not

- **Higher GSM and BF in the liners** raise burst strength and edge crush strength (ECT), and with them stacking strength.
- **Fluting GSM** helps stiffness and flat crush.
- **Ply and flute** often matter more than GSM alone: a 5-ply board is much stiffer than a heavier 3-ply. See [single, double and triple wall](/blogs/corrugated-wall-differences).
- **Paper source matters too.** Virgin and recycled papers of the same GSM do not perform the same: see [virgin vs recycled kraft](/blogs/kraft-paper-grades).

There is no neat "twice the GSM, twice the strength" rule, and any table that promises exact strength from GSM is guessing. What you can rely on is the test: ask for ECT or burst figures for the board you are buying. [Burst strength vs ECT](/blogs/burst-strength-ect-guide) explains the two.

## Typical specifications in the trade

Specs vary, so treat these as starting points to discuss, not standards.

| Board | Liner GSM (each) | Fluting GSM (each) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-ply | 120 to 180 | 100 to 150 | Light to medium goods |
| 5-ply | 120 to 200 | 100 to 150 | Heavier goods, stacking |
| 7-ply | 150 to 200+ | 120 to 150 | Heavy and bulky goods |

## How to compare quotes fairly

Ask each supplier for the same list:

1. **Ply and flute** (for example 5-ply, B + C).
2. **GSM and BF of each layer**, and whether each is virgin kraft, semi-kraft or recycled.
3. **Board test figures**: ECT and burst for the board, and the test method.
4. **Box style and inside size.**
5. **Print and finish.**

If the supplier cannot or will not tell you the layers, you cannot tell what you are buying. A sample you can weigh and test is the best check.

## Why heavier is not always better

More paper means more cost and more weight in the courier bill, and in some cases a higher environmental load. The aim is the **lightest board that does the job**: strong enough for your stack and journey, no stronger. The designer's strength guidance helps you find that point for your size and weight. ## Frequently asked questions

### What GSM is a normal box?
There is no single normal. Liner GSM commonly ranges from about 120 to 200, with the fluting medium lighter. The right spec depends on the box size, load and stacking.

### What is BF in corrugated paper?
BF, or bursting factor, is the paper's burst strength per 1,000 GSM. It measures how strong the paper is for its weight, so a higher BF at the same GSM is a stronger paper.

### Is higher GSM always stronger?
Usually, but BF, ply, flute, paper source and humidity also matter. Compare board test figures, not GSM alone.

### Can I check GSM myself?
You can weigh a 10 cm × 10 cm sample of a single layer and multiply by 100. For a finished board, ask the supplier for the paper spec and test results.
