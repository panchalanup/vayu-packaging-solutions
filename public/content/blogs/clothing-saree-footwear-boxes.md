# Saree, Clothing and Footwear Boxes: Styles and Sizes

A saree is a long, soft, expensive thing that must reach a customer unwrinkled. A pair of sandals is a light, awkward shape that must not get crushed on a shelf. A kid's frock must look lovely when the box opens. Clothing and footwear are soft, varied products, and one generic carton rarely suits them.

Our earlier guide on [apparel and footwear mailers and shoe boxes](/blogs/apparel-footwear-mailer-shoe-boxes) covers the basics. ## The problem: soft products, too many shapes

- **Folded size varies.** A cotton shirt, a silk saree and a woollen stole fold to very different thicknesses.
- **Presentation sells.** A saree or a handloom piece is a gift as often as a purchase; the opening should feel considered.
- **Shipping is hard on boxes.** Courier handling crushes thin boxes, and returns multiply the damage.
- **Footwear stacks.** Shoe boxes are stacked floor to ceiling in shops and godowns, so they must hold up.
- **Many sizes.** Garments come in sizes, shoes in widths and lengths. Keeping the range manageable matters.

## Garments: a mailer or a window mailer

A mailer is the best all-round garment box: it ships flat, folds quickly, closes without tape at the front and prints well inside and out. The **window mailer** adds a clear film window in the lid, so the customer (or shelf buyer) sees the product. It suits children's clothing, patterned fabrics and anything where colour is the selling point.

**Sizing a garment box:** fold the garment the way you will pack it and measure the folded length, width and height. Add a little room for tissue paper, a card and a hang tag. Remember that a stack of folded clothes is springy, so a box that is a bit shallow will bulge and may not close flat.

## Sarees: wide, flat and gift-worthy

A saree folded for sale is a wide, flat shape: long and fairly narrow, but shallow. The **saree box** template uses a **telescope box** (a base and a slightly larger lid) at 38 x 28 x 8 cm in gold-coloured 3-ply B-flute board with a white inside. The lid lifts straight off, which feels considered, and the wide top surface carries a name, a monogram or a graphic.

**Planning tips**

- **Fold first, then measure.** Different fabrics fold differently; silk is thinner than a heavy weave. Measure your own folded saree and blouse piece.
- **Leave room for tissue** and a note or care card.
- **Use the lid for the brand.** A short word such as "SAREE" with your label name looks elegant; the template uses a saree graphic and lettering in a serif font.
- **Use the inside.** A colour on the inside of the lid or base (white, cream or a rich shade) completes the reveal.
- **For weddings and festivals**, plan a few colours and a matching hamper box; see [festive gift and hamper boxes](/blogs/festive-gift-hamper-boxes).

## Footwear: shoe boxes, sandal boxes and slide boxes

A shoe box is a stack-bearing box, not just a container. In a shop or godown, boxes sit under many others, so the lid and base walls do real work. The **shoe box** template is a two-piece telescope box at 33 x 20 x 12 cm in white 3-ply B-flute; the **sandal or slipper box** is 30 x 15 x 9 cm.

**Choosing a shoe box:**

1. **Size from your largest pair in each range.** Measure the shoe with its tissue or stuffing and add clearance. Many brands keep two or three box sizes across the range rather than one per shoe size.
2. **Choose a board that stacks.** 3-ply is common; use the **Price & order** tab's strength guide to check stack height for your weight. For heavy boots or long stacks, step up. See [will my box stack?](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide).
3. **Set the lid depth.** A full-depth lid is stronger; a shallow lid is cheaper. Move the lid depth slider and watch the lid lift off.
4. **Put the shoe size on the end panel**, because shops stack boxes with the end facing out. Add a short size line in **Customize**.

## Tuck-end cartons for small clothing items

A **tuck-end carton** is a tube with a flap at each end that tucks in, closing without glue or tape. It fits socks, handkerchiefs, scarves, innerwear and accessories, and it ships flat. ## Branding ideas for clothing and footwear

- **One colour, one idea.** A single logo plus a short line prints cleanly and costs less than a full-colour wrap.
- **Pattern fill** is a cheap way to add richness. Repeat a small graphic across the lid, or choose a stripe or dot pattern.
- **Print the inside.** The surface a customer sees on opening is the best branding space you have.
- **Use plain words only.** Use "Handmade", "New" or "Gift". Do not print certifications or origin claims you cannot back up.
- **Keep text clear of folds and windows.** The print checks warn you.

## Frequently asked questions

### What box is best for shipping clothes?

A mailer or window mailer ships flat, closes without tape at the front and packs fast. Add a plain outer carton or a courier bag if the journey is rough.

### Can I add a window to a clothing box?

Yes. The window mailer includes one, and some other styles offer it as a switch in the Cut-outs section. The model shows a clear film, and the dieline has the cut line.

### How do I choose a saree box size?

Fold the saree the way you will sell it and measure the folded length, width and height, then add room for tissue. The saree box template starts at 38 x 28 x 8 cm inside; adjust it to your fold.

### Are slide boxes and tuck-end boxes made of corrugated board?

The designer models them in thin corrugated board. They are also commonly made from paperboard or rigid board; tell us what you prefer.

### Can I use one box for several shoe sizes?

Many brands do, using two or three sizes across the range. Size from your largest pair in each group.
