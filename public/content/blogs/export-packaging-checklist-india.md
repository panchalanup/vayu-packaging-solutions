# Export Packaging Checklist: Cartons, Pallets and Marks

Export packaging is mostly a set of questions you answer before you pack: what your importer requires, what your forwarder and carrier require, and what official rules apply to your product and destination. This checklist covers cartons, pallets, marks, wood packaging and paperwork in plain language, and says who decides each item.

## The one rule: ask three sources

Packaging rules for exports come from three places, and a carton maker is none of them.

| Source | What it decides | Where to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Your importer** | Carton size and weight limits, marks, labels, barcodes, language, pallet type, material preferences | The purchase order and a direct question |
| **Your forwarder or carrier** | Pallet and container loading, height and weight limits, moisture precautions, documents | Your freight forwarder or customs house agent |
| **Official sources** | Customs, plant-quarantine and product rules for India and the destination | Indian customs and trade authorities, the destination's authority, and the IPPC for wood packaging |

Get the importer's requirements in writing before you order cartons. A change after printing costs more than a question before it.

## The checklist

### Cartons

- [ ] **Packed weight per carton.** Weigh a packed sample. Weight decides the board ([how many kg can a box hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold)).
- [ ] **Board and ply from the load.** As a planning guide only, about 8 kg for 3-ply, 20 kg for 5-ply and 40 kg for 7-ply. Export cartons are often 5-ply or 7-ply ([export cartons](/blogs/export-cartons-5-ply-7-ply)).
- [ ] **Inside size, stated as inside.** Say "inside" on every order ([inside versus outside](/blogs/box-measurements-guide)).
- [ ] **Style.** A full overlap carton for heavy or long items; a regular slotted carton for lighter ones ([box styles in 3D](/blogs/box-styles-in-3d)).
- [ ] **Carton weight limit for handling.** Ask the importer or carrier whether they set a maximum weight per carton.
- [ ] **Stacking.** Work out the stack height each carton must bear at the bottom of a pallet and a container ([stacking strength guide](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide)).
- [ ] **Moisture.** Board loses strength when damp, and a sea voyage can mean damp conditions. Ask your forwarder what to expect and your box maker what is available.
- [ ] **Test one.** Pack a real sample and test it with the contents.

### Marks and labels

- [ ] **Importer's mark requirements.** Names, references, destination, carton numbers: the importer decides.
- [ ] **Weights and dimensions.** Gross and net weight per carton are commonly shown; confirm the format.
- [ ] **Country of origin.** Whether and how to mark it depends on the destination; check with the importer.
- [ ] **Handling marks.** Common ones are this way up, fragile and keep dry. Graphical handling symbols are standardised internationally, so use the usual pictures, not your own.
- [ ] **Barcodes or labels** if the importer asks. Leave a clear, flat area for them.
- [ ] **Same marks on two sides**, so a handler sees one whichever way the carton sits.

### Pallets

- [ ] **Pallet type and size.** A footprint of 120 x 100 cm is commonly used, but your importer and carrier decide; confirm before you design cartons to divide it.
- [ ] **Carton layout.** Pick a carton footprint that divides the pallet with little wasted floor ([standard carton sizes and pallet fit](/blogs/standard-corrugated-box-sizes-india)).
- [ ] **Height limit.** The carrier sets how high a pallet can be loaded.
- [ ] **Edge protection and wrap.** Corner or edge boards, stretch film and strapping keep the load tight; ask what your forwarder requires.
- [ ] **No overhang.** Cartons that stick out past the pallet edge are easily crushed.

### Wood packaging (ISPM 15)

- [ ] **Which wood do you use?** ISPM 15 is an international standard for **wood packaging material** such as pallets, crates and dunnage used in international trade. It asks for an approved treatment and a mark on the wood.
- [ ] **Cartons.** Paper and corrugated board are generally outside its scope, and so is processed wood such as plywood. Confirm with your forwarder and the importing country's rules, because countries can add their own.
- [ ] **Ask your pallet supplier** for treated and marked pallets, and keep the paperwork your forwarder asks for.
- [ ] **Source of truth.** The standard is published by the IPPC; the destination's plant-quarantine authority has the final say.

### Container and loading

- [ ] **Carton size that fits the container.** Work out cartons per pallet and pallets per container ([CBM and container guide](/blogs/cbm-calculator-cartons-per-container)).
- [ ] **Weight per container.** Total packed weight must stay within the limits your forwarder gives you.
- [ ] **No gaps.** A loose load shifts in transit. Ask your forwarder about bracing and dunnage.
- [ ] **Photos.** Photograph the packed pallet and the loaded container for your records.

### Documents

- [ ] **Packing list that matches the pallet.** Carton count, packed weights and dimensions on the list should match what is on the pallet.
- [ ] **Everything else.** Your forwarder or customs house agent will list the documents for your product and destination. Do not rely on a generic list.

## Worked example: an export carton size

Say your importer wants cartons that fit a 120 x 100 cm pallet footprint with no overhang. A carton of 40 x 30 cm footprint fits 3 along the 120 cm side (3 x 40 = 120) and 3 along the 100 cm side (3 x 30 = 90, with 10 cm spare). Turning it the other way gives only 4 x 2 = 8. The best simple grid is 9 cartons per layer, using 9 x 1,200 = 10,800 of 12,000 cm2, so 90% of the floor.

A 40 x 25 cm footprint does better: 3 x 4 = 12 cartons a layer and no floor left over, as in the table of pallet-friendly footprints in [standard carton sizes](/blogs/standard-corrugated-box-sizes-india). Check your own carton in the [CBM calculator](/tools/cbm-calculator) before you commit to a size. These are outside carton sizes, so add the board thickness to your inside size.

## Common export packaging mistakes

- **Ordering cartons before asking the importer.** Marks, sizes and materials change after printing.
- **Mixing inside and outside sizes** between the carton order and the pallet plan.
- **A carton that passes on a bench but not under a stack.** The bottom carton carries every carton above it.
- **Unprotected edges under strapping.** The strap crushes the board.
- **Untreated wood pallets** where the importing country needs ISPM 15 treated wood. Ask first.
- **A packing list that does not match the pallet.** It slows clearance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do corrugated cartons need ISPM 15 treatment?

ISPM 15 covers wood packaging material such as pallets and crates. Paper and corrugated board are generally outside its scope, but countries can set their own rules, so confirm with your forwarder and the importing country's authority.

### Which ply is best for export cartons?

It depends on packed weight, stacking and the journey. As a planning guide, 5-ply is common for mid-weight cartons and 7-ply for heavy ones, but test a packed sample. See our [export cartons guide](/blogs/export-cartons-5-ply-7-ply).

### What should be printed on an export carton?

Your importer decides. Commonly requested items are names, references, destination, carton number, weights, dimensions, country of origin and handling marks. Get the list in writing.

### What pallet size should I plan for?

A 120 x 100 cm footprint is commonly used, but confirm with your importer and carrier. Choose a carton footprint that divides it with little wasted floor.

### Who tells me the rules for my destination?

Your importer, your forwarder or customs house agent, and the official authorities for India and the destination. A carton supplier can help with the carton, not with the rules.
