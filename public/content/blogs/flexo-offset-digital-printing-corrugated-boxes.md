# Flexo, Offset or Digital: Printing on Corrugated Boxes

Choose by quantity and artwork detail. Flexo prints one to three colours straight onto the board and is usually economical for thousands of boxes. Offset is printed on paper laminated to the board, for photographic retail boxes. Digital needs no plates and suits short runs and changing artwork. Ask for quotes on two routes.

## The three routes in one table

| | Flexo | Offset (litho-laminated) | Digital |
|---|---|---|---|
| How it works | Flexible plates, one per colour, print ink directly on the board | Artwork is printed on paper by offset litho, then the paper is laminated onto the board | Inkjet prints the image straight from a file, with no plates |
| Best at | Logos, text, solid colours, one to three colours | Fine detail, photographs, smooth gradients, full-colour retail boxes | Short runs, many versions, names or variable artwork |
| Weaker at | Fine lines and photographs on rough board, tight multi-colour registration | Short runs, price, speed, extra steps | Very large runs, where cost per box stays flat |
| Setup | A plate per colour, a one-time cost | Printing plates plus lamination setup | No plates; file preparation |
| Cost per box as quantity grows | Falls clearly | Falls, from a higher starting point | Falls less |
| Typical use | Shipping cartons, simple branded boxes | Gift, cosmetic and premium retail boxes | Samples, pilots, personalised boxes |

Some makers also **pre-print**, where the liner paper is printed on the reel before it is made into board, which is another way to get higher-quality flexo. Ask whether it is offered.

---

## Why order size changes the answer

The costs that matter are the **setup** (plates, dies, file preparation) and the **cost to run each box**. Setup is paid once, however many boxes you order. A route with high setup and low run cost wins as the quantity grows; a route with no setup wins when the quantity is small.

An illustrative sum, in cost units and not prices:

- Route A (flexo-style): 3,000 setup + 4 per box.
- Route B (digital-style): 0 setup + 9 per box.
- Break-even: 3,000 / (9 - 4) = **600 boxes**. Below 600, B costs less; above it, A does.

Real numbers move the break-even, sometimes a lot. The point is the shape: ask each maker for setup and per-box cost for your quantity, and compare totals.

## Artwork decides as much as quantity

Two jobs of the same size can need different routes:

- **A one-colour logo on kraft.** Flexo is a natural fit: it is a solid shape, and kraft board gives it a good rustic look. Fewest plates, lowest cost.
- **A photograph of the product on a white box.** Flexo on corrugated may not hold the detail. Offset-laminated or digital is the usual answer.
- **Different names or flavours on each box.** Digital avoids a plate per version.
- **Three brand colours that must touch.** Ask about registration. Colours that must line up exactly are harder than colours that sit apart.
- **A small logo with thin lines.** Fine lines on rough board can fill in. Ask for the minimum line weight.

Board matters too: a white-top liner gives brighter colours than brown kraft, which dulls them. See [kraft paper grades](/blogs/kraft-paper-grades).

## Keep cost down in the artwork

1. **Fewer colours.** In flexo each colour is a plate. The designer's estimate includes a one-time plate cost for each print colour. See [what drives the cost per box](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide).
2. **Tint artwork to one colour.** In the designer, [cutouts](/blogs/how-to-add-cutouts-box-design) can be recoloured to a single brand colour and patterns repeated, which gives a designed look on a one-colour print.
3. **Keep to the safe area.** The designer's print checks flag artwork within 0.5 cm of a fold or edge, images below 150 dpi at print size and text under 8 pt. Fixing these before the file reaches the printer saves a proof round. <!-- VERIFY-LATER[BLOG-664]: the 150 dpi, 0.5 cm and 8 pt thresholds are the designer's own warning levels (printChecks.ts, DSG-62) and may differ from the print room's limits. -->
4. **Reuse a plate.** The same logo on several box sizes can use the same plate; ask.

## Three example jobs

These are illustrations of the reasoning, not quotes.

| Job | What matters | Routes to ask about |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 shipping cartons with a one-colour logo on kraft | Low cost, simple artwork, solid colour | One-colour flexo; a stamp or label if you are below the minimum |
| 3,000 cosmetic mailers with a photograph and soft gradients | Surface finish and detail matter more than the lowest price | Offset laminated to the board, or digital; ask for a sample |
| 800 boxes where each carries a different customer name or flavour | The artwork changes every box | Digital, or a blank space for a label to be added |

In each case the right route follows from two questions: how many boxes, and how much detail does the artwork need?

## Common print problems and how to avoid them

- **The colour looks different from the screen.** Screens glow and board absorbs ink, so printed colour is duller on kraft. Ask for a printed sample, and see the [kraft paper guide](/blogs/kraft-paper-grades) for why the surface matters.
- **Fine lines fill in.** Thin strokes and small text blur on rough board. Thicken lines and enlarge text, and ask for the printer's minimum.
- **A colour edge shows a gap or overlap.** That is a registration issue. Where two colours meet, ask the printer how they handle it, or keep the colours apart in the design.
- **Ink rubs off in transit.** Heavy solid areas on boxes that rub together can scuff. Ask about varnish or coating options, and test by rubbing two sample boxes together.
- **Artwork lands on a fold or the glue flap.** Check the position on the folded 3D box and the [dieline](/blogs/dielines-explained) before you approve the proof.

## What to ask the printer

Send the same brief to every maker so the quotes compare:

- Which print routes do you offer for my quantity, and what is the minimum for each?
- What is the setup cost (plates, dies, file preparation), and is it repeated on a reorder?
- How many colours can you print in one pass, and what is the registration tolerance?
- What is the finest line or smallest text you recommend on this board?
- Can I see a printed sample or a proof on the board I will use?
- How do you match a brand colour, and what variation should I expect?

## Frequently asked questions

### Which printing is cheapest for corrugated boxes?

For a simple logo in one or two colours and a few thousand boxes, flexo is usually the economical route because the plate cost is spread over many boxes. For very small runs, a stamp, a label or digital printing can cost less. Compare total cost, not per-box price alone.

### Can corrugated boxes be printed in full colour?

Yes, through offset litho laminated onto the board, through digital printing, or through multi-colour flexo for suitable artwork. Photographs usually look best on a smooth surface, so ask for a sample.

### What is the difference between flexo and offset?

Flexo uses flexible plates and prints directly onto the board, which suits logos and solid colours. Offset prints on paper first, and the paper is laminated onto the board, which allows finer detail but adds steps and cost.

### Is digital printing good for small orders?

Often, because it needs no plates, so the setup cost is small. Not every box maker offers it, and cost per box stays higher at volume. Ask whether they offer it for your board and size.

### How many colours should my box have?

As few as your brand needs. Each extra colour adds a plate in flexo and increases cost. One brand colour on kraft, with a pattern or cutout in the same colour, often looks finished.
