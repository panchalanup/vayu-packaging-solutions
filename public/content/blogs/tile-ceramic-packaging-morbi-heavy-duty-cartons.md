# Tile and Ceramic Packaging for Morbi: Heavy-Duty Cartons

Start a tile carton spec from the weight of the packed carton, not its size. A box of four 600 x 600 x 9 mm tiles weighs about 31 kg on a typical density, which puts it in 7-ply, full-overlap territory before you think about corners, separators and strapping. This guide is spec thinking, not a promise of strength.

## Who this is for

Morbi in Gujarat is widely known as a centre of ceramic tile and sanitaryware making. If you pack tiles, sanitaryware or other ceramics for dispatch, you already know how they break: corners chip, edges crack under point loads, and a carton that bulges or crushes lets tiles move. This guide gives you a way to write a carton spec and ask a box maker the right questions. We do not describe how any Morbi unit packs, and we do not claim a rating for any carton here.

## Step 1: weigh the packed carton

Everything else follows from the weight of one packed carton, because a heavy carton is stressed when it is lifted, stacked and strapped. Tile weight is simple to calculate:

**Tile weight = length x width x thickness x density**

With length, width and thickness in cm, volume is in cm3, and an assumed ceramic density of about 2.4 g/cm3 gives grams. Replace 2.4 with the figure from your own tile specification.

| Tile (mm) | Pieces | One tile | Packed carton (approx.) | Inside size to try (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 600 x 600 x 9 | 4 | 7.8 kg | 31.1 kg | 62.5 x 62.5 x 6 |
| 800 x 800 x 10 | 2 | 15.4 kg | 30.7 kg | 82.5 x 82.5 x 5 (designer minimum) |
| 300 x 600 x 8 | 8 | 3.5 kg | 27.6 kg | 62.5 x 32.5 x 9 |

Worked example, first row: 60 x 60 x 0.9 cm is 3,240 cm3, times 2.4 is 7,776 g, so 7.8 kg a tile and about 31 kg for four. The inside size adds the designer's default padding of 1 cm a side and 0.3 cm of ease: 60 + 2 + 0.3 rounds up to 62.5 cm, and four tiles 3.6 cm thick plus 2.3 cm is 5.9, rounded to 6 cm. Add corner guards or separators and the inside size grows.

## Step 2: choose the board from the load

Our guides use a planning guide of up to about 8 kg for 3-ply, about 20 kg for 5-ply and about 40 kg for 7-ply. It is indicative, not a promise, and it is the same guide in [how many kg a corrugated box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold). The three carton weights above sit between 20 and 40 kg, so 7-ply is the sensible starting point to test.

| Packed carton weight | Starting board to test | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Under about 8 kg | 3-ply | Small sanitary parts, light ceramics |
| About 8 to 20 kg | 5-ply | Small tile counts, mid-size ceramics |
| About 20 to 40 kg | 7-ply | Typical tile cartons as above |
| Over about 40 kg | Rethink | Fewer pieces per carton, or packing at pallet level |

Treat 7-ply as the start of the discussion. A real spec also names the papers (liner GSM, burst factor) as explained in [reading a box spec](/blogs/reading-box-spec-ply-bf-gsm), and a compression test on a packed carton tells you more than any table.

## Step 3: pick a style that adds strength where the load goes

The **full overlap carton (FOL)** has flaps that meet and overlap completely, so the top and bottom are two layers thick. The designer describes it as the style for heavy or long items, rough handling and high stacks. A regular slotted carton (RSC) is cheaper in board but has a gap where the flaps meet in the middle. For heavy tile cartons, many buyers test FOL first.

## Step 4: protect the corners and the contents

Tiles usually fail at corners and edges. The carton is only part of the answer, so the spec should say what else goes inside and around it:

- **Corner and edge protectors** on the tile stack, so a drop does not point-load a corner. Specify the material and size with your supplier.
- **Separators** between tiles, such as paper or board sheets, if tiles can scratch each other. They add thickness, so remember them in the inside size.
- **No empty space.** Tiles that can slide inside the carton are the commonest cause of chipped edges. Size the carton to the packed stack.
- **Strapping.** Plastic strapping round the carton adds stiffness and keeps the flaps closed, but tight strap on an unprotected edge can crush the board. Use edge protectors under the strap. See [PP strapping](/products/pp-strapping).
- **Strapping at pallet level.** Full pallets are usually wrapped and strapped as a unit; your forwarder or carrier will say what they require.

## Step 5: think about the pallet and the container

A carton can pass a bench test and still fail at the bottom of a stack. Work out what each carton must carry:

1. **Cartons per pallet layer and layers per pallet.** Multiply by carton weight to get the pallet's total weight, then compare with the limits your carrier, warehouse and forklift set.
2. **Stack height.** The bottom carton bears the weight of every carton above it, and the [stacking strength guide](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide) shows how to estimate it, with a safety factor.
3. **Container fit.** If you export, the carton size must also divide the pallet and the container; the [CBM and container guide](/blogs/cbm-calculator-cartons-per-container) walks through it, and the [export checklist](/blogs/export-packaging-checklist-india) lists what to confirm with your forwarder.
4. **Moisture.** Board loses strength when it is damp. If your cartons wait on a damp dock or travel by sea, ask your box maker about moisture protection.

---

## Step 6: print the information handlers need

Plain, high-contrast marks cost little and prevent mistakes. Typical items are the product and size, shade or batch where relevant, piece count, packed weight, "this way up", "handle with care" and your brand. One or two colours printed in a single pass is usually enough ([printing on corrugated](/blogs/flexo-offset-digital-printing-corrugated-boxes)). Put the weight on two sides so a handler sees it whichever way the carton sits.

## A checklist for your next tile carton enquiry

Send this with your enquiry so the box maker can quote like for like:

- Tile size and thickness, pieces per carton and packed weight in kg
- Inside size in cm (state "inside"), with any corner guards or separators included
- Board: ply, flute, liner GSM, burst factor or ECT you need
- Style (full overlap or slotted), joint type and flap arrangement
- Print: colours, marks and where they go
- Quantity per order and whether you need it on pallets
- Whether the cartons go by road only, or by sea and in what conditions
- Your intended test: compression, drop and the stack height you expect

## Frequently asked questions

### Which ply is best for tile cartons?

It depends on the packed weight and how the carton is handled and stacked. As a planning guide, cartons from about 20 to 40 kg are often tested in 7-ply, and lighter cartons in 5-ply. Test a packed sample before you order volume.

### Is a full overlap carton better than an RSC for tiles?

A full overlap carton has two layers on top and bottom, so it is stronger there, and it is the style our designer recommends for heavy items. An RSC may be enough for lighter tile cartons. Compression-test both.

### How heavy can a corrugated box be?

There is no single answer: it depends on board, size, style, contents and handling. Our guide is up to about 40 kg for 7-ply, but that is a planning figure, not a promise. Above that, ask about fewer pieces per carton.

### Do corner guards really matter?

Corners and edges are where tiles usually chip, and a stack with protected corners spreads a knock over more of the tile. Ask your box maker or packing supplier which material suits your tile.

### Can I print my brand on tile cartons?

Yes. One or two colours is usual on corrugated cartons, and the designer can show a logo and handling marks on the model so you can approve placement before ordering.
