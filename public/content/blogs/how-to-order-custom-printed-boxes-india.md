# How to Order Custom Printed Boxes in India: 9 Steps

To order custom printed boxes in India, decide what the box holds, measure the inside size, pick the style and board, prepare artwork, choose a quantity, get a written quote, approve a proof, confirm payment and lead time, then plan delivery. Do the first five steps in a free 3D designer and you reach the quote with a complete spec.

*Last checked: October 2026. Prices, minimums and lead times are confirmed in your quote, not here.*

[Start your box in the 3D designer](/box-designer?style=mailer&l=30&w=22&h=8&ply=3-ply&fl=B)

Most first orders go wrong in the same quiet way. The buyer sends a logo and a rough size, the supplier guesses the rest, and a few weeks later a box arrives that is half a centimetre too small, a ply too light or printed in the wrong place. Every one of those mistakes is cheap to fix on screen and expensive to fix on a pallet.

This guide walks the whole order in nine steps, in the order you will actually meet them, and says what to bring to each one.

## Step 1: Know what goes inside

A box is a container for something specific. Before you open any tool, write down four facts about one packed unit:

- **Size** of the product, or of its inner pack (a jar in its sleeve, a shirt folded in its bag).
- **Weight** of one packed unit, in kilograms.
- **Fragile or not**, because fragile goods need more padding and a stronger board.
- **How it travels:** courier parcel, local delivery, retail shelf, pallet, or export.

If you are unsure, the designer's **Find my box** mode asks exactly these four questions and suggests a style, size, ply and flute. For the reasoning behind it, see [how to find the right box size](/blogs/find-right-box-size).

## Step 2: Measure the inside size

Suppliers and the 3D designer both work from **inside dimensions**: the space your product actually gets. The outside is larger by the board thickness, and that depends on the ply, so you should not work it out by hand.

Measure the product, not an old box. Then add padding: the fit checker starts at 1 cm per side for ordinary goods and 2.5 cm for fragile ones, adds a little ease so the product slides in, and rounds up to the next 0.5 cm. You can change all three. The [measuring guide](/blogs/box-measurements-guide) explains length, width and height order, and why inside size matters.

## Step 3: Pick the style

Box style decides how the box folds, how it opens, how it ships flat and how much board it uses. The designer has **20 styles in five groups**: shipping, retail and gifts, food and bakery, fruit and produce, and display. A plain regular slotted carton (RSC) is the everyday shipper; a mailer suits apparel and unboxing; a tuck-end carton suits light retail goods.

Use the style picker's "what are you packing?" shortcut, or read [which of the 20 box styles to choose](/blogs/which-box-style-should-you-choose). One practical note for step 6: die-cut styles such as mailers need a cutting die, so they carry a one-time tooling cost that plain slotted styles do not.

## Step 4: Choose the board

Board means the ply, the flute and the surface:

- **3-ply (single wall)** for light goods and most parcels.
- **5-ply (double wall)** for heavier goods, stacking and many export cartons.
- **7-ply (triple wall)** for heavy, bulky or industrial loads.
- **Surface:** natural kraft, white, or a printed colour over the board.

The designer's strength guidance gives a rough contents limit for each ply and tells you if your weight is too much for the board you picked. Treat it as a starting point and test a sample for anything valuable. The [wall guide](/blogs/corrugated-wall-differences) and [how many kg a box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold) go deeper.

## Step 5: Prepare the artwork

The designer accepts a logo or image as SVG, PNG, JPG or WebP, plus text and ready-made cutout stickers. Place everything on the 3D box and run the fold slider once: a logo that looks centred on a flat face can end up under a flap.

Three things from artwork change your price and your result:

1. **Number of colours.** Each ink colour normally needs its own printing plate, so one-colour print is the cheapest route. A full-colour photo counts as four colours in the designer's estimate.
2. **Resolution and size.** The print checks flag images that are too small for their printed size, art too close to a fold or edge, and text that is too small to read on the board colour.
3. **Board colour.** Colours on brown kraft look darker and warmer than on screen. If colour matters, ask about white board or a printed proof.

Which print method is used for your job depends on the quantity and the artwork. ## Step 6: Choose a quantity

Quantity drives the price per box more than most buyers expect, because the one-time setup (plates for each colour, and a die for die-cut styles) is shared across the order. The designer's estimate shows slabs at 100, 500, 1,000 and 5,000 boxes so you can see the curve for your own design.

Two cautions:

- **Minimum order.** Our current minimum order is 500 boxes, but confirm it against your spec. The 100-box column in the estimate is a planning view of how a short run behaves. See [minimum order quantity for custom boxes](/blogs/minimum-order-quantity-custom-boxes). <!-- VERIFY-LATER[BLOG-401]: the 500-box minimum comes from FACTS.moqBoxes (FACT-07). - **Storage.** Boxes usually arrive flat, but 5,000 of them still need a dry place off the floor. Order for what you will use before the paper is likely to take on damp, not for the best price slab alone.

## Step 7: Get a written quote

Send the design rather than a description. In the designer, **Send my design to Vayu** asks for name, phone, email, quantity, city or pincode, an optional sample request and a note, and attaches your spec, share link and preview. You can also open the [quote form](/quote) directly with your size filled in.

A quote you can act on states, at least:

| Line | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Inside size and style | So the right box is made |
| Ply, flute and paper spec | So quotes compare fairly (see [reading a box spec](/blogs/reading-box-spec-ply-bf-gsm)) |
| Print: colours, faces, positions | Print is where most surprises hide |
| Quantity and unit price | And the total |
| One-time charges shown separately | Plates, die, proof |
| GST treatment and invoice | Whether the price is before or after GST |
| Lead time from approval | The date that matters to you |
| Payment terms, delivery, validity | What happens before the boxes ship |

If a quote leaves one of these out, ask. The estimate in the designer is a guide to the right range; only the quote is a price.

## Step 8: Approve a proof

Do not skip this step, even for a simple box. Before printing, look at three things:

1. **The spec sheet.** The designer produces a one-page PDF with three views, the dimensions in your chosen unit, board, finish, quantity, a dieline thumbnail and a sign-off block.
2. **The dieline.** The flat layout downloads as SVG or PDF at 1:1 scale in millimetres, with cut and crease lines. It shows structure only, not artwork, and the plant sets the final allowances. See [dielines explained](/blogs/dielines-explained).
3. **A sample or printed proof,** if the job justifies it. ## Step 9: Confirm payment, lead time and delivery

Put these in writing before you pay anything:

- **Payment terms:** advance, balance and when each is due.
- **Lead time:** counted from artwork or proof approval, not from enquiry. Printed and die-cut jobs take longer than plain boxes, and the designer's delivery estimate reflects that with an indicative number of days for your city or pincode.
- **Delivery:** who arranges transport, the address, unloading, and a contact who can receive the boxes.
- **GST invoice:** ask for it with the order.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending outside dimensions** when the supplier expects inside, or the other way round. Say "inside" every time.
- **Choosing the board by price alone.** A box that crushes in a stack costs more than the ply you saved.
- **Printing before folding on screen.** Check the logo against the creases first.
- **Comparing quotes with different specs.** Ask each supplier for ply, flute, paper and print on the same lines.
- **Skipping the proof** because the order is small.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to order custom printed boxes?

It depends on the box, the print and the quantity. The steps up to the quote can take under an hour if you bring a full spec. Production and delivery time are confirmed in your quote and counted from proof approval. The designer shows an indicative number of days for your city, but only the quote is a promise.

### Do I need a dieline or design file to start?

No. Send the inside size, style, ply and quantity, plus a logo if you have one. You can download a dieline from the designer to check the structure, but the box plant sets the final allowances.

### What file format should my logo be in?

SVG gives the sharpest print. PNG, JPG and WebP also work if the image is large enough for its printed size. Avoid a small JPG saved from a website or forwarded on WhatsApp.

### Can I get a sample before I order?

We list samples as available on request. Ask what applies to your box and whether it is a printed sample or a plain one, and how much time it adds.

### Is the estimate in the designer the price?

No. It is an indicative range based on size, ply, print and quantity. The quote confirms the price.
