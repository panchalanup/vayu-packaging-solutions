# 8 Box Styles in 3D: Which One Fits Your Product?

"Box style" sounds like a detail, but it decides a lot: how the box opens, how much board it uses, how fast it is to pack, how it looks when your customer opens it, and how much it costs. Two boxes with exactly the same inside size can differ in price and protection simply because of the style.

The trouble is that style names (RSC, HSC, FOL, 0427) mean little until you see the box fold. So this guide explains the eight styles in the free **3D Box Designer** in plain words, with what each is best for, and shows you how to try each one on your own size in a few seconds.

## The eight styles at a glance

| Style | What it is | Best for | Opens by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RSC** (regular slotted carton) | The everyday shipping carton: four flaps top and bottom, outer flaps meet in the middle | Shipping, storage, e-commerce, almost anything | Cutting the tape |
| **HSC** (half slotted carton) | An RSC with no top flaps | Display, bins, a base under a separate lid | It is already open |
| **Full overlap (FOL)** | Like an RSC, but the outer flaps run the full width and overlap completely | Heavy or long items, rough handling, extra top and bottom strength | Cutting the tape |
| **Sleeve** | Four walls, open at both ends | Wrapping a tray or product, multipacks, retail bands | Sliding it off |
| **Telescope lid and base** | Two pieces: a base and a slightly larger lid that slides over it | Gift boxes, shoe boxes, apparel, premium presentation | Lifting the lid |
| **Mailer** | A one-piece die-cut box with a hinged lid that tucks in at the front | E-commerce, subscription boxes, unboxing | Lifting the lid |
| **Pizza box** | A shallow die-cut mailer, wide and flat | Pizza, flat food, flat goods such as prints or tiles | Lifting the lid |
| **Tray** | A die-cut base with no lid | Bakery, fruit, retail display, inside a sleeve | It is already open |

## The slotted family: RSC, HSC and full overlap

These three are made the same way: a long blank is creased into four wall panels, joined with a glue (or stitched) flap, and closed with flaps. They are quick to produce, need no special cutting die, and are usually the most economical per box.

### RSC (FEFCO 0201): the default for good reason
If you are not sure, start here. The RSC packs flat, folds in seconds and protects well. Its outer flaps are half the box width, so they meet in the middle and are sealed with one strip of tape.

**Choose it when:** you ship by courier or truck, stack boxes in a warehouse, or simply need a dependable carton.
**Watch out for:** a very wide RSC has large flaps and uses more board. If your product is long and flat, a mailer or tray may be better.

### HSC (FEFCO 0200): an RSC without the top
Half slotted cartons have bottom flaps only. Used alone they are bins and display boxes; with a separate lid they become a two-piece box.

**Choose it when:** the box is filled from the top and stays open, such as parts bins, shop-floor trays or a display carton.

### Full overlap, FOL (FEFCO 0203): more board where it counts
In a full overlap carton the outer flaps are the full width of the box, so they lie on top of each other. That doubles the board on the top and bottom, which helps with heavy, dense or long products and with rough handling where things get dropped on their ends.

**Choose it when:** you ship heavy goods, rolls or long items, or need the top and bottom to resist punctures.
**Trade-off:** the extra flap board costs more. Compare it in the estimate panel against a 5-ply RSC; sometimes a stronger board in a plain RSC is cheaper than an FOL.

In the designer, fold an FOL with the assembly slider and you can see the overlapping flaps close one over the other.

## Sleeves and two-piece boxes

### Sleeve: four walls, no ends
A sleeve is a tube of board. On its own it holds a stack of trays together, adds a branded band around a plain box, or protects long products. In the designer it is shown open at both ends so you can see straight through it.

**Choose it when:** you want a printed outer on a plain inner (cheaper than printing every box), or to bundle products.

### Telescope lid and base (FEFCO 03xx family)
Two pieces: a base and a lid that is slightly larger so it slides over. The lid can cover the base fully or only partly. In the designer you set the **lid depth** separately, and the fold animation lifts the lid off so you can see both parts.

**Choose it when:** presentation matters: gift boxes, shoe boxes, apparel, hampers, premium products.
**Watch out for:** two pieces means two blanks to make and two parts to pack, so it costs more than a one-piece mailer of similar size. If you want a lift-off lid look on a budget, compare with a mailer.

## The die-cut family: mailer, pizza box and tray

These are cut from a single sheet with a cutting die, which makes shapes the slotted styles cannot: tuck-in lids, double side walls and locking tabs. They look neat, need no tape, and are the favourites for e-commerce and food. A cutting die is a one-time cost per size, which matters for small orders.

### Mailer (FEFCO 0427): the e-commerce favourite
The roll-end tuck-front mailer has a hinged lid that tucks into the front, and double side walls with dust flaps that make it rigid without glue. It opens like a book, which is why it shows up in so many unboxing videos.

**Choose it when:** you sell online, run a subscription box, or want a box that looks finished without tape.
**Tip:** put your main logo on the **inside of the lid** too. It is the first thing the customer sees when they open it.

### Pizza box: a shallow mailer
Wide, flat and shallow, built the same way as a mailer. Used for pizza, of course, but also for flat bakery items, art prints, tiles and anything that is large but thin.

**Choose it when:** the product is flat and you want a box that stacks neatly at the counter.
**Food note:** for direct food contact, ask us about food-grade board; the designer previews shape and print, not material certification.

[Design a pizza box](/box-designer/pizza-box)

### Tray: a base without a lid
A die-cut tray is a mailer base with no lid. Bakeries and fruit sellers use trays on their own; brands put a tray inside a printed sleeve for a drawer-style pack.

**Choose it when:** the product is displayed or picked from the top, or as the inner part of a sleeve pack.

## How to choose in 30 seconds

Answer these in order and you will usually land on the right style:

1. **Does it ship by courier in a single box?** Start with a **mailer** (light goods, nice unboxing) or an **RSC** (heavier goods, lower cost).
2. **Is it heavy, long or dense?** Look at an **RSC in 5-ply** or a **full overlap** carton.
3. **Is presentation the priority (gifts, shoes, apparel)?** Try a **telescope lid and base**. For a gift box start here: [design a gift box](/box-designer/gift-box). For shoes: [design a shoe box](/box-designer/shoe-box).
4. **Is it flat and wide?** A **pizza box** style.
5. **Is it displayed or picked from the top?** A **tray** or **HSC**.
6. **Do you want a printed outer on a plain inner?** A **sleeve** over a tray or box.

Still not sure? The designer's **Find my box** mode asks about your product, weight, fragility and shipping method and recommends a style for you, with the reason in one line.

## How to compare styles in the designer

The quickest way to decide is to try two or three styles on your actual size:

1. Open the [3D Box Designer](/box-designer) and set your inside size first.
2. Click through the style cards. The size stays the same while the style changes.
3. For each, run the **assembly slider** from flat to sealed. You will see how the box opens and where the flaps land.
4. Open the **estimate** and note the per-box price at your quantity. Die-cut styles carry a one-time die cost, so their price per box falls faster with quantity.
5. Look at the **dieline** for each: a mailer's blank is wider than an RSC's for the same inside size, which is why it uses more board.
6. Share the two finalists with your team using a share link and let them choose.

## Tips from the factory floor

- **Courier rates follow outside size.** A style that adds 1-2 cm to the outside can move you into a higher courier slab. The designer shows outside dimensions, so check them.
- **Die-cut pays off with volume.** For a short trial run, an RSC avoids a cutting die. Once you reorder regularly, a mailer often makes sense.
- **Tape is a cost too.** Mailers and trays need none; RSCs need it on top and bottom. Include packing time in your comparison.
- **Stacking needs vertical walls.** Mailers stack fine in small piles, but for tall pallet stacks an RSC in the right ply is stronger. See [will my box stack?](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide)

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common corrugated box style?
The regular slotted carton (RSC, FEFCO 0201). Most shipping cartons you see are RSCs.

### What is the difference between a mailer box and an RSC?
An RSC is a tube with four flaps top and bottom, sealed with tape. A mailer is die-cut from one sheet with a hinged tuck-in lid and double side walls. Mailers look finished and need no tape; RSCs use less board and are usually cheaper for heavier goods.

### Is a telescope box the same as a lid and base box?
Yes. "Telescope" describes the lid sliding over the base like the sections of a telescope.

### Can every style be printed?
Yes. In the designer you can add artwork to any printable face of every style. Die-cut styles also have inside surfaces such as the inside of a mailer lid.

### Which style uses the least board?
For the same inside size, usually the RSC or HSC. Check the **dieline** and the **estimate** in the designer to compare your exact size.

### My style is not listed. Can you make it?
Very likely. The designer covers the most common styles; ask us about inserts, partitions, crash-lock bottoms and other constructions.
