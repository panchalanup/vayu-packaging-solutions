# Which Box Style Should You Choose? A Picker by Product

You know what you sell. You may not know what its box is called. The free 3D Box Designer has **20 box styles**, from plain shipping cartons to ventilated fruit boxes and window cake boxes, and the right one depends on your product, not on the style name.

This guide is the shortcut. Start with the table that matches your product, read the short note on the one or two styles it points to, and open each in the designer on your own size. There is a link for every style.

## How the style picker works

You do not have to remember any of the names. The picker in the designer helps in four ways:

- **Group tabs.** The 20 styles are sorted into five groups: Shipping, Retail and gifts, Food and bakery, Fruit and produce, and Display.
- **A Popular row** with the styles people start from most.
- **"What are you packing?" chips** such as fruits and veg, cake and sweets, clothes, shoes, cosmetics, gifts, electronics, bottles and jars, books. Tap one and the list is filtered and sorted with the best match first.
- **Search**, which understands everyday words. Typing "aam", "burger", "saree" or "bottle" finds the right style even if the name does not contain the word.

Each card shows a short best-for line and small option hints such as Window, Vents, Hand holes, Lid depth or Cut-down front, so you can see at a glance what a style can do.

## Group 1: Shipping (6 styles)

Built to travel. Most are made with the slotted method, so they are economical and quick to produce. If you want the basics, see our guide to [types of corrugated boxes](/blogs/types-of-corrugated-boxes).

- **[Regular slotted carton (RSC)](/box-designer/shipping-carton):** the everyday shipper, four flaps top and bottom. Start here when unsure. *Watch out:* very wide cartons use a lot of board for the flaps.
- **[Half slotted carton (HSC)](/box-designer?style=hsc):** an RSC without top flaps. Bins, display cartons or a base under a separate lid.
- **[Full overlap (FOL)](/box-designer/heavy-duty-box):** outer flaps cover the full width, so top and bottom are double thick. Heavy, long or rough-handled goods. *Watch out:* more board means a higher price; compare with a stronger ply in a plain RSC.
- **[Sleeve](/box-designer/box-sleeve):** four walls, open ends. A printed outer on a plain inner, or a multipack band.
- **[Mailer (tuck front)](/box-designer/mailer-box):** a die-cut box with a hinged lid and double side walls, no tape needed. E-commerce and subscription boxes. See [the unboxing guide](/blogs/ecommerce-mailer-box-unboxing).
- **[Carry box with hand holes](/box-designer/carry-box):** a sealed slotted carton with a hand hole in each short wall. Hampers, sweets, appliances, moving. *Watch out:* hand holes lower stacking strength; see [vents, hand holes and windows](/blogs/vents-hand-holes-windows-box-design).

## Group 2: Retail and gifts (7 styles)

Built to be looked at and opened in front of someone.

- **[Telescope box (lid and base)](/box-designer/gift-box):** two pieces, a base and a lid that slides over it. Gifts, shoes, premium packs; set the lid depth in the designer. *Watch out:* two blanks cost more than a one-piece mailer.
- **[Tuck-end carton](/box-designer/tuck-end-box):** a folding carton, a tube with a lid flap and tuck tab at each end. Cosmetics, soap, small retail packs, cards.
- **[Crash-lock bottom box](/box-designer/crash-lock-box):** the bottom locks itself when the box is opened out (pre-glued), with a tuck top. Heavier bottles, jars and quick-to-set-up packing lines.
- **[1-2-3 bottom box](/box-designer?style=one-two-three):** three bottom flaps fold in order and lock. Lighter than crash-lock, quick to set up by hand; light to medium retail, food and gift items.
- **[Window mailer](/box-designer/window-mailer-box):** a tuck-front mailer with a film window in the lid, so the product shows before opening. Apparel, gifts, cosmetics.
- **[Slide box (tray and sleeve)](/box-designer/slide-box):** a tray slides out of a printed sleeve. Gifts, cosmetics, electronics, premium packs; two parts, one reveal.
- **[Book-style flip-lid box](/box-designer/book-box):** the lid is hinged along the long side like a book, closed with a tuck tongue. Gifts, books, hampers and keepsakes.

**Crash-lock or 1-2-3?** If the product is heavy (a glass bottle, a full jar), choose crash-lock. If it is light and you pack by hand in small batches, 1-2-3 is simpler. If you are not sure, put both on your size and compare the estimates.

## Group 3: Food and bakery (3 styles)

- **[Pizza box](/box-designer/pizza-box):** a shallow, wide mailer. Pizza, flat bakery, and also prints and tiles.
- **[Window cake box](/box-designer/cake-box):** a square hinged-lid box with a big window, deep enough for a cake or sweet tray. The window is part of the style; the size is a slider.
- **[Takeaway clamshell](/box-designer/takeaway-box):** a food box with the lid hinged on the back wall and a front tuck, closing without tape or glue. Burgers, sandwiches, lunch.

For anything touching food directly, remember that the designer previews shape and print, not material certification; ask us about board grades for food contact. More in [pizza and bakery box design](/blogs/pizza-bakery-box-design).

## Group 4: Fruit and produce (2 styles)

- **[Ventilated produce carton](/box-designer/fruit-box):** a sealed slotted carton with vent slots and hand holes for airflow and carrying. Mangoes, apples, grapes, vegetables.
- **[Open produce tray (HSC)](/box-designer?style=produce-tray):** a low open tray with vent slots, stackable and easy to see into. Berries, tomatoes, small fruit, market and shelf display.

For fresh produce, ventilation and stacking pull in opposite directions. Read [vents, hand holes and windows](/blogs/vents-hand-holes-windows-box-design) before you decide how many slots to cut.

## Group 5: Display (2 styles)

- **[Tray (die-cut, no lid)](/box-designer/bakery-box):** the mailer base on its own, with double walls all round. Bakery, produce, displays, or the inner part of a sleeve pack.
- **[Shelf display tray](/box-designer/display-tray):** a tray with a cut-down front, so products show and can be picked straight off the shelf. Snacks, small retail packs, counter displays.

## A five-step way to choose

1. **Say what the box must do in one sentence:** "carry 8 kg of mangoes", "show a cake", "arrive with a T-shirt looking good". The verb picks the style.
2. **Pick the group, then the style.** Use the table above or a packing chip.
3. **Put your real inside size in first.** Then click through the two finalists; the size stays the same while the style changes. Use the [fit checker](/blogs/find-right-box-size) if you only know the product size.
4. **Fold both** with the assembly slider and look at the dieline. A style that needs a lot more board for the same inside size will show in the estimate.
5. **Compare the estimates** at your real quantity. Die-cut styles carry a one-time cutting die, so their per-box price falls faster as the quantity grows. The [price guide](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide) explains why.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best box style for a small business?
There is no single best. For shipping, an RSC or mailer. For retail, a tuck-end carton, window mailer or slide box. For food, a cake box or clamshell. Use the table above.

### Which style is cheapest?
For the same inside size, slotted styles (RSC, HSC) usually use the least board and need no cutting die. Die-cut styles cost more per box at low quantities, then come closer as volume grows. Check the estimate for your exact size.

### Can I change the style after I add artwork?
Yes. A size you set yourself is kept (a size you never changed follows the new style's default), and cut-outs the new style cannot have are dropped. Faces differ between styles, so re-check where your artwork lands and fold the box.

### Why do some styles show a window or vents and others do not?
Each style offers only the cut-outs that suit its construction: vents and hand holes on slotted cartons, windows on mailers, cake boxes and cartons. See [vents, hand holes and windows](/blogs/vents-hand-holes-windows-box-design).

### A style I want is missing.
Tell us. The designer covers the most common styles; we can often make others, such as inserts and partitions.
