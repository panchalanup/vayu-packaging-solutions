# Cardboard vs Corrugated vs Duplex Boxes: The Difference

"Cardboard" is a loose everyday word for stiff paper. Corrugated board is paper liners around a wavy fluted layer (3, 5 or 7 ply), strong and cushioning, built for shipping. Duplex board is solid paperboard, usually white on the front and grey at the back, built for printed retail cartons.

## The short version

| | Corrugated | Duplex (paperboard) | "Cardboard" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Flat paper liners with a wavy fluted layer between them | One solid sheet of layered paper, white-coated on the front, grey on the back | A loose word. People use it for both, and for thin card |
| **Look at the cut edge** | A wavy layer inside, like a sandwich | A solid, dense edge with no waves | Depends what is meant |
| **Strength for shipping** | High. The flutes carry load and cushion knocks | Low. It dents and bends under stacking | Depends |
| **Print** | Good on E flute or white top, plainer on thick flutes | Very good. A smooth white face takes sharp, colourful print | Depends |
| **Typical use** | Shipping cartons, mailers, produce boxes, export packs | Retail cartons for cosmetics, medicines, small gifts, food packs | Whatever the speaker means |

If you remember one line, remember this: **corrugated is for moving things, duplex is for showing things**, and many products use both.

## What "cardboard" means

There is no board called "cardboard" in a supplier's spec sheet. In everyday talk it covers everything from a cereal box to a moving carton. That is why a search for "cardboard box" gets mixed results, and why a quote request that says "cardboard box" needs a second question: which kind?

A useful habit: replace "cardboard" with the board's actual name and its layers. "A 5-ply corrugated box, B and C flute" is a spec. "Cardboard, thick" is not. The [spec-reading guide](/blogs/reading-box-spec-ply-bf-gsm) shows how a supplier writes it.

## Corrugated board

Corrugated board is a sandwich. Flat paper **liners** on the outside and inside hold a wavy paper layer, the **flute**, between them. The arches make the board stiff for its weight and give a bit of cushioning.

| Board | Layers | Wall | Indicative thickness | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-ply | 2 liners + 1 flute | Single wall | about 3 mm | Light goods, mailers, retail cartons |
| 5-ply | 3 liners + 2 flutes | Double wall | about 6 to 7 mm | Heavier goods, stacking, many export cartons |
| 7-ply | 4 liners + 3 flutes | Triple wall | about 10 mm | Very heavy or bulky goods |

The flute type changes the feel. Thicker flutes such as B and C give more cushion and stack strength. Thin flutes such as E and F are flatter and print better. Read the [flute guide](/blogs/flute-types-guide) and [single, double and triple wall corrugated](/blogs/corrugated-wall-differences) for the detail.

Corrugated board is made in many paper grades, so two boxes of the same ply can differ. See [kraft paper grades](/blogs/kraft-paper-grades) for virgin and recycled liners.

## Duplex board

Duplex board is a paperboard: a solid sheet built up from layers of paper pulp, usually with a white-coated face for print and a grey or brown back. It is flat, smooth and light, and it folds and creases cleanly.

- **Good for:** folding cartons that sit on a shelf. Cosmetic packs, medicine cartons, small electronics retail packs, bakery and snack boxes.
- **Not good for:** carrying weight, being stacked in a godown, or travelling loose in a courier network. It has no flutes to absorb knocks.
- **Print:** the coated face gives sharp images and solid colour, which is why it is the choice for shelf packaging.

Duplex cartons are normally printed on a sheet and cut with a die, then folded and glued. The designer shows a tuck-end carton in thin corrugated board, a close cousin of the paperboard carton. If you want duplex or rigid board for a style, tell us when you ask for a quote.

## How to tell them apart in ten seconds

1. **Look at the cut edge.** A wavy layer inside means corrugated. A solid edge means paperboard.
2. **Flip it over.** A white, coated face with a grey or brown back is typical of duplex.
3. **Press the side wall.** Corrugated springs back and resists. Paperboard dents or folds.
4. **Check the weight.** A paperboard carton is light and thin. A corrugated carton of the same size is thicker and a little heavier.
5. **Ask the supplier.** Ask them to write the board as "3-ply corrugated, B flute", or as duplex with its GSM, so the quote is clear.

## Which board for which product

| You pack | Likely board | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Parcels sent by courier | Corrugated, 3 or 5-ply | Needs stack strength and cushion. See [packing for Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho](/blogs/packing-for-amazon-flipkart-meesho) |
| Garments and accessories in a mailer | Corrugated, thin flute | Strong, prints well, folds without tape. See [mailer box sizes](/blogs/mailer-box-sizes-tshirt-kurti-saree-jewellery) |
| Fruit and vegetables | Corrugated, vented | Needs strength and airflow. See [ventilated boxes](/blogs/fruit-vegetable-ventilated-boxes) |
| Heavy or export goods | Corrugated, 5 or 7-ply | See [export cartons](/blogs/export-cartons-5-ply-7-ply) |
| Cosmetic or medicine retail pack | Duplex or thin corrugated | Shelf look and sharp print |
| Pizza, cake and food boxes | Corrugated (thin) or paperboard | Depends on the food. See [takeaway boxes](/blogs/takeaway-tiffin-pizza-boxes-cloud-kitchens) |
| Premium gifts | Rigid board, or thin corrugated | Feel and look. See [festive gift boxes](/blogs/festive-gift-hamper-boxes) |

For many products the best answer is **both**: a duplex retail carton inside a corrugated shipper. The retail box sells, and the shipper carries.

## The middle ground: thin corrugated

Between duplex and heavy corrugated sits thin corrugated, in E or F flute. It prints close to paperboard quality, but the flute adds stiffness, so it holds shape better than duplex and suits mailers, tuck-end cartons, slide boxes and book-style boxes. ## Mistakes people make

- **Using paperboard to ship.** A duplex carton looks good in the shop but may crush or tear in the courier network. Ship in a corrugated outer.
- **Over-buying corrugated.** A light retail pack may not need 5-ply. Choose by the weight of the contents, using [how many kg a box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold).
- **Quoting by the word "cardboard".** Two suppliers may price two different boards. Name the board and the layers.
- **Ignoring paper grade.** The same ply can be strong kraft or weaker recycled paper. Ask for GSM and BF, as in the [GSM guide](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength).
- **Assuming brown means recycled.** Colour does not tell you the fibre.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between cardboard and corrugated?

"Cardboard" is a loose term for stiff paper, and in packaging it can mean paperboard or corrugated. Corrugated is a specific board: paper liners around a wavy fluted layer. It is stronger and more cushioning than thin paperboard of the same size, which is why shipping cartons use it.

### Is a corrugated box the same as a cardboard box?

Often people call a corrugated box a cardboard box, but the trade names are different. A corrugated box is made from fluted board. A paperboard (cardboard) box such as a cereal or medicine carton is one solid sheet without flutes.

### What is duplex board?

Duplex board is a solid paperboard, usually with a white-coated face for print and a grey or brown back. It is used for folding retail cartons, not for heavy loads or rough shipping.

### Which is stronger, corrugated or duplex?

Corrugated is much stronger for stacking and shipping, because the flutes resist crushing and absorb knocks. Duplex is stiff in a small carton but dents and bends under a load. For anything heavy or stacked, use corrugated.

### Can I print on corrugated as well as duplex?

Yes. Thin E or F flute corrugated and white-top liners print well, and printed paper can be laminated to corrugated. Duplex still has the smoothest face for fine colour. The [flute guide](/blogs/flute-types-guide) and [logo and artwork guide](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes) explain the options.
