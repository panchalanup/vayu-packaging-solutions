# How to Pack Fragile Items for Courier: Step-by-Step

To pack a fragile item for courier, wrap it on its own, put it in a snug box with at least 2.5 cm of cushioning on every side, fill every gap, tape every seam and label it. A mug about 10 x 10 x 9.5 cm needs a box of 15.5 x 15.5 x 15 cm inside.

## Why fragile parcels break

A parcel rarely fails because of one big drop. It fails because of three smaller things:

- **Movement inside the box.** The item shifts, hits the wall and cracks. Empty space is the main cause.
- **Compression from above.** Parcels are stacked, and a thin or oversized box bows under the weight.
- **Hits on the corners and edges.** The item touches the wall, and the shock goes straight through.

Everything below is aimed at these three. The sections that follow also keep shipping cost in check, because a box that is too big raises the bill when a courier charges on [volumetric weight](/blogs/volumetric-weight-calculator-india).

---

## The 8 steps

1. **Wrap each item on its own.** Bubble wrap, foam or paper, so pieces never touch each other.
2. **Choose an inner box or a snug outer box** that fits the wrapped item with room for cushioning, and no more.
3. **Add at least 2.5 cm of cushioning on every side**, including the bottom and the top. The designer's fit checker raises padding to 2.5 cm when you tick "fragile".
4. **Fill every gap.** Crumpled paper, foam or air pillows. Press on the closed box; if anything shifts or the lid sags, add more.
5. **Pick the board for the weight and the stack.** Use 3-ply for light items and 5-ply for heavy or tall ones. See [how many kg a corrugated box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold).
6. **Seal all seams.** Tape the centre seam and both edges, the H pattern, with strong tape.
7. **Label clearly.** Put the address on the top, a "Fragile" and "This way up" sticker on more than one face, and a copy of the address inside.
8. **Test one pack before you ship fifty.** Shake it, then drop it a few times from table height onto each face and corner, and open it to look.

A "Fragile" sticker asks for care; it does not guarantee it. The padding does the work.

## Box size worked out for four fragile items

The fit checker adds the padding on each side, plus 0.3 cm of ease so the item slides in, and rounds up to the next 0.5 cm. With fragile padding of 2.5 cm, each measurement grows by 5.3 cm. Measure the item **as wrapped**, or measure it bare and add your wrap as padding.

## Double boxing: when it helps and what it costs

For very fragile or high-value items, many sellers put the wrapped item in an **inner box**, then put that box in a **larger outer box** with cushioning between them. The outer box takes the knocks and the inner box keeps the item still.

Take the mug box above, 15.5 x 15.5 x 15 cm inside. Treat its outside as about 1 cm bigger each way, then add 2.5 cm of cushioning on each side of it:

- Length and width: 15.5 + 1 + 5 = **21.5 cm**.
- Height: 15 + 1 + 5 = **21 cm**.
- Outer box: **21.5 x 21.5 x 21 cm**, which is 9,707 cm3 and **1.94 kg** volumetric weight at 5000.

That is about 2.7 times the volumetric weight of the single box (0.72 kg). Double boxing is a trade between protection and freight, so use it where a break costs more than the extra weight, such as glassware, or where the retail box must stay clean.

## Item notes

- **Glass and ceramics.** Wrap each piece separately, and add more at the rim, base and handles, which break first. Never let two pieces touch.
- **Candles.** Jar candles can crack and wax can soften in heat. Cushion the jar and keep the box away from heat, and wrap loose wax so it does not mark the board.
- **Electronics.** Keep the product in its retail box if you have one, which makes the retail box the inner box. Add cushioning around it. Keep cables and accessories inside the retail box so they do not rattle.
- **Liquids in glass bottles.** See the guide to [packing pickle, ghee and spice jars without leaks](/blogs/pack-pickle-ghee-spice-jars-courier).

## Choosing the board

Use weight, not size, to pick the board. In the designer's guidance, 3-ply is comfortable for contents up to about 8 kg and 5-ply up to about 20 kg, and the fit checker adds 25% headroom to the weight for fragile goods. Tall boxes and boxes that will be stacked need more. The [stacking strength guide](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide) and the free [box strength calculator](/tools/box-strength-calculator) give an indicative check, which is a guide and not a rating.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you pack fragile items for courier?

Wrap each item on its own, put it in a snug box with at least 2.5 cm of cushioning on every side, fill all gaps, tape every seam and label the parcel. Then shake and drop-test one sample. A "Fragile" sticker is not enough on its own.

### How much padding does a fragile item need?

A common planning figure is 2.5 cm on every side, which is what the 3D designer's fit checker uses for fragile goods. Heavy or very delicate items may need more, so test a real pack.

### Should I use a double box for fragile items?

It helps for glass, ceramics and high-value items, because the outer box takes the knocks and the inner box holds the item still. It raises the box size, and so the volumetric weight, by roughly 2 to 3 times in our mug example. Use it where breakage costs more.

### Which box is best for shipping fragile goods?

A new corrugated box, snug around the wrapped item, in 3-ply for light goods and 5-ply for heavier or taller ones. A box that is too big lets the item move, and a box that is too small leaves no cushioning.

### How do I stop a parcel from being crushed?

Choose a board that suits the weight and the stack, avoid overlarge boxes, fill the space so the lid cannot sag, and never leave a gap at the top. A box that is full and firm resists crushing better than an empty one.
