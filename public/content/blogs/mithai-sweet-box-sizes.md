# Mithai Box Sizes: 250 g, 500 g, 1 kg and Gift Boxes

Size a mithai box from the piece, not the weight: weigh one piece, divide the pack weight by it to get the piece count, lay the pieces in a grid and add 1 cm at each edge. For 25 barfi pieces of 4.5 x 4.5 cm in one layer, that gives a 24.5 x 24.5 cm box.

## Why weight alone does not size a box

"500 g" tells you the sweets weigh half a kilo, not how much room they take. A dense peda and a light soan papdi can have the same weight and very different volumes. Sweet shops also pack different shapes: flat barfi squares, round laddu in paper cups, thick gulab jamun in syrup (which needs a leak-proof tub, not a plain box).

So the method works backwards from your own piece:

1. **Weigh one piece** in grams. Use an average of five.
2. **Piece count = pack weight / piece weight.** Round down to a count that makes a neat grid.
3. **Measure one piece** (or its paper cup) and add about 0.5 cm for a gap. That is your **cell**.
4. **Lay out the grid** along the length and width: cells x count, plus 1 cm at each edge, so 2 cm in total.
5. **Height = piece height + 2.5 to 3 cm** for the liner, finger room and lid clearance. Two layers add the second piece height and a sheet of paper between.
6. **Round up** to the next 0.5 cm and quote the **inside** size.

The 3D designer's fit checker uses a similar rule (padding on each side, a little ease, round up to 0.5 cm), so you can check any row with the [box size calculator](/tools/box-size-calculator).

## Trays, inserts and liners

The designer models the box, not what sits inside it. Mention these when you ask for a quote:

- **A liner or tray** under the sweets, so oil does not mark the board. Sweets with ghee or oil can stain plain board over time, so ask your supplier what liner or coating suits your product.
- **Paper cups** for laddu, or a **partition** if pieces must not touch. Partitions and inserts are usually a quote item.
- **A top sheet** (butter paper or similar) over the pieces.
- **A band or sticker** to seal the lid.

Sweets are food, so say clearly what touches the box and ask what documents can come with the board and any liner. We do not claim a food-safety certificate here; see [kraft paper grades](/blogs/kraft-paper-grades) for how paper choice affects food contact.

## Board and finish

A single layer of sweets is light, so thin board is usually enough. The designer's sweet box template uses 3-ply with E flute, a thin flute that prints well. A 1 kg pack with two layers is heavier and needs the lid and base walls to hold the stack in a car boot or a bag, so test a packed sample before you commit.

A gloss finish and a one- or two-colour print on a red or gold box look rich. A plain kraft box with a stamp is cheaper. The [box price guide](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide) shows what drives cost, and [cake, sweet and bakery boxes](/blogs/cake-sweet-bakery-boxes) covers cupcake and cookie boxes.

## Ordering before the festival

Festive demand arrives all at once and dates change every year. Work back from the day you need boxes in the shop:

- Settle the sizes and artwork early. Every change after that costs time.
- Ask for the production and delivery time for your quantity, not a general promise, and for a sample if time allows.
- Ask about the minimum order. Our current minimum is 500 boxes; see [minimum order quantity for custom boxes](/blogs/minimum-order-quantity-custom-boxes).
- Order a little more than your forecast, because a second run mid-season may not fit the queue.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size is a 500 g mithai box?

It depends on the sweet. As an example, 25 flat barfi pieces of 4.5 x 4.5 cm in one layer need about 24.5 x 24.5 x 4 cm inside, and 12 laddu in paper cups need about 24 x 18.5 x 7 cm. Weigh your own piece and use the method above.

### How do I work out the box size for 1 kg of sweets?

Divide 1,000 g by the weight of one piece, arrange that many pieces in a grid, and add 2 cm to each grid side and about 3 cm to the height. If the box would be very long, split the pieces into two layers.

### Which box style suits mithai?

A telescope box (lid and base) is the usual choice, because the lid lifts off like a gift. A slide box suits flat gift packs, and an open tray suits shop display.

### Do sweet boxes need a liner?

Usually yes. Oil and syrup can mark plain board, so use a liner, a tray or paper cups, and ask your supplier what suits your sweets. Wet sweets in syrup need a leak-proof tub instead.

### Can I print my shop name on the box?

Yes. In the designer you can add text and graphics to the lid and walls, check the print warnings and share the design. Read [putting a logo on a box](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes) for file tips.
