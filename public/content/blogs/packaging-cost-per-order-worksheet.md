# Packaging Cost per Order: A Worksheet for D2C and Kitchens

Packaging cost per order is the box, tape, filler and labels, plus the shipping the box causes, plus an allowance for damage. Add every line for one order, then multiply by monthly orders. In the worked example below (placeholder numbers), a smaller box cuts a Rs 133 order to Rs 104.

## The worksheet

Copy this table into a sheet or notebook and fill the last column for **one** order. Do the sum for your most common order first.

| # | Line | What to include | Your number (Rs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Box** | Price per box for the size you really use, at your order quantity | |
| 2 | **Tape** | Metres used per box x price per metre | |
| 3 | **Filler** | Paper, air pillows or bubble wrap used per order | |
| 4 | **Label and inserts** | Shipping label, invoice, thank-you card, stickers | |
| 5 | **Shipping** | Billed kg x your rate per kg (see below) | |
| 6 | **Damage allowance** | Damage rate x cost of one damaged order | |
| | **Cost per order** | Add lines 1 to 6 | |
| 7 | **As a share of order value** | Cost per order / average order value | |
| 8 | **Monthly packaging cost** | Cost per order x orders a month | |

Two lines need a short explanation.

**Shipping (line 5)** is not always the parcel's real weight. A courier bills on the **larger** of actual weight and volumetric weight, then rounds up to a slab. Volumetric weight is length x width x height in cm, divided by a divisor. The divisor, slab and rate are on your rate card, and they differ by courier and service. See the [volumetric weight article](/blogs/volumetric-weight-calculator-india) and the free [volumetric weight calculator](/tools/volumetric-weight-calculator).

**Damage allowance (line 6)** is the share of orders that arrive damaged, times what one damaged order costs you: the replacement, the return or reverse freight, and the repack. If 2 orders in 100 are damaged and each costs Rs 400, the allowance is Rs 8 per order.

## A worked example for a D2C seller

These are the same example numbers the free calculator starts with. They are placeholders.

**Today's box:** 30 x 20 x 15 cm at Rs 20, extras (tape, filler, label) Rs 5. Parcel weighs 1 kg. Divisor 5000. Rate Rs 50 per kg, rounded up to the next 0.5 kg. Damage 2% at Rs 400 per damaged order.

| Line | Current box (30 x 20 x 15 cm) | Proposed box (25 x 18 x 12 cm) |
|---|---|---|
| Box | Rs 20 | Rs 16 |
| Tape, filler and labels | Rs 5 | Rs 5 |
| Volumetric weight | 9,000 / 5,000 = 1.8 kg | 5,400 / 5,000 = 1.08 kg |
| Billed weight (larger of 1 kg and volumetric, rounded up to a 0.5 kg slab) | 2 kg | 1.5 kg |
| Shipping at Rs 50 per kg | Rs 100 | Rs 75 |
| Damage allowance (2% x Rs 400) | Rs 8 | Rs 8 |
| **Cost per order** | **Rs 133** | **Rs 104** |

The smaller box saves Rs 29 per order. On 1,000 orders a month, that is Rs 29,000. Most of the saving is in shipping, not in the box.

Two things can change this result:

- **Heavy, compact goods.** If the parcel weighs 4 kg and the volumetric weight is 1.8 kg, actual weight sets the bill and a smaller box saves little. The calculator shows which one applies.
- **A smaller box may protect less.** If damage goes up from 2% to 3%, the allowance rises from Rs 8 to Rs 12, and the saving drops from Rs 29 to Rs 25. Test the smaller box with a packed sample before you switch.

## A worked example for a cloud kitchen

Food delivery has no courier weight line, because the delivery partner usually charges differently. Packaging cost per order is the container, the bag and the small extras, plus an allowance for spills. Again, every figure is a placeholder.

| # | Line | Placeholder (Rs) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Main container and lid (for example a clamshell or tiffin box) | 12 |
| 2 | Side container or sauce cup | 3 |
| 3 | Carry bag | 3 |
| 4 | Tamper seal or sticker | 1 |
| 5 | Cutlery set and tissue | 2.5 |
| 6 | Spill allowance: 1.5% x Rs 200 (food cost to remake plus redelivery) | 3 |
| | **Packaging cost per order** | **24.5** |
| 7 | As a share of an order of Rs 300 | about 8% |

The share in line 7 is the number to watch. If packaging is about 8% of order value, a Rs 1 saving per order is worth about 0.3% of every order's revenue, and a spill rate that doubles can cost more than a cheaper container saves.

The sums are the same for any food box. The size has to fit the meal, the wrapped food and the delivery bag. See [takeaway, tiffin and pizza boxes for cloud kitchens](/blogs/takeaway-tiffin-pizza-boxes-cloud-kitchens) and the [pizza box guide](/blogs/pizza-bakery-box-design) for sizes, and keep in mind that board touching food needs a conversation with your supplier about what documents come with it.

## How to find each number

Do not guess. Each line has a source:

| Line | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Box price | Your last supplier invoice, divided by quantity, plus delivery per box |
| Tape | Metres per roll and roll price, then metres used on one packed box |
| Filler | Weigh or count what one packer uses, over a day |
| Label and inserts | Printer or stationery bills divided by orders |
| Shipping | Your courier invoice: billed weight and charge for the last 20 orders |
| Damage | Returns and complaints in the last 3 months divided by orders; cost of each from your records |

Then make the sum **for each product size**, not just the average. A seller with three box sizes has three costs per order.

## Where the money is: five levers

1. **Shrink the box to fit.** It lowers the box price and, when volumetric weight applies, the shipping line. The [fit checker](/blogs/find-right-box-size) and the free [box size calculator](/tools/box-size-calculator) work out the right inside size from your product.
2. **Use fewer box sizes.** Two or three sizes keep the order quantity per size high, which usually lowers the price per box, and make packing faster. See [minimum order quantity](/blogs/minimum-order-quantity-custom-boxes).
3. **Replace filler with fit.** Paper and air pillows cost money and time. A snug box needs less.
4. **Print fewer colours.** One colour on kraft keeps cost low. See [what a 5-ply box costs](/blogs/what-does-a-5-ply-box-cost) for how each choice moves the estimate.
5. **Cut damage, not just cost.** A saving of Rs 3 on the box is lost if damage rises. Use the [packing checklist](/blogs/reduce-damage-rto-packing-checklist) and test a sample.

## A note on RTO

RTO (return to origin) is when a parcel is not delivered and comes back. It costs forward freight, return freight and the work of repacking and restocking. Much RTO has nothing to do with packaging, because address problems and refused deliveries are among the usual causes. But if a parcel comes back damaged, the box is part of the cost. Track RTO as a separate line, and look at which returns were damaged.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I calculate packaging cost per order?

Add the price of the box, tape, filler and labels, then the shipping charge for the parcel, then an allowance for damage (damage rate times the cost of one damaged order). The free packaging cost calculator does the sum for your current box and a smaller one.

### What should packaging cost as a share of order value?

There is no standard. Work out your own share and watch it over time. If it rises, check box price, filler use and damage rate. We do not give a benchmark because it varies widely by product and margin.

### Will a smaller box really cut my shipping cost?

It can, when you are billed on volumetric weight. If your parcels are heavy and compact, the actual weight sets the bill and a smaller box saves little. Compare both numbers on your own parcel.

### Why is the courier's billed weight higher than my parcel's weight?

Couriers bill the larger of actual and volumetric weight, then round up to a slab. A big, light box is billed on its volume. The divisor and slab depend on the courier, so check your rate card.

### How do I include damage in packaging cost?

Count the orders damaged or returned in a period, divide by total orders to get the rate, and multiply by what one damaged order costs: replacement, return freight and repack. Add that per-order allowance to the other lines.
