# Reduce Damage and RTO: A 12-Point Packing Checklist

To cut transit damage, pack to 12 points: right-size the box, choose board by weight, use a new dry box, stop the contents moving, cushion fragile items, load heavy items low, tape every seam, label clearly, mark the orientation, photograph before sealing, test one sample, and log every damage. Packing cannot fix a wrong address.

*Last checked: October 2026. This is a practical checklist, not a courier or platform rule or a guarantee against damage. Check your courier's and marketplace's own packaging terms.*

## The checklist

Photograph this table, print it or paste it into your packing station's chat. Each point has a short reason below.

| # | Check | Done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The box is the **right size**: snug, with padding and nothing more | ☐ |
| 2 | The **board suits the weight**, not the size of the parcel | ☐ |
| 3 | The box is **new, dry and undamaged** | ☐ |
| 4 | The contents **cannot move**: shake test passed | ☐ |
| 5 | **Fragile items** are wrapped alone with cushioning on every side | ☐ |
| 6 | **Heavy items sit low**, light ones on top | ☐ |
| 7 | **Every seam is taped**, centre and both edges | ☐ |
| 8 | The **label** is on the top face, flat, and an address copy is inside | ☐ |
| 9 | **Orientation and fragile marks** are on more than one face | ☐ |
| 10 | A **photo** of the packed, open box is taken before sealing | ☐ |
| 11 | One **sample per product** has been tested | ☐ |
| 12 | Every damaged or returned parcel is **logged with its cause** | ☐ |

## The 12 points in detail

### 1. Right-size the box

A box that is too big lets the product slide and costs more to ship, because couriers can bill on [volumetric weight](/blogs/volumetric-weight-calculator-india). A box that is too small crushes the product. The designer's fit checker adds 1 cm of padding on each side (2.5 cm for fragile goods) plus 0.3 cm of ease, and rounds up to 0.5 cm. Read [find the right box size](/blogs/find-right-box-size) or use the free [box size calculator](/tools/box-size-calculator).

### 2. Choose the board by weight

Size and weight are separate questions. A big box of pillows is light, and a small box of tools is heavy. In the designer's guidance, 3-ply is comfortable for contents up to about 8 kg and 5-ply up to about 20 kg. That is a planning guide, not a promise. See [how many kg a corrugated box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold).

### 3. Use a new, dry box

A box that has been used has weaker walls and creases, and a damp box is weaker still. Store boxes flat, off the floor and away from the monsoon damp. Never reuse a box for a long trip.

### 4. Stop the contents moving

Fill every gap with paper, air pillows or foam. Close the lid, hold the box and shake it. If you can hear or feel movement, add filler. Movement inside is the most common cause of damage.

### 5. Cushion fragile items

Wrap each piece on its own so pieces never touch, and keep 2.5 cm of cushioning on every side. The full method, with box sizes, is in [how to pack fragile items for courier](/blogs/how-to-pack-fragile-items-for-courier).

### 6. Load heavy items low

Heavy goods at the bottom, light goods on top. A heavy item on top of a fragile one is a common cause of crush damage. If one item is much heavier than the rest, use a stronger board.

### 7. Tape every seam

Close the centre seam and both edges with strong tape, the H pattern. Weak tape and a single strip are common ways a box opens. For heavy boxes, add a second layer on the bottom seams. See our [BOPP tape](/products/bopp-tape) page.

### 8. Label well

Put the address label flat on the top face, away from the seams, so it does not tear when the box opens. Remove old labels and barcodes from reused boxes, because they confuse sorting. Put a copy of the address inside.

### 9. Mark the orientation

Add "this side up" and "fragile" marks on more than one face. They ask for care; they do not guarantee it. The designer can print fragile, this-side-up and keep-dry icons on the box through its shipping icons option.

### 10. Photograph before sealing

Take a photo of the packed, open box with the product and the order slip visible, and note the weight. If a customer or the courier disputes the condition, you have a record. Check each courier's and marketplace's own claim rules, which we do not state here.

### 11. Test one sample per product

Pack a real product the way you will ship it. Shake it, drop it a few times from table height onto each face and corner, then open it and look. One sample test costs a few rupees and can save a whole batch. This is an informal check, not a certified drop test.

### 12. Log every damage with its cause

Keep a simple sheet: date, product, damage, cause (box crushed, product moved, tape opened, label lost). After a month, patterns appear. If one product is damaged more than the rest, fix its pack first.

## What packaging can fix, and what it cannot

| Problem | Can packing help? | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Item broken or crushed | Yes | Points 1 to 6 |
| Box opened in transit | Yes | Points 3 and 7 |
| Item scuffed by the label or courier marks | Partly | A plain shipper around a printed box |
| "Item not as described" or wrong item | Partly | Photo before sealing, a tamper-evident seal |
| Wrong or incomplete address | No | Address checks before dispatch |
| Customer refuses delivery | No | Order confirmation and your own policies |

RTO (return to origin) means a parcel comes back undelivered. A part of it is damage and tampering, which packing can reduce. A part of it has nothing to do with the box. We cannot give you a percentage, because it depends on your product, city, courier and payment mix. Measure your own, and split returns into "damaged" and "other". The [packaging cost worksheet](/blogs/packaging-cost-per-order-worksheet) shows how to cost a damaged order.

## Does a window, vent or hand hole change the checklist?

Yes. Cut-outs weaken a box. The designer's strength guidance warns that hand holes and vents often reduce stacking strength by about 10 to 40% depending on size and position, so check the strength panel and test a sample. A window with film needs the lid to stay stiff. See [vents, hand holes and windows](/blogs/vents-hand-holes-windows-box-design). For parcels that will be stacked, avoid cut-outs on the load-bearing walls.

## A one-minute packing-station routine

1. Pick the box that matches the order from a short size card on the wall (two or three sizes).
2. Check the box is new and dry. Put a layer of filler on the bottom.
3. Place the heavy item, then the rest. Wrap fragile pieces on their own.
4. Fill the gaps. Close, shake, add filler if needed.
5. Photograph the open box. Seal with the H tape pattern.
6. Label the top, add the orientation marks, and weigh.

A packer who follows this does the same thing for the thousandth order as for the first. Few sizes also keep box orders larger, which can help price; see [minimum order quantity for custom boxes](/blogs/minimum-order-quantity-custom-boxes).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reduce damage in courier shipping?

Use a snug, new corrugated box suited to the weight, stop the contents moving with filler, cushion fragile items on every side, tape every seam, and test a packed sample. Then log every damage with its cause, so you can fix the weakest pack first.

### Does packing reduce RTO?

Partly. Good packing cuts damage, tampering and some "item not as described" returns. It does not fix wrong addresses or refused deliveries. Track your returns by cause so you know how much is packaging.

### How do I stop items moving inside the box?

Fill every gap with paper, air pillows or foam, and use a box that is only as big as the product plus padding. Close the box and shake it. If anything moves, add filler.

### Which tape is best for sealing parcels?

Use strong tape in the H pattern: the centre seam and both edges. A single thin strip is a common weak point. Heavier parcels benefit from a second layer on the bottom seams.

### Should I take a photo before sealing a parcel?

It is a good habit. A photo of the open box with the product and the order slip, plus the weight, gives you a record if the condition is disputed. Check your courier's and marketplace's own rules on claims.
