# Pizza and Bakery Boxes: Sizes, Board and Design Tips for Food Businesses

If you run a pizzeria, a bakery or a cloud kitchen, your box does a lot of quiet work. It keeps food warm and in shape on the back of a delivery bike, it stacks on the counter during the dinner rush, and it carries your name into the customer's home. When it fails, the cheese slides into a corner, the cake gets dented, or a soggy base leaves the customer with a poor last impression.

This guide covers how to choose sizes, board and print for pizza and bakery boxes, and how to design and preview them in the free **3D Box Designer** before you order.

## The problem: one box size does not fit every menu

Food businesses often buy boxes the way they buy disposables: whatever the local supplier has. The result is familiar:

- **Pizzas slide around** in a box that is too wide, and toppings end up on the lid.
- **Lids touch the food** because the box is too shallow for a thick base or a decorated cake.
- **Boxes go soft** from steam and grease on longer deliveries.
- **The counter is cluttered** with several almost-identical sizes.
- **The brand disappears.** A plain box looks the same as the shop next door.

Designing your own boxes means sizing them to your actual menu and using the lid as a small billboard.

---

## Food-contact and hygiene: a careful note

Packaging that touches food needs extra care, and the right choice depends on what you are packing.

- **Ask for board suitable for food contact** when the food sits directly on the board. Tell your supplier exactly what will touch it: hot pizza, oily snacks, cream cakes or dry biscuits.
- **Greasy or moist food** usually needs a suitable barrier: a coated or lined inside surface, a greaseproof liner, a base paper or a separate tray or board under the food.
- **Inks and print** on surfaces that may touch food need particular attention. Many businesses keep print to the outside and leave the food-contact side unprinted.
- **Rules apply.** Food-safety regulations in India cover packaging that comes into contact with food. Requirements depend on the food and the use, so confirm them with your food-safety adviser and ask your supplier for the relevant documentation.

## Choosing pizza box sizes

Pizza boxes are usually described by the pizza diameter they hold. Common Indian menus use small, medium and large pizzas of roughly 7, 10 and 12 inches, with some shops offering larger family sizes. Your menu is what matters, so measure your own bases.

**A simple sizing rule:** make the inside of the box a little larger than the pizza, enough that it does not touch the walls or need to be squeezed in, but not so much that it slides around. Then set the inside height so the lid clears the toppings comfortably.

| Pizza (indicative) | Starting inside size to test | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 7 inch (small) | about 7.5 × 7.5 inch | Shallow box is usually enough |
| 10 inch (medium) | about 10.5 × 10.5 inch | Check height for thick crusts |
| 12 inch (large) | about 12.5 × 12.5 inch | Consider 5-ply or E/B flute for rigidity |

## Choosing bakery box styles

Bakeries pack a wider range of shapes. Three styles cover most needs:

- **Tray with a separate lid** (a telescope or lid-and-base pair): good for cakes, where the lid lifts straight off without touching the decoration.
- **Tray only**: for pastries, cookies or sweets that will be wrapped or put in a carry bag.
- **Mailer-style folding box**: for cookies, brownies and gift assortments that need a neat hinged lid.

For cakes, the height matters most. Measure the tallest point, including any decoration, then add clearance so the lid does not touch the icing.

## Practical tips for food businesses

- **Standardise sizes.** Fewer sizes means less storage space, simpler ordering and better prices per size.
- **Order flat, store dry.** Corrugated board absorbs moisture. Keep stock off the floor and away from steam.
- **Think about stacking.** During a rush, boxes stack on the counter and in delivery bags. A stiffer board helps.
- **Ventilation for hot food.** Steam softens board and makes crusts soggy. Ask about vent options for hot deliveries.
- **Keep the food side plain.** Print the outside and leave food-contact surfaces unprinted unless your supplier confirms the inks are suitable.
- **Use the lid for repeat orders.** A phone number, website or QR code on the lid is the cheapest marketing you will do.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size pizza box do I need?

Measure your pizza bases and make the inside of the box slightly larger than the pizza, with enough height for the toppings. Try your sizes in the designer before ordering.

### Are corrugated pizza boxes safe for food?

Corrugated board is widely used for food delivery, but what is suitable depends on the food and how it touches the board. Ask for board suitable for food contact, consider a liner for greasy food, and confirm requirements with your food-safety adviser.

### Can I print on the inside of a pizza box?

You can design an inside print in the tool, but many businesses keep the food-contact side unprinted. Discuss inks and food contact with us before printing inside.

### Which flute is best for pizza boxes?

E and B flute are common for pizza boxes because they are stiff, light and print well. The right choice depends on size and delivery distance.

### Can I design a cake box with a separate lid?

Yes. Use the telescope (lid and base) style and set the lid depth. The lid lifts straight off without touching the decoration.

### How many sizes should a bakery stock?

As few as your products allow. Many bakeries cover most items with two or three tray sizes and one or two cake box heights.

### Can I order a small quantity to test?

Minimum quantities depend on the style, size and print. Send your design and quantity and we will tell you what is possible.
