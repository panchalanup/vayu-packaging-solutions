# Pizza Box Sizes, Board and Design for Pizzerias and Bakeries

A pizza box has one job before it has a brand: keep a hot, round, cheesy thing flat and in one piece on the back of a delivery bike. A box that is too wide lets the pizza slide, one that is too shallow squashes the toppings, and a soft base ruins the last impression.

This guide is about **pizza boxes**: how to size them for your menu, which board suits, what to do about grease and steam, and how to design the lid. It also covers flat bakery items that use the same box. For cakes and sweets read [cake, sweet and bakery boxes](/blogs/cake-sweet-bakery-boxes), and for burgers and meals read [takeaway boxes for cloud kitchens](/blogs/takeaway-tiffin-pizza-boxes-cloud-kitchens).

## What a pizza box is

A pizza box is a shallow, square, die-cut box with a hinged lid, built like a mailer. It folds flat for storage, closes without tape and gives you a large flat lid for print. It is made as one piece from a cutting die, so each size needs its own die, which is a one-time cost. See [how die-cut and slotted styles differ](/blogs/box-styles-in-3d).

## Size the box from the pizza

Pizza sizes on menus are in inches, so work in inches and let the designer show the metric equivalent. The inside of the box must be a little bigger than the pizza so it slides in without touching the walls, but not so big that it slides around.

| Menu size | Pizza diameter | Starting inside size to test | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 inch | about 20 cm | 21 × 21 × 5 cm | Shallow box is enough |
| 10 inch | about 25 cm | 26 × 26 × 5 cm | Check height for thick crusts |
| 12 inch | about 30.5 cm | 31 × 31 × 5 cm | Tight: add a little if your pizzas run wide or have a puffy crust |

These are the starting sizes in the designer's pizza preset, not a standard. <!-- VERIFY-LATER[BLOG-53]: pizza sizes and clearance are indicative starting points (the preset gives about 0.5 cm margin on a 12 inch pizza); confirm typical clearances with production and with a real pizza from a customer before publishing as a recommendation. --> Measure your own bases, including a crust that rises, and test one box before you order a run. Keep the height low: a tall box lets slices slide and traps steam, but a box that is too shallow flattens the toppings.

## Board and flute

Pizza boxes are commonly made from thin corrugated board, in E or B flute, because it is stiff, light, folds cleanly and prints well. The designer's pizza preset uses 3-ply. A 12 inch pizza on a long delivery may justify a stiffer board. Kraft hides grease marks better, and white gives brighter print. See [flute types compared](/blogs/flute-types-guide).

## Grease, steam and food contact

This is general guidance, not food-safety advice:

- **Plain corrugated board is not a grease barrier.** Oily pizzas can mark and soften the base. Ask about a liner, a greaseproof sheet or a coating, and tell your supplier what you serve.
- **Steam softens board.** A sealed box traps steam, which can leave crust soggy. Allow some air release, and do not keep hot pizzas sealed for long.
- **Print the outside** and keep the food-contact surface plain unless your supplier confirms the inks are suitable.
- **Food rules apply** to packaging that touches food. Confirm requirements with your food-safety adviser and ask your supplier what documentation they can provide.
- **The designer plans size, style and print.** It does not certify any board or ink as food safe.

## Step by step: design a pizza box

1. **Open the [pizza box designer](/box-designer/pizza-box).** It loads a shallow, square box with a hinged lid.
2. **Set the size.** In the **Edit Box** tab, switch to inches if your menu uses them, and enter the inside size for your first pizza. Save each menu size as its own design so you can compare them.
3. **Choose board and colour.** Kraft or white, and an inside colour if you want to see a white inside.
4. **Brand the lid.** In the **Customize** tab, place your logo, a phone number or a QR code for ordering. Keep it bold and simple, and let the print checks catch small text.
5. **Watch it fold.** Run the fold slider to check the lid opens fully and the tuck closes neatly. Use the cut-away view to see the space above the pizza.
6. **Check the price and send it.** In **Price & order**, look at the indicative estimate at your monthly quantity, then send the design to us with a note about the food you pack.

## Practical tips

- **Standardise sizes.** Two or three sizes simplify stock and give better prices.
- **Store flat and dry.** Corrugated board absorbs moisture, so keep stock off the floor and away from steam.
- **Think about stacking.** Boxes stack in the delivery bag and on the counter at rush hour, and a stiffer board helps.
- **Use the lid.** A phone number or QR code on the lid is cheap repeat-order marketing.
- **Test a real order.** Pack a hot pizza, take a 20-minute ride and check the base and lid.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size pizza box do I need?
Measure your pizza bases and make the inside a little larger than the pizza, with enough height for the toppings. Try the sizes in the designer before ordering.

### Which flute is best for pizza boxes?
E and B flute are common because they are stiff, light and print well. The right choice depends on pizza size and delivery distance.

### Are corrugated pizza boxes safe for food?
Corrugated board is widely used for food delivery, but what is suitable depends on the food and how it touches the board. Ask for board suitable for food contact, consider a liner for greasy food and confirm requirements with your food-safety adviser.

### Can I print inside a pizza box?
You can design inside print in the tool, but many businesses keep the food-contact side unprinted. Discuss inks and food contact with us first.

### Can I order a small quantity to test?
Minimum quantities depend on style, size and print. Send your design and quantity and we will tell you what is possible.
