# Packaging Product Shots and 360° Videos, No Photoshoot

You need pictures of your packaging long before you have the boxes. The launch post, the pitch deck, the product page "what's in the box" image, the investor update, the slide for your distributor: all of them want a good-looking box **now**, while the real one is still a quote.

A photoshoot needs a sample, a photographer, lights and a day. The free **3D Box Designer** gives you a quicker option for many of these uses: **studio-style images and short 360° videos** of your exact box, with your artwork, made from the same 3D model you designed.

This guide shows how to make them, how to make them look good, and where a render is the right choice (and where it is not).

## Step by step: a product shot in two minutes

1. **Finish the design first.** Style, size, board, artwork. Clear the [print checks](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes) so the picture shows a box that can actually be printed.
2. **Open the Showcase tab.**
3. **Pick a background.** Options include a clean **studio** backdrop, **warm wood**, **concrete**, or your **brand colour**.
4. **Pick an angle.** Use a preset (3/4 hero, front, side, top or back 3/4) or rotate the box yourself.
5. **Set the fold.** A closed box is classic; a half-open mailer shows the inside print and invites the viewer in.
6. **Choose the resolution:** **2x** for web and social, **4x** for print and large screens.
7. **Optional watermark,** if you are sending drafts to a client.
8. **Download** the PNG.

## Step by step: a 360° or fold video

1. Set up the box and background as for a still shot.
2. Choose **360° spin** or **fold**.
3. Press **record**. The designer plays the animation and records it to a short clip of about 8 seconds, ready for reels and status updates.
4. Download the video. It is saved as **MP4** where your browser supports it, otherwise as **WebM**, which plays in browsers and most apps. Convert it with any free converter if a platform rejects it.

**Tip:** a fold video of a **mailer** or **telescope gift box** is one of the most effective ways to explain a box without words. The viewer sees the lid open and the inside print appear.

## Making renders look good

The 3D model does the hard work, but a few choices separate an ordinary image from a good one.

### Angles
- **Three-quarter view** (two sides and the top) is the most informative and the most "product-like".
- **Front-on** works for flat graphic layouts, in grids and on catalogue pages.
- **Top-down** suits pizza boxes, trays and anything with a printed lid.
- **Lid half open** (set with the fold slider) shows inside printing and makes the box feel tactile.

### Backgrounds
- **Studio white or light grey** for marketplaces, catalogues and anything that will be cut out.
- **Wood** for food, handmade, gifting and anything warm.
- **Concrete** for tech, tools and industrial goods.
- **Brand colour** for social posts, where recognition matters more than realism. Choose a shade that contrasts with the box: a kraft box on a kraft-brown background disappears.

### Consistency
If you are making a set (a range of sizes, or one box per product), keep the **same angle, background and resolution** for all of them. A consistent set looks professional even when each image is simple.

### Show real size
A box floating on a plain background has no scale. Use the **size reference** objects (an A4 sheet or a 1 litre bottle) when the size is part of the message, for example "fits through a letterbox" or "holds six bottles".

## Renders vs photos: be honest about which is which

A render shows **the design**: shape, size, artwork placement and colour intent. A photo shows **the product**: the real board texture, the actual print, the real colours. Both are useful, but for different jobs.

**Renders are ideal for:** pre-launch marketing, decks and proposals, explaining a box style, internal approvals, and secondary images.

**Use real photos for:** the main image on marketplaces that require photos of the actual product (check your marketplace's image rules), anything that promises exact print colour, and customer reviews or case studies.

Print colours on board, especially on kraft, differ from screen colours. A render is not a colour proof. When colour matters, approve a printed proof.

When you post a render publicly, a simple caption such as "packaging design preview" keeps things clear.

## Tips

- **Finish the artwork before the shoot.** It is tempting to shoot early; you will end up shooting twice.
- **Use 4x for anything that might be printed** (a catalogue, a banner). You can always shrink a large image.
- **Keep videos short.** A single smooth spin or fold is better than a long clip.
- **Name your files** with the product and size, for example "mailer-250x180x80-front.png"; it saves confusion later.
- **Save the design** in My designs so you can come back and re-shoot after a change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use the images commercially?
Yes. Images of your own design are yours to use in your marketing.

### What resolution are the images?
You choose 2x or 4x of the on-screen view. 4x is large enough for most print uses.

### What format is the video?
MP4 where the browser supports it, otherwise WebM. Both play in browsers and most apps.

### Can I see the box in AR?
On iPhone and iPad you can open an AR view and place the box on a real table at real size. On other devices you can download the 3D model (GLB).

### Are renders accurate enough to approve a box?
For shape, size and artwork placement, yes; that is what the designer is for. For exact print colour, approve a printed proof.
