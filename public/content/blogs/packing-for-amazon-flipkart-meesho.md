# Packing for Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho: Seller Checklist

Each marketplace sets its own packaging rules and changes them, so read the packaging pages in your seller account first. Beyond that, the basics work everywhere: a box sized to the product, a board that matches the weight, proper tape, a free label area and a returns plan. This checklist covers those basics and states no platform rule.

*Last checked: October 2026. We have not copied any platform's rules here, on purpose: confirm them in your own seller account.*

[Open a seller-size mailer in 3D](/box-designer/mailer-box)

If you sell on more than one marketplace, you will soon notice that you cannot find one definitive rule sheet that covers all of them. Programmes differ, and a rule that applies to one way of shipping may not apply to another. So this article does two separate jobs. It shows you where to look for the rules, and it gives you a practical checklist of good packing habits that cost little and prevent damage, whichever platform sends the order.

## Where the rules live

The rules come from the marketplace, not from your box supplier. Look inside your own seller account, in the help or learning section, and search for "packaging" or "packing". Names and menus change, so use the list below as questions to answer, not as facts.

| Marketplace | Where to look | Questions to answer from its page |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | The help pages in your Seller Central account, for the programme you use | Is there a packaging requirement for my programme? Any limits on box size or weight? Labelling rules? |
| Flipkart | The seller help and learning pages in your Seller Hub account | Who supplies the packaging for my shipping method? How should I label and seal? What applies to returns? |
| Meesho | The help pages in your Supplier Panel | What packing guidance applies to my category? Who arranges pick-up? What happens to a damaged return? |

Write down the answers you find, with the date. Platform pages are updated without notice, so a note from last year can be wrong this year.

## The checklist

These are practical habits, not platform requirements. Use them as a baseline and add whatever your platform asks for.

### 1. Size the box to the product

Empty space is the cause of most transit damage, and it also raises your shipping bill. Measure the product (or its inner pack), add padding, and order the **inside** size. The designer's fit checker starts at 1 cm of padding per side for ordinary goods and 2.5 cm for fragile ones, and rounds up to the next 0.5 cm. See [how to find the right box size](/blogs/find-right-box-size) and the free [box size calculator](/tools/box-size-calculator).

### 2. Check the volumetric weight before you order

Couriers usually bill on the greater of actual weight and volumetric weight, where volumetric weight is length x width x height in centimetres divided by a number that depends on the courier and the service. Here is the arithmetic for a 30 x 20 x 15 cm parcel: 9,000 cm3 divided by 5,000 is 1.8 kg; divided by 4,000 it is 2.25 kg. Use your own courier's divisor, not these. A box 3 cm shorter in each direction can be a different billing slab. The [volumetric weight calculator](/tools/volumetric-weight-calculator) does the sum, and the [measuring guide](/blogs/box-measurements-guide) explains it further. <!-- VERIFY-LATER[BLOG-408]: the 4,000 and 5,000 divisors are examples only (see BLOG-210). Confirm which divisor each courier or marketplace logistics programme uses before quoting either figure as typical. -->

### 3. Match the board to the weight

As a starting guide, the designer treats about 8 kg per box as comfortable for 3-ply, about 20 kg for 5-ply and about 40 kg for 7-ply. Most light parcels are fine in 3-ply. For heavier goods, glass, ceramics or anything that will be stacked, go up a ply. Read [how many kg a corrugated box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold) before you decide, and test a packed sample.

### 5. Seal the box properly

Use proper box tape, not household tape. Run it along the centre seam and over both edges, so the flaps cannot lift. Add a second strip for heavy boxes. A box that opens in transit is the most avoidable kind of loss.

### 6. Fill the gaps

If the product moves when you shake the closed box, add paper, a card insert or a wrapped inner pack. Shake it once more. The aim is a box that is snug without bulging.

### 7. Protect the fragile

Fragile goods need padding on every side, a wrapped product and a stronger board. If the product sits in an inner box, the outer carton needs padding around that, too. For a glass or ceramic item, do your own simple drop check: close the box, drop it flat and then on a corner from waist height onto a hard floor, and open it. That is your own quick test, not a certified one.

### 8. Leave a plain area for the label

Keep one face of the box, usually the top or a large side, free of print and stickers so the shipping label sits flat and the barcode is easy to scan. If your artwork wraps all the way round, leave a clear panel.

### 9. Plan for returns

Returns arrive in whatever box the buyer finds, often the original one. Choose a box that can be opened without tearing and sealed again with tape, and avoid glue-heavy inserts. Check what your platform says about returned goods, and photograph a good pack and a damaged one as a record.

### 10. Check before you brand

Printing your logo is fine for many sellers, but platform policies on branded boxes, inserts and outer packaging differ between programmes and over time. Read your platform's page, then print. If you are not sure, start with a plain box and a label.

### 11. Test a small batch first

Order a small batch of one size, pack ten real orders and look at the results: crushed corners, torn tape, products that moved. Fix the box before you order in volume. See [minimum order quantity for custom boxes](/blogs/minimum-order-quantity-custom-boxes) for how much you may need to buy at once.

## A one-page packer's checklist

Print this and tape it above the packing table.

- [ ] Box inside size matches the product (no big gaps)
- [ ] Shake test passed (nothing rattles)
- [ ] Fragile goods padded on all six sides
- [ ] Tape on the centre seam and both edges
- [ ] One plain area free for the label
- [ ] Label flat, barcode not creased
- [ ] Invoice or insert inside, not stuck outside
- [ ] Photo of the packed parcel for the record (if your process needs it)

## Where the money goes

Packaging costs add up across many orders, and its mistakes cost more than the box itself. The three that matter most:

1. **Shipping a box that is too big,** which can push you into a higher weight slab.
2. **Using a board that is too light,** which turns into replacements and refunds.
3. **Ordering many sizes,** each with its own setup and minimum order.

The free [packaging cost calculator](/tools/packaging-cost-calculator) lets you add the box, tape, filler and a damage allowance to see your real cost per order.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho require a specific box?

Requirements depend on the programme and the way you ship, and they change. We do not state any here. Check the packaging pages in your own seller account, and ask your logistics partner if you use one.

### Can I print my logo on the box?

You can order printed boxes from us, but whether a platform accepts branded outer packaging for your programme is for the platform to say. Read its policy before you print, and start with a plain box if you are unsure.

### Which box size is best for returns?

There is no best size. Pick a box that fits the product snugly, opens without tearing and can be re-taped. Ask your platform what it expects for returned items.

### How do I reduce volumetric weight?

Make the box fit the product. Remove empty space, choose a flatter style such as a mailer for soft goods, and compare sizes in the volumetric weight calculator with your courier's own divisor.

### Is 3-ply enough for courier parcels?

For light goods it often is. The designer treats about 8 kg per box as comfortable for 3-ply. For heavier or fragile goods, or stacked cartons, 5-ply is safer. Test a packed sample either way.
