# How to Choose a Corrugated Box Manufacturer in Ahmedabad

Choose a corrugated box manufacturer in Ahmedabad by asking twelve questions: who makes the box, minimum order, samples, written spec, size basis, proof, print charges, GST invoice, lead time, delivery, fault policy and who answers. Get every answer in writing, compare two quotes line by line, and be wary of anyone who will not answer.

## How to use this list

Buyers in Ahmedabad often search by area as well as by product, and the choice can look hard: many suppliers, similar claims and prices that are hard to compare. A short list of questions makes the comparison fair.

1. Write your spec first: inside size, quantity, ply, print and delivery PIN code.
2. Send the **same spec** to two or three suppliers.
3. Ask the twelve questions below, by phone or WhatsApp, and keep the replies in writing.
4. Compare the answers, not just the price.

We are a supplier, so we have an interest in your choice. The list is written to work whichever supplier you pick.

## The questions behind the questions

### Who makes the box (questions 1 and 2)

Some suppliers make boxes on their own machines, some convert board bought from mills, and some trade boxes made elsewhere. None of these is wrong, but you should know which it is, because it decides who can fix a problem and how quickly. Ask for a visit or photos before a large or repeat order, or a video call.

Minimum orders exist because cutting dies, print plates and machine set-up cost the same for 100 boxes as for 1,000. The more fixed cost is spread across fewer boxes, the higher each box costs. Ask what happens under the minimum: a stock size, a smaller run at a higher rate, or a different supplier.

### Samples, spec and size (questions 3 to 5)

A sample shows you the board, the print and the folds in your hands, and it is the cheapest insurance you can buy. Ask what the sample costs and how long it takes.

A spec in writing means ply, flute, paper weight and, where you need it, strength test values. Our guides explain what the terms mean: [types of corrugated boxes](/blogs/types-of-corrugated-boxes), [GSM and BF](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength) and [burst strength and ECT](/blogs/burst-strength-ect-guide). Ask which tests the supplier runs and whether a report can be shared on request.

Always settle inside versus outside size. Box makers normally quote inside size, the space for your product. If you quote outside size by mistake the box will not fit, and the argument that follows is common. See [inside versus outside size](/blogs/box-measurements-guide) for the difference.

### Proof and print (questions 6 and 7)

Never approve printing on a verbal OK. Ask for a dieline with your artwork on it, and a 3D preview or printed proof. For exact colours, ask for a printed proof.

The 3D designer on this site gives you a preview, a dieline in SVG and PDF, and a one-page spec sheet PDF with an approval block, so you can send the same file to any supplier for a quote. Read [dielines explained](/blogs/dielines-explained) for what the dieline file contains. For print charges, read [how the price estimate works](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide).

### Paper trail (question 8)

Ask for a GST tax invoice with the GSTIN and the HSN code printed on it, and verify the GSTIN on the government portal. Corrugated cartons are generally classified under heading 4819, and rates can change, so check the rate on your invoice against the current notification or ask your accountant. This is general information, not tax advice.

### Time and delivery (questions 9 and 10)

Lead time depends on whether the size is in stock, whether it needs a new die, whether it has print, and how busy the season is. Festive and wedding seasons are busy for everyone, so order early; our [festive packaging guide](/blogs/festive-gift-hamper-boxes) covers planning.

For delivery, give your PIN code and ask for the freight in the quote, not after.

### After the order (questions 11 and 12)

Ask what happens if something is wrong, and get it in writing: the time window to report, the proof you must send (photos, the batch label), and the remedy (replacement, credit or rework). Then ask who you will speak to.

## Compare two quotes line by line

Price per box is only one line. Put each quote in the same grid:

| Line | Quote A | Quote B |
|---|---|---|
| Inside size (mm) | | |
| Ply, flute and paper GSM or BF | | |
| Quantity and tolerance | | |
| Print and number of colours | | |
| One-time costs (die, plates) | | |
| Price per box | | |
| GST shown separately | | |
| Freight to your PIN code | | |
| Lead time and what it depends on | | |
| Sample, proof and fault policy | | |

Then work out the landed cost per box. If the price per box is P, the one-time costs are D, freight is F and the quantity is N:

**Landed cost per box = P + (D + F) / N**

The one-time part shrinks as quantity rises: spread over 500 boxes it is 10 times what it is over 5,000, because 5,000 / 500 = 10. So a supplier with a low P and a high D can look cheaper at 5,000 and dearer at 500. Compare at the quantity you will actually order, then add GST.

## Visit checklist, if you can go

You do not need to be an expert to learn something from a visit.

- Is finished stock stored flat and dry, away from the floor and from water?
- Are boxes labelled by order or batch?
- Do the folds and creases look straight, and do the flaps meet squarely?
- Is the print crisp, with the colour matching the sample?
- Can they show you the dieline and the spec for your order?

If you cannot visit, ask for a video call.

## Check your first delivery

When the boxes arrive, check before you sign or unpack:

1. **Count** the cartons against the invoice.
2. **Measure** three boxes with a tape and compare with the quoted inside size.
3. **Read the print** against the approved proof.
4. **Check the board:** thickness, and no dampness or warping.
5. **Open a few** and look at the glue or stitch joint and the creases.
6. **Photograph problems** the same day, with the label, and report within the window you were given.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find a reliable corrugated box manufacturer in Ahmedabad?

Send the same written spec to two or three suppliers, ask the twelve questions above, and compare the written answers. Look for specifics: numbers, dates and named contacts. Order a sample first and visit if you can.

### Should I choose the cheapest quote?

Not on price per box alone. Compare the spec line by line, include one-time costs, freight and GST, and work out the landed cost per box at the quantity you will really order. A low price for a thinner board or a looser size is not the same product.

### Can I order a small quantity of custom boxes?

It depends on the supplier, because dies, plates and set-up cost the same for a small run as a large one. Ask each supplier for its minimum and what it can offer below that. Our website currently states a minimum order of 500 boxes.

### Do I need a GST invoice for corrugated boxes?

If you are a registered business claiming input credit, you normally need a tax invoice with GSTIN. Check the GSTIN on the government portal and ask your accountant about the rate and HSN code. This is not tax advice.

### How do I check box quality when the order arrives?

Count the cartons, measure three against the quoted inside size, compare the print with the proof, check the board for dampness, and photograph problems the same day. Report within the window you were given in writing. For strength, only a compression test in a lab gives a real number.
