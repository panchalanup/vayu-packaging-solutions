# What Does a 5 Ply Box Cost? Worked Estimator Examples

A 5-ply box has no single price: it depends on size, print, quantity and style. As an illustration, the designer's estimator puts a 40 x 30 x 25 cm kraft carton with one-colour print at roughly ₹47 to ₹63 a box for 500, before GST and delivery. That is an indicative range built on starting rates, not a quote.

*Last checked: October 2026. These ranges are generated by the designer's estimator on 27 October 2026 and will change when the rates behind it are confirmed.*

[Run your own estimate in the designer](/box-designer?style=rsc&l=40&w=30&h=25&ply=5-ply&fl=BC)

You will not find a price list on this page, and that is deliberate. Corrugated box prices move with paper costs, and a box that is 5 cm bigger or has one more colour on it is a different price. What this page does instead is show you how the number is built, run the designer's own estimate for three sizes and three quantities, and show which choices move it most. With that, you can read any quote, ours included, with some confidence.

## The examples

Every row uses the same box: a regular slotted carton (RSC) in 5-ply with B + C flute, natural kraft board, matte, with one colour of print (a logo). Sizes are **inside** dimensions. Prices are per box, before GST and delivery, rounded to the rupee.

| Inside size | 500 boxes | 1,000 boxes | 5,000 boxes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small, 25 x 20 x 15 cm | ₹23 to ₹31 | ₹20 to ₹27 | ₹17 to ₹23 |
| Medium, 40 x 30 x 25 cm | ₹47 to ₹63 | ₹43 to ₹58 | ₹38 to ₹51 |
| Large, 60 x 40 x 40 cm | ₹90 to ₹122 | ₹83 to ₹113 | ₹75 to ₹102 |

## How the estimator builds a price

The designer's estimate follows the way a board plant thinks about a box. For each design it works out:

1. **The flat blank area** the box is cut from, in square metres, plus a wastage allowance for trim and set-up.
2. **Board cost** for that area, at a rate that depends on the ply. 5-ply costs more per square metre than 3-ply, and white board adds a little over kraft.
3. **Print cost** by the colour and the area, plus a one-time plate cost for each colour. A photo counts as four colours.
4. **Finish**, if you choose gloss varnish.
5. **Conversion:** slotting, gluing or stitching and bundling for each box, plus window film if the design has a window.
6. **A quantity factor** that is higher for short runs and lower for large ones.
7. **One-time setup,** meaning plates and, for die-cut styles, a cutting die, spread over the number of boxes.
8. A **plus or minus 15 per cent** range around the result.

The rates for each step are inputs the team can change in one place, which is why the numbers here will change. The [price estimate guide](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide) explains each step in words, and this page puts numbers to it.

## The five levers that move the price

The table shows what each lever did to the medium box (40 x 30 x 25 cm, 5-ply, one colour, 500 boxes), keeping everything else the same. Percentages compare midpoints.

| Lever | Change | Estimator effect on the medium box | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Size** | Small to large | about four times the price | More board, in proportion to the blank area |
| **2. Ply** | 5-ply to 3-ply | about 40% lower | Cheaper board per square metre, slightly smaller blank |
| | 5-ply to 7-ply | about 47% higher | More layers, more paper |
| **3. Print** | One colour to none | about 12% lower | No print cost and no plate |
| | One colour to four-colour image | about 37% higher | Four plates and more ink |
| **4. Quantity** | 500 to 1,000 | about 9% lower | Setup spread wider, volume factor |
| | 500 to 5,000 | about 19% lower | Same effect, larger |
| **5. Finish and style** | Add gloss varnish | about 8% higher | Varnish over the whole blank |
| | White board instead of kraft | about 7% higher | Premium on the board rate |
| | Die-cut style (mailer) instead of RSC | a one-time die cost, and a bigger blank | See below |

A few of these deserve a comment.

**Size is the biggest lever.** Price follows the area of the blank, and area grows faster than any single dimension. Taking 3 cm off each side of an oversized box saves more than most people expect. It also lowers volumetric weight if you ship by courier. The [box size calculator](/tools/box-size-calculator) and [find the right box size](/blogs/find-right-box-size) help you get to the smallest box that still protects the product.

**Ply is the next lever, and it is also the one to be careful with.** Going from 5-ply to 3-ply cuts a lot from the estimate, but 3-ply is comfortable for about 8 kg of contents against about 20 kg for 5-ply. Check the weight before you save the money; see [how many kg a corrugated box can hold](/blogs/how-many-kg-can-corrugated-box-hold).

**Quantity helps up to a point.** From 500 to 5,000 the per-box figure falls by about a fifth, mainly because setup is shared. Most of that comes early; the step from 500 to 1,000 is small.

**Style carries a die.** For a mailer at the same inside size (40 x 30 x 10 cm, 3-ply, 500 boxes, one colour), the estimator comes out about two-thirds higher than an RSC, because the mailer blank is larger and the die is a one-time cost on top of the plate. If the box is mostly seen by couriers, an RSC may be enough.

## What the estimate leaves out

The designer labels the figure "ex-GST, ex-delivery", and so do these examples. Four things are outside it:

- **GST.** Ask how it is shown on your quote and invoice, and read your own tax advice for the rate. We do not state one here.
- **Delivery.** Transport to your address depends on the city, the load and who arranges it.
- **Artwork changes and proofs.** A new design later means new plates.
- **Samples.** If you need one, ask what it costs and how much time it adds.

## How to read a quote against the estimate

When a quote arrives, line it up with the estimate for your design:

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Inside size and style** | The same as your design, not "similar" |
| **Ply and flute** | 5-ply B + C, or whatever you chose |
| **Paper spec** | GSM and BF per layer, as in [reading a box spec](/blogs/reading-box-spec-ply-bf-gsm) |
| **Print** | Number of colours and faces, not just "printed" |
| **One-time charges** | Plates and die, shown separately or included |
| **Quantity** | Per box at your quantity, and the order total |
| **Price against the estimate** | Within the range, or an explanation if it is not |

A price outside the range is not necessarily wrong. The estimator uses typical boards and starting rates, and a real quote reflects the paper actually used. But you are entitled to ask why, and a good supplier can tell you.

## Three ways to bring the price down without breaking the box

1. **Shrink the box to fit.** A smaller blank is cheaper and ships lighter.
2. **Print fewer colours.** One-colour print on kraft keeps cost low and looks clean.
3. **Choose the lightest board that does the job.** Test it first. The [GSM guide](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength) explains how to compare boards.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the price of a 5 ply box per piece?

There is no single price. It depends on the size, print, quantity and style. As an illustration, the designer estimates roughly ₹47 to ₹63 per box for a 40 x 30 x 25 cm kraft carton with one-colour print at 500 boxes, before GST and delivery. Your quote confirms the real price.

### Why is a 5-ply box dearer than 3-ply?

It has more paper: three liners and two fluted layers instead of two liners and one. In the estimator, the same box in 5-ply comes to about two-thirds more than in 3-ply. It is also much stronger, so for heavier goods the extra cost is often cheaper than damage.

### Does the estimate include GST and delivery?

No. The designer shows the figure before GST and before delivery, and so do the examples here. Ask how your quote shows both.

### Can I get a price for 100 boxes?

The designer shows a 100-box slab so that you can see how short runs behave, but our current minimum order is 500 boxes. Ask us if your quantity is lower.

### Why does my quote differ from the estimate?

The estimate uses typical boards and starting rates and gives a range of plus or minus 15 per cent. A quote reflects your exact papers, print, quantity and the day's paper costs, so it can sit inside or outside the range.
