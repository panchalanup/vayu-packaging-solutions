# How to Add Cutouts to Your Box Design: The Complete Guide to Stickers, Patterns and Brand Colours

You have a box style, a size and a board. You open the artwork tab and meet a blank carton. You do not have a designer on call, your logo is a small file, and you still want to know one thing: **will this box look like it belongs to my brand?**

That is the job of **cutouts**. A cutout is a ready-made cut-out graphic (a mango, a gift, a star, a "Handmade" badge, a fragile mark, a leaf pattern) that you drop on any face of the 3D box. You can make it bigger or smaller, turn it, recolour it to your brand colour, repeat it across a face as a pattern, and see the result on the folded box straight away. No drawing skills, no design software, nothing to install.

The designer ships with **512 cutouts** in 17 groups, and you can use up to 80 on one design. This guide shows how to find the right ones, place them, recolour them, build patterns, avoid the usual mistakes and send the result to a printer.

## When cutouts help (and when your logo is enough)

Use cutouts when:

- **You have no artwork yet** and want a box that looks finished for a pitch, a shop-owner meeting or a first quote.
- **Your product tells the story**: fruit boxes with fruit, bakery boxes with a wheat ear or a cupcake, clothing boxes with a hanger or a shirt.
- **You want a quiet repeating pattern** on the sides of a plain box so it does not look unfinished, while the logo stays on the front.
- **You need handling marks** (this way up, fragile, keep dry) placed where a warehouse worker will see them.

If you already have a proper logo and printer-ready artwork, keep cutouts to a minimum and use the designer for size, fold position and print checks, as in our [logo and artwork guide](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes). Cutouts are a design aid, not a replacement for your brand.

Two things are worth knowing. The badges use **plain generic words only**, with no "organic", "certified" or similar claims, because a claim you have not earned on a food box is a legal problem, not a design choice. If you hold a certification mark, upload it as an image from your certifier's file. And there are **no third-party brand logos**; the contact icons are generic symbols.

## Step 1: Open the Cutouts section

1. Open the [3D Box Designer](/box-designer) and choose a style and size (see [how to use the designer](/blogs/how-to-use-3d-box-designer) if this is your first visit).
2. Select a face of the box. The face you select is where new cutouts land. If nothing is selected, the first printable face is used.
3. Open the **Artwork** tab and find **Cutouts**, next to image and text.

You will see a counter ("12/80 used"), two helper buttons (Surprise me and Match brand colour), a search box, group chips and a grid.

**Tip:** the designer adds a **Recommended for this box** row at the top. It looks at your box style and, if you started from a template, the template's category, so a fruit carton suggests fruit and a cake box suggests bakery items. Start there.

---

## Step 2: Find the right cutout

You have three ways to find one:

1. **Recommended row.** Fastest. One tap adds it.
2. **Group chips.** Scroll sideways and pick Fruits and veg, Gifts and festive, Shipping marks and so on.
3. **Search.** Type a word such as mango, gift, star, hanger or even a local word. The search also looks at alternative names, so "aam" finds the mango.

Tap a thumbnail once and the cutout appears on the selected face, ready to move. If it is not what you wanted, press Undo; every change in the designer can be undone.

---

## Step 3: Place, resize and turn it

A cutout behaves like any element on the box face:

- **Move** by dragging on the 3D box or the flat face view.
- **Resize** with the size slider or the handles. Scale is a share of the face, so the cutout keeps its look when you change the box size.
- **Rotate** with the handle. A small tilt (5 to 10 degrees) looks playful; a straight cutout looks formal.
- **Opacity** fades it. Use 15 to 30 percent for a quiet background motif behind the logo.
- **Layer order** brings a cutout forward or back (the `[` and `]` keys do the same). **Lock** and **Flip** are there too.
- **Duplicate** makes a copy (or press Ctrl+D). Arrow keys nudge the selected cutout, and Delete removes it.

![A sticker selected on the front face with the size, opacity, colour and pattern controls open beside it](/blog-images/designer/designer-cutout-controls.png)
*Select a cutout to see its controls: size, opacity, colour, pattern, flip, layer order, duplicate and delete.*

**A simple rule for balance:** one large hero cutout (about half the face width), two or three small ones around it, and the logo or brand name where the eye should land first. If everything is the same size, nothing is the star.

## Step 4: Recolour a cutout to your brand colour

Cutouts come in their original colours, but any of them can be **tinted to one colour**. This is the feature that turns a generic sticker into something that looks like yours.

1. Select the cutout and open its **Colour** control.
2. Pick **Original** to go back, one of the swatches, or the colour picker for any colour. If you saved a brand kit, your brand colours are in the swatches.
3. The whole cutout becomes that colour, keeping its shape and details as a one-colour print.

One-colour cutouts are also the kindest to print cost; our [price guide](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide) explains how print colours move the estimate.

### Match brand colour: one tap for all of them

If you have placed many cutouts and want them all in the same colour, use **Match brand colour** at the top of the Cutouts section. It recolours every cutout on the design to the colour you choose, and **Original colours** restores them. It is the quickest way to compare, say, green, navy and maroon on the same layout.

![The Match brand colour swatches open above a box whose stickers all use the same green](/blog-images/designer/designer-cutouts-brand-colour.png)
*Match brand colour: choose a swatch and every cutout on the box changes together.*

**Contrast check:** a dark green cutout on dark kraft nearly disappears. Pick tints that stand out from the board colour.

## Step 5: Turn one cutout into a pattern

A repeating pattern makes plain sides look intentional, and it is the cheapest way to make a box look designed.

1. Place one cutout (a leaf, a dot, a star) on a face.
2. Switch on **Repeat as a pattern**. The cutout is tiled across the whole face.
3. Adjust **Spacing** (how far apart the tiles are), **Angle** (straight rows or a diagonal), **Shift across** and **Shift down** (where the pattern starts), and **Offset every other row** for a brick or staggered look.
4. Lower the opacity to 15 to 30 percent so the pattern sits behind the main artwork.
5. Use **Apply to all surfaces** if you want the same pattern on every face.

The library has dedicated tile patterns, but any cutout can be a tile. A small mango repeated across the long sides of a fruit carton works well.

## Step 6: Let "Surprise me" suggest a layout

Stuck? **Surprise me** builds a complete layout for the box you are on: a hero cutout, a few small ones around it, quiet patterns on the other faces, and a matching board colour from a set of curated palettes (natural kraft, fresh produce, bakery, fashion, festive, soft pastel and others).

Not keen? Press **Undo**, or tap again for a different arrangement. Treat it as a first draft: keep what you like, delete the rest, then add your logo and text. The palettes are original combinations and use no brand's colours.

## Step 7: Check the fold, then send it to print

Before you finish, run through this short list:

1. **Fold the box.** Drag the assembly slider from flat to sealed. A cutout that looks centred on a flat face can end up across a fold or under a flap. The print checks warn you when artwork crosses a fold.
2. **Check the dieline.** Cutouts are included in the flat dieline view and the PDF, so your printer sees the same placement. Read [dielines explained](/blogs/dielines-explained) for how to hand it over.
3. **Take product shots.** Studio backgrounds and the 360 spin include your cutouts ([product shots guide](/blogs/packaging-product-shots-360-video)).
4. **Share the link.** A share link stores each cutout's id, place, size, colour and pattern, so whoever opens it sees your full design.
5. **Ask the printer about detail.** Printing on corrugated is coarser than on paper. Very fine lines and tiny cutouts may not reproduce, especially on kraft, so ask for the minimum line weight and number of colours.

## Five tips for better cutout designs

1. **Fewer, bigger, clearer.** Three well-placed cutouts beat twelve small ones.
2. **One colour family.** Tint everything to two or three brand colours with Match brand colour.
3. **Keep clear board around the logo.** About a thumb's width of empty space looks premium.
4. **Patterns on the sides, message on the front.**
5. **Put handling marks on two sides**, so a warehouse worker sees one whichever way the box sits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are cutouts really free to use?
Yes. The designer has no sign-up and no locked features. The cutout library is part of it.

### Can I use cutouts on a real print order?
Yes, as part of your artwork. The cutouts are original or openly licensed graphics, and credits are listed on the [image credits page](/image-credits). Your own logo goes in as an image on the Artwork tab (see the [artwork guide](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes)).

### How many cutouts can I add?
Up to 80 on one design. A pattern fill counts as one cutout however many times it repeats.

### Do cutouts change the price?
The designer's estimate mainly follows size, board, quantity, print colours and finish. More print colours generally mean a higher price, so tinting everything to one or two colours is a sensible way to keep the cost down. The estimate stays indicative; the quote confirms it.
