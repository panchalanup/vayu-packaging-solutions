# E-commerce Mailer Boxes: Design an Unboxing Your Customers Remember

For an online brand, the box is the first physical thing a customer touches. They have seen your product on a screen; now it arrives on their doorstep, and the next thirty seconds decide whether it feels like a gift or like a parcel.

A good mailer box does three jobs at once: it protects the product in transit, it is cheap enough to send with every order, and it makes opening the parcel feel like part of the product. This guide walks you through designing one in the free **3D Box Designer**, from size to print to a preview you can approve before ordering.

## The problem: plain cartons, oversized boxes, forgettable unboxing

Many small online sellers start with whatever cartons are in stock. That works, until:

- **Products rattle** because the box is too big, and arrive scuffed or broken.
- **Courier charges creep up**, because many couriers price by volumetric weight, so an oversized box costs more to ship even when the product is light.
- **Customers do not remember you.** A brown carton with a courier label looks like every other parcel that day.
- **Unboxing videos look flat.** If your customers post about you, the box is in the first frame.

A custom mailer fixes most of this. It is sized to your product, folds from one piece into a sturdy box with a hinged, tuck-in lid, and gives you two surfaces to brand: outside and inside.

---

## When a mailer box is the right choice

Mailers (often referred to by the FEFCO style 0427) suit:

- **Apparel, accessories, cosmetics, books, small electronics and gift sets.**
- **Subscription boxes**, where opening the box is part of the experience.
- **Products under a few kilograms** that ship individually rather than in bulk.

They are less suited to heavy items or long, thin products. For those, a regular slotted carton (RSC) in 5-ply may be the better choice; see our [shipping carton preset](/box-designer/shipping-carton).

## Step by step: designing your mailer

### 1. Start from the mailer preset

Open the [mailer box designer](/box-designer/mailer-box). It loads the mailer style with sensible starting dimensions. You can also reach it from the main designer through the style picker or the "E-commerce shipper" template.

### 2. Size it from your product, not from a guess

Measure your product (or the product in its inner packaging) and use the **fit checker**: enter length, width and height, add padding (a few millimetres for snug items, more if you use tissue or crinkle paper) and tick "fragile" if relevant. The tool suggests an inside size; apply it with one tap.

Two tips:

- **Measure the product as it will be packed**, including any pouch, tissue wrap or insert card.
- **Keep the box as small as the product allows.** Every centimetre you add costs board and may raise volumetric shipping charges.

If you are not sure how to measure, see [how to measure corrugated boxes](/blogs/box-measurements-guide).

### 3. Choose the board

Most e-commerce mailers are 3-ply, often in E or B flute for a crisp fold and good print surface. Heavier or more fragile products may need 5-ply. The designer's strength guidance gives an indicative verdict for the size and ply you have chosen.

Pick a board colour: natural kraft feels honest and eco-minded; white looks clean and makes colours pop; a coloured board makes a bold brand statement.

### 4. Brand the outside

Add your logo to the lid. Keep it simple: one logo, maybe a short line such as your website or a tagline. On the sides, consider a small "Handle with care" or "Open here" for practical value.

### 5. Print the inside

This is where mailers shine. Set an **inside colour** (for example a white-lined inside on a kraft outside, or your brand colour inside) and place a message on the inside of the lid: "Thank you for shopping with us", a care note, a QR code to your reorder page or a social handle.

Use the **fold animation** to open and close the lid and see exactly what the customer sees first.

### 6. Run the print checks

The print-ready checks flag common problems: a low-resolution logo, artwork sitting too close to a fold or over an edge, or text that will be too small to read. Click a warning to jump to the face that needs fixing.

### 7. Take a product shot and share it

Before you order, export a product shot (studio, wood or brand-colour background) or a short turntable video, and show it to your team. It is also a ready-made image for a "new packaging" post.

## Design ideas that make unboxing memorable

- **Inside print beats outside print for delight.** The outside travels through sorting hubs and gets scuffed; the inside is seen once, by your customer, at the best moment.
- **One strong colour is enough.** A single brand colour inside a kraft box looks deliberate and keeps print costs reasonable.
- **Say thank you.** A short message on the inside lid costs nothing extra once you are printing.
- **Make it reusable.** Mailers are sturdy enough for customers to keep for storage or returns. A clean design encourages that.
- **Think about the courier label.** Leave a plain area on the lid or base where the label can go without covering your logo.
- **Avoid tiny text.** Anything smaller than a few millimetres high may not print cleanly on corrugated board. The print checks warn you.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Designing around the product without packing material.** Add tissue, inserts and a little air.
2. **Choosing 5-ply "to be safe" for light products.** It adds cost and weight without much benefit for a T-shirt.
3. **Full-colour print everywhere on a first order.** Start with one or two colours; upgrade once the design is proven.
4. **Ignoring the inside.** It is the cheapest place to delight your customer.
5. **Not checking the lid with the product in place.** A thick product can stop a tuck lid from closing flat.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a mailer box?

A mailer is a one-piece die-cut corrugated box with a hinged lid that tucks into the front, and double side walls for strength. It assembles without tape and is popular for e-commerce and subscription boxes.

### Is 3-ply strong enough for a mailer?

For many light and medium products, yes. Heavier or fragile items may need 5-ply. The designer's strength guidance gives an indicative verdict for your size and board; we can confirm it for your product.

### Can I print the inside of the box?

Yes. In the designer you can set an inside colour and place artwork on the inside faces, including the inside of the lid. Inside print is usually quoted separately from outside print.

### Will the colours on screen match the printed box?

Screen colours are approximate. Corrugated board, especially kraft, changes how inks look. Confirm brand colours on a physical proof before a large run.

### How do I keep shipping costs down?

Keep the box as small as the product and packing allow, and choose the lightest board that protects the product. Many couriers charge by volumetric weight, so excess space costs money.

### Do I need to sign up to save my design?

No. Designs save automatically in your browser. Use a share link or the spec sheet PDF to keep a copy elsewhere.

### What is the minimum order for printed mailers?

It depends on the print method and design. Send your design through the enquiry form and we will tell you the options for your quantity.
