# Takeaway Boxes for Cloud Kitchens: Clamshell, Pizza, Carry

A cloud kitchen has no dining room, no waiter and no table. The box is the restaurant. It is the first impression, the plate, the heat shield and the brand, all for a customer who may never see your kitchen. It also has to survive a scooter ride, an insulated bag and a lift.

Many kitchens start with one stock box for everything and discover the problems fast: burgers squashed in a box that is too deep, pizza slices sliding in one that is too big, a meal combo that will not fit the bag. This guide helps cloud kitchens, tiffin and meal-subscription services, cafes and street-food brands choose the right shape, size it to the menu and the delivery bag, and design it in the free **3D Box Designer**.

## A careful note on food and heat

Takeaway food is hot, steamy and often oily. Packaging that touches food needs care, and this article is **general guidance, not food-safety advice**.

- **Plain corrugated board is not a grease barrier.** Oily or saucy food usually needs a suitable lining, coating, greaseproof paper or a separate tray inside.
- **Steam softens board.** A box that traps steam can go limp and soggy. Allow some air release for crisp food, and avoid leaving hot food sealed for long.
- **Say what you serve** when you ask for a quote, and whether it touches the board.
- **Print and inks:** many kitchens keep print on the outside and the food-contact surface plain.
- **Food rules apply.** Confirm the requirements for food-contact packaging with your food-safety adviser, and ask your supplier what documentation they can provide.
- **The designer plans size, style and print. It does not certify any board, ink or film as food safe.**

## Size from the menu, then check the bag

Cloud kitchens often choose a box before measuring the food. Do it the other way:

1. **List the menu items** and group them by shape: round and tall (burgers), long (rolls), flat (pizza, flatbread), deep (rice and gravy bowls), wide (combos).
2. **Measure the food in its wrapper.** A burger in paper is bigger than a burger on a plate. Add clearance for steam and for the lid to close without squashing.
3. **Measure your delivery bag.** The inside of the bag decides your maximum box size. Note how many boxes must stand side by side or stack.
4. **Pick two or three sizes,** not ten. Fewer sizes means simpler stock and cheaper orders.

## Step by step: a takeaway clamshell

1. Open the [takeaway box designer](/box-designer/takeaway-box). It loads a 20 x 18 x 8 cm clamshell in natural kraft (3-ply E-flute). Or open the **Burger takeaway box** template from **Templates**, in the **Food & bakery** category.
2. In **Edit Box**, enter the inside length, width and height from your wrapped food. The unit switch lets you use inches or cm.
3. Move the **fold slider** from flat to open lid to closed. See how the hinged lid and the front tuck work.
4. Choose the board: 3-ply E-flute is thin and prints well. Kraft looks natural; white looks clean.
5. In **Customize**, add your kitchen name and logo, then food graphics from the **Cutouts** panel (burger, pizza, rice bowl, noodles, flatbread, bento box, fries and more). Use **Match brand colour** to tint graphics in your colour.
6. If you want a window, switch **Window** on in **Cut-outs** and size it. It suits salads and desserts more than hot, oily food.
7. In **Price & order**, compare 500, 1,000 and 5,000 boxes. In **Actions**, download the dieline and share the link with your team.

## Step by step: a pizza box

A pizza box is a shallow, wide die-cut box, so the right size is the one that matches your pizza.

1. Open the [pizza box designer](/box-designer/pizza-box), or the **Pizza box** template (31 x 31 x 5 cm, kraft, white inside; a snug fit for a 12-inch pizza, so add a little if your pizzas run wide).
2. Enter the inside length and width to suit the pizza with a small margin. Keep the height low: a tall box lets slices slide and traps steam.
3. Put your brand on the lid. Large lids work well: logo, a short tagline and a phone number or QR.
4. Check the fold: the lid opens and closes like a mailer.
5. Check price at your usual order sizes and send the design.

## Combos and multi-meal orders: tray and sleeve, or a carry box

**Tray and sleeve.** Use the **slide box** for a meal combo, a dessert set or a premium order. The sleeve carries the print; the tray holds the food. It adds a little reveal for a modest cost, but two parts need packing, so it suits slower orders more than a rush.

**Carry box.** For office tiffins, catering or a family order, a **carry box with hand holes** lets one person carry several meals. Hand holes are cut-outs, so they weaken the walls and let heat and steam escape. Keep them modest, choose a stronger board for heavy loads and test a sample. General packaging literature reports that holes and vents can cut stacking strength by roughly 10-40%, and our [ventilated carton guide](/blogs/fruit-vegetable-ventilated-boxes) explains the trade-off. Switch the hand holes on and off in the **Cut-outs** section and watch the model.

## Branding for delivery

- **The lid is your billboard.** On a delivery order, the lid is the first thing the customer sees. Put the name large and keep other text short.
- **One colour on kraft** is cheap and reads as honest and natural.
- **Print the inside lid** with a thank-you or a menu code, which costs little and surprises the customer.
- **Use graphics sparingly.** One food graphic and one badge ("Fresh", "Hot", "Handmade") beats a crowded lid.
- **Keep text off the folds and the window edges.** The print checks warn you.
- **Do not print claims** you cannot back up. The cutout library has none.

## Practical tips for cloud kitchens

- **Test the real order.** Pack a real meal, put it in your delivery bag, and take a 20-minute ride.
- **Test the hot one.** Check what steam and oil do to the board over 20 minutes.
- **Order small first.** A sample run tells you more than any drawing.
- **Standardise sizes** across brands run from the same kitchen where you can.
- **Keep a spec.** Save the spec sheet so reorders match.
- **Think about the stack.** Boxes stack in the bag; a lid with a tuck helps them stay closed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which box is best for burgers and sandwiches?

A takeaway clamshell: a hinged lid and a front tuck, no tape or glue. Size from the wrapped food.

### What size pizza box do I need?

One that matches your pizza with a small margin. The pizza preset is 31 x 31 x 5 cm, a snug fit for a 12-inch pizza; set the size for each menu size and test one box.

### Can hot or oily food go straight into a corrugated box?

Plain corrugated board is not a grease barrier. Oily food usually needs a suitable liner, coating or tray. Tell us what you serve, and we will discuss options.

### Can you make a tiffin box?

The designer does not model reusable steel or plastic tiffin carriers. It can design the paper packaging around a tiffin-style service: meal clamshells, tray-and-sleeve combos and carry boxes.

### Can I add a window to a takeaway box?

Yes, on the clamshell. Switch the window on in Cut-outs and size it. The film is a separate material from the board.
