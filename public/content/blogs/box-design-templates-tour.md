# Start from a Template: A Tour of 74 Ready Box Designs for Fruit, Food, Fashion, Gifts and More

The hardest moment in any box project is the empty screen. You know you need "a box for mangoes" or "something for our candles", but you do not yet know the style, the size, the board, where the artwork goes or how it should look. Every choice is open, so nothing gets decided.

A **template** removes that moment. It is a complete, sensible starting box for a common job: style, size, board, colour, finish, cut-outs such as vents or a window, a small set of cutout stickers and sample text, all set up so the box looks right the second it opens. The 3D Box Designer has **74 templates in 13 categories**. You pick one, the box folds into shape, and you change what is yours.

This guide shows how to open a template, what each one contains, what to change first, and walks through the categories so you can find yours in under a minute.

## What is inside a template

Opening a template sets all of this at once:

| Part | What you get | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Box style** | One of the 20 styles, chosen for the product (a ventilated carton for mangoes, a window cake box for cakes) | The style decides how the box opens and how much board it needs |
| **Inside size** | A realistic length, width and height in cm | A first guess you then correct with your product's own size |
| **Board** | 3-ply, 5-ply or 7-ply with a suitable flute | Light retail packs get 3-ply; heavy fruit cartons get 5-ply |
| **Colour and finish** | Board colour, inside colour, matte or gloss | Sets the mood in one step |
| **Cut-outs** | Vents, hand holes or a window where the product needs them | Shown on the 3D model; the dieline has the true cut lines |
| **Cutouts and text** | A small sticker layout and sample text such as "Your Brand" | Placeholders you replace with your own |

Everything stays editable. A template is a **starting point, not a locked design**.

Two details are worth knowing. Applying a template **keeps your unit** (mm, cm or inches) and your **quantity**, so you do not lose work you already did on the estimate. And the sample text and cutouts are explicit placeholders: the designer reminds you to replace them in the Artwork tab. No template uses a third-party brand, a certificate or a claim such as "organic".

## How to open a template

There are two doors in.

1. **Quick start.** At the top of the Edit tab, **Templates** ("Ready-made boxes") opens the gallery, next to **Find my box**. Under them, a **Quick picks** row offers a few templates that suit the style you are on, such as the mango carton, T-shirt mailer or cake box with window.
2. **A style landing page.** Pages such as [fruit boxes](/box-designer/fruit-box) or [cake boxes](/box-designer/cake-box) open the designer with a matching starting design.

In the gallery you have three ways to narrow 74 down to a handful:

- **Category tabs** along the top: Fruit and produce, Food and bakery, Clothing and fashion and so on.
- **Search** for what you are packing. Try mango, cake, saree, gift or candle; the search also reads extra words people use for the product.
- **Box style filter**, a drop-down that lists only the templates built on one style, handy if you already know you want a mailer.

## Your first five minutes with a template

Do these in order and you will have a design worth showing:

1. **Replace the sample text** with your brand name. Text sizes are set from the face width, so a short name will fit; check long names.
2. **Check the size against your product.** Open the [fit checker](/blogs/find-right-box-size), enter your product's real size and let it set the inside dimensions.
3. **Change the colour.** Keep the layout, try your brand colour on the board. [Match brand colour](/blogs/how-to-add-cutouts-box-design) recolours every cutout in one tap.
4. **Swap or delete cutouts.** Keep the hero cutout if it fits your product; remove the rest if your own logo is stronger.
5. **Fold the box.** Run the assembly slider from flat to sealed, then look at the [estimate](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide).

That is the whole job for most buyers. Save it, share the link, or send it for a quote.

## A tour of the 13 categories

Sizes below are the inside sizes the templates start with (length x width x height in cm). Treat them as typical starting points, not standards.

### Fruit and produce (10 templates)

Fruit boxes are the most distinctive group because they use **ventilated** styles: vent slots let air move through the carton, and hand holes make a full carton easy to lift.

- **Mango carton:** 36 x 26 x 14, 5-ply, vented. The classic single-layer carton.
- **Apple tray**, **berry tray** and **vegetable crate:** open produce trays in 3-ply or 5-ply.
- **Grape box**, **banana carton**, **citrus carton** and **tropical fruit box:** ventilated cartons sized for each fruit.
- **Dry fruits gift box:** a slide box, since dry fruits are bought as gifts, not shipped in bulk.
- **Farm basket carton:** a carry box with hand holes.

Vents and hand holes cost some strength, so read [vents, hand holes and windows](/blogs/vents-hand-holes-windows-box-design) before you finalise a fruit carton.

### Food and bakery (12 templates)

Cake box with window, cupcake box, sweet box (mithai), cookie box, burger takeaway box, honey jar box, spice box, tea or coffee box, bottle box for oil and sauce, pizza box, bakery tray and a plain bottle carton. Notable ones:

- **Cake box with window:** 25 x 25 x 12, with a film window so the cake is visible.
- **Cookie box** and **burger takeaway box:** the takeaway clamshell style, a hinged lid with a front tuck.
- **Honey jar box** (10 x 10 x 13) and **bottle box** (9 x 9 x 30): crash-lock bottoms that hold the weight of glass.
- **Sweet box (mithai):** a telescope box, shallow and wide.

For direct food contact, material and board grade matter; the designer previews shape and print, not material certification. Our [pizza and bakery guide](/blogs/pizza-bakery-box-design) covers that.

## How to choose the right template

Match on **what the product needs**, not what it is called:

- **Does it need air?** Fresh produce, flowers, anything that sweats: a ventilated template.
- **Must it be seen?** Cakes, toys, earbuds: a window template.
- **Is it carried by hand?** Hampers, plants, hardware: a carry box with hand holes.
- **Is it heavy or fragile?** Pick a shipping template, then raise the ply.
- **Is it a gift?** Telescope, slide or book-style templates feel more like a present than a shipper.

If two templates fit, open both, fold them, and compare estimates. The style filter makes this quick.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I change anything in a template?
Everything: size, board, colour, finish, cutouts, text and even the box style. Changing the style keeps your sizes sensible and drops cut-outs the new style cannot have.

### Do templates use real brands or certifications?
No. All sample art is original or from the cutout library, and no template claims an organic, certified or similar status.

### Are the template sizes standard sizes?
No. They are typical starting points. Always enter your own product size; the [measurement guide](/blogs/box-measurements-guide) explains how to measure.

### Will a template give me the final price?
It gives an indicative estimate that changes with your edits. The quote confirms the price.

### Can I save and share a template-based design?
Yes. Share links, saved designs and My designs all keep your edits.

### My product is not in the list.
Start with the closest template and edit it, or answer "Find my box" for a recommendation. If the style you need is missing, tell us; we may still be able to make it.
