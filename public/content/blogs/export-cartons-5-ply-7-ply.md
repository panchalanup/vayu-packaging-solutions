# Export Cartons: Choosing 5-Ply or 7-Ply for Long Journeys

A carton that is fine for a local delivery can fail badly on an export journey. Between your factory and the buyer's warehouse, it may be stacked under other cartons for weeks, handled many times, carried through humid ports and moved by forklifts that do not care what is inside.

This guide covers how to plan export and heavy-duty cartons, when 5-ply or 7-ply board makes sense, how to size cartons for pallets and containers, and how to design and check them in the free **3D Box Designer** before you place an order.

## The problem: long journeys find every weakness

Common export carton failures include:

- **Crushed bottom layers**, because the carton was chosen for the product weight but not for the weight of everything stacked above it.
- **Soft, weakened board** after time in humid conditions.
- **Bulging sides** when the product does not support the walls, or the carton is overfilled.
- **Wasted space** in pallets and containers, because carton sizes were chosen without thinking about the pallet footprint.
- **Marking problems**: missing handling symbols, unreadable labels or no room for shipping marks.

Each of these is cheaper to fix on screen than at the destination.

## 5-ply or 7-ply: a practical guide

| Board | Construction | Typical uses (general guidance) |
|---|---|---|
| 3-ply | Single wall | Light goods, local delivery, inner cartons |
| 5-ply | Double wall | Heavier goods, longer journeys, stacked pallets |
| 7-ply | Triple wall | Very heavy or bulky goods, tall stacks, demanding export routes |

The right board depends on more than the weight of one carton. Think about:

- **Stack height**: how many cartons sit on top of the bottom one, in the warehouse and in the container.
- **Journey length and climate**: longer, more humid journeys weaken board.
- **Whether the product supports the load**: rigid products (machinery parts, tins) share the load; soft or hollow products (garments, empty containers) do not.
- **Handling**: how many times the carton is picked up, and whether it is moved by hand or by forklift.

For the underlying theory, see [single, double and triple wall board](/blogs/corrugated-wall-differences) and [burst strength vs edge crush test](/blogs/burst-strength-ect-guide).

## Step by step: designing an export carton

### 1. Start from the shipping carton preset

Open the [shipping carton designer](/box-designer/shipping-carton). It loads a regular slotted container (RSC), the most common and economical export carton. If your product needs a stronger base, look at the **full overlap (FOL)** style in the style picker, where the outer flaps overlap fully for a double layer top and bottom.

### 2. Size from the packed product

Measure the product with any inner packaging, inserts or dividers. Use the **fit checker** with a padding allowance, and tick "fragile" if relevant. Switch to millimetres or inches to match your buyer's specification.

### 3. Check the pallet footprint

Before finalising the size, think about how cartons will sit on a pallet. A carton size that divides neatly into the pallet length and width wastes less space and avoids overhang, which weakens the stack. Use the **stacked / pallet view** to see a stack of your cartons and judge the fit.

![A pallet of identical cartons shown in the designer's stacked view](/blog-images/designer/designer-pallet-view.png)
*The stacked / pallet view: a quick way to see whether your carton size makes a tidy, stable pallet.*

Pallet and container sizes vary by country, buyer and forwarder, so confirm the pallet size your buyer or freight forwarder uses before you lock the carton size.

### 4. Choose 5-ply or 7-ply and read the strength guidance

Set the ply and look at the **strength guidance** in the **Price & order** tab. It estimates, from the board's caliper and the box size, roughly how much load the carton can carry and how high it can be stacked, with a safety margin, in plain language. Compare 5-ply and 7-ply for your size.

*Strength guidance: an indicative estimate of load and stack height. For critical shipments, confirm with physical testing.*

This is an estimate, not a lab test. Humidity, time in storage, handling and the product itself all change real-world strength. For high-value or critical shipments, ask for test reports or a physical sample test.

### 5. Add the marks you need

Use the artwork editor to place:

- your company name or logo,
- a panel for shipping marks (consignee, port, carton number),
- handling symbols such as "this way up", "fragile" or "keep dry",
- product codes, gross and net weight fields.

Keep marks clear and large. The print checks warn you about text that is too small.

### 6. Export the dieline and spec sheet

Download the **dieline** (a 1:1 cut-and-crease drawing) and the **spec sheet PDF**. The spec sheet shows views, dimensions, board, finish and quantity, which makes it easy for your buyer, your forwarder and us to agree on the same carton.

### 7. Get a price and send the design

Check the indicative estimate, then send the design to us for a firm quote. Mention the route, the stack height and any buyer packaging requirements in the note.

## Moisture, climate and long journeys

Corrugated board is made from paper, and paper absorbs moisture. On long or humid journeys:

- **Board loses strength** as it absorbs moisture, so a carton that is strong enough when dry may not be strong enough at the destination.
- **Store cartons dry** before packing: off the floor, away from walls and covered.
- **Ask about moisture-resistant options** if your route is especially humid.
- **Use desiccants or liners** inside the carton where your product needs them.

## Pallets, strapping and stretch film

Cartons rarely travel alone. A stable pallet load protects them as much as the board does:

- **Stack cartons squarely**, corner on corner, so the walls carry the load.
- **Avoid overhang** at the pallet edge.
- **Strap and wrap** the load with [PP strapping](/products/pp-strapping) and [stretch film](/products/stretch-film).
- **Wooden pallets for export** may need treatment and marking under international phytosanitary rules (often referred to as ISPM 15). Your freight forwarder can tell you what the destination requires.

## Tips for exporters

- **Write down the stack height** in the warehouse and the container before choosing board.
- **Ask your buyer for their packaging specification.** Many importers have rules on carton size, weight, marking and labelling.
- **Keep carton weight manageable.** Very heavy cartons are hard to handle by hand and tend to be dropped.
- **Use dividers for multiple items** so products do not rub or knock each other.
- **Test before the big order.** A small trial shipment or a drop and stack test is cheap insurance.
- **Keep a spec sheet on file** for every carton, so a repeat order is exactly the same.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need 7-ply for export?

Not always. Many export shipments use 5-ply. 7-ply is for very heavy or bulky goods, tall stacks or demanding routes. Compare both in the designer's strength guidance, then confirm with us.

### What is the difference between RSC and FOL cartons?

In an RSC, the outer flaps meet in the middle. In a full overlap (FOL) carton, the outer flaps overlap across the full width, giving a double layer of board on the top and bottom.

### How accurate is the strength estimate?

It is an indicative estimate from board caliper and box size, with a safety margin. Real strength depends on humidity, storage time, handling and the product. For critical shipments, ask for test reports or test a sample.

### What pallet size should I design for?

It depends on your buyer, the destination and your forwarder. Confirm the pallet size first, then check your carton size in the stacked view.

### Can I get the dieline for my own printer or die maker?

Yes. Export the 1:1 dieline. Your die maker may adjust allowances for the board and machines used.
