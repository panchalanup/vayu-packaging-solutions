# Packing Pickle, Ghee and Spice Jars Without Leaks

Keep a leak inside the parcel by sealing the jar, putting each jar in its own sealed bag, wrapping it, and holding it upright in a carton with 2.5 cm of cushioning on every side. Two 500 g pickle jars about 8.5 cm across and 11.5 cm tall need a carton of about 25 x 14 x 17 cm inside.

## Why jars leak in transit

A jar rarely leaks because it is dropped from a height. It leaks for three plainer reasons:

- **The closure works loose.** Vibration and temperature changes loosen a cap, and oil creeps along the thread.
- **Pressure and heat.** Ghee and oil soften and expand in warm weather. A jar filled to the brim has nowhere to go.
- **Knocks and stacking.** Jars touch each other in the carton, a wall flexes, and a lid or a thin glass wall gives.

A leak also soaks the carton, which weakens the board and then ruins the parcels around it. So the plan is two layers of defence: **keep the liquid in** (seal, bag) and **keep the jar still** (cushion, partition, carton).

---

## The leak-proof inner pack, in 8 steps

1. **Check the jar and its cap.** Use a closure with a liner or inner seal that suits the product, and ask your jar supplier what they recommend. Plastic jars are light and do not break, glass breaks. Check that whatever you use is suitable for food.
2. **Leave headspace.** Do not fill to the brim. Oil, ghee and pickle brine expand with heat, so leave a little air under the cap.
3. **Wipe the thread** clean and dry before closing. Oil on the thread is how leaks begin.
4. **Seal the cap.** Use a neck band, a shrink sleeve or a strip of tape over the cap, whichever you can apply reliably.
5. **Bag each jar on its own.** Put the jar in a sealed plastic bag, with a sheet of absorbent paper if you like. A leak stays inside the bag.
6. **Wrap the bagged jar** in bubble wrap, at least twice round, so the jars never touch.
7. **Pack upright** in a carton, with the jar's cap facing up and a "this side up" mark outside.
8. **Test one pack.** Pack a jar as you will ship it, turn it upside down for a day, then check the bag, the wrap and the carton. This is an informal test, not a certified one.

If you ship food, also check your courier's own rules on liquids, oils and food, which we do not state here.

## How the carton is sized

The designer's fit checker uses 2.5 cm of padding on each side for fragile goods. For jars in a grid, this becomes a simple rule: **2.5 cm around the group and 2.5 cm between neighbouring jars**, plus 0.3 cm of ease, then round up to 0.5 cm.

For *n* jars in a row, each jar *d* cm across: length = n x d + (n + 1) x 2.5 + 0.3.

Worked example, 6 pickle jars (2 x 3) with an assumed diameter of 8.5 cm and height 11.5 cm.

- Length: 3 x 8.5 + 4 x 2.5 + 0.3 = 35.8, rounded up to **36 cm**.
- Width: 2 x 8.5 + 3 x 2.5 + 0.3 = 24.8, rounded up to **25 cm**.
- Height: 11.5 + 5 + 0.3 = 16.8, rounded up to **17 cm**.
- Inside size: **36 x 25 x 17 cm**, which is 15,300 cm3.

The designer models the carton, not the dividers, so mention the partitions or the foam when you ask for a quote.

## Partitions and inserts

Cushioning between jars is what stops glass hitting glass. There are three common ways:

| Method | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bubble wrap on each jar | Each jar is wrapped on its own | Simple and cheap. See our [bubble wrap](/products/bubble-wrap) page. |
| Corrugated partition (cell divider) | A grid of cells holds each jar upright | Keeps jars still and apart. A quote item, because the designer models the carton only. |
| Moulded foam or pulp tray | A tray with a pocket per jar | Good for a fixed jar size and larger runs |

A partition sized to your jar is the strongest answer for multi-jar cartons, because it holds each jar in place. For a one-off start, bubble wrap and filler are enough. Either way, the jars must not be able to move when you shake the carton.

## Spice jars and powders

Spices do not leak the way oil does, but they have their own problems.

- **Moisture.** Humidity ruins spice, so use a jar or pouch with a good seal, and keep the carton dry.
- **Dust and smell.** A jar with a loose lid leaks powder, and strong spices can scent other goods in the carton. Bag each jar.
- **Glass.** A glass spice jar needs the same cushioning as any fragile item. See [how to pack fragile items for courier](/blogs/how-to-pack-fragile-items-for-courier).

For pouches, a small mailer or a snug carton is enough, with no grid. Use the [box size calculator](/tools/box-size-calculator) to find the size.

## Close, label and ship

- **Fill the gaps** so nothing moves, and press on the closed carton to check.
- **Tape every seam** in the H pattern. Put an extra strip across the bottom seams for a heavy carton. See our [BOPP tape](/products/bopp-tape) page.
- **Mark it** "this side up" on more than one face. The designer can print this-side-up and fragile icons.
- **Label** the top face, and put a copy of the address inside.
- **Photograph** the open carton before you seal it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you pack a pickle jar for courier without leaking?

Wipe the thread, close the cap and seal it, put the jar in its own sealed bag, wrap it in bubble wrap, and stand it upright in a carton with 2.5 cm of cushioning around and between the jars. Test one packed jar upside down first.

### How do I pack ghee jars for courier?

Leave a little headspace, because ghee expands in heat. Seal the cap, bag each jar, wrap it and pack it upright. Ghee softens in warm weather, so keep the parcel away from heat and use a bag so any leak stays inside.

### What size box do I need for 500 g pickle jars?

It depends on the jar. For two jars of about 8.5 cm across and 11.5 cm tall, you need about 25 x 14 x 17 cm inside; for six jars, about 36 x 25 x 17 cm. Measure your own jars and use the method above.

### Do I need partitions for glass jars?

They help, because they hold each jar upright and keep glass from touching glass. For a few jars, bubble wrap and filler can work. Partitions are usually a quote item, so ask when you enquire.

### Can I send pickle and ghee by courier?

Many sellers do, but each courier has its own terms for food and liquids, so check them first. Use sealed, bagged jars and a strong carton, and test a pack before you ship a batch.
