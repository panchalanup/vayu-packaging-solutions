# How to Use the 3D Box Designer: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Ordering a custom corrugated box usually means describing it in words: "about this big, brown, our logo on the front, strong enough for 5 kg". Words leave a lot of room for guessing, and guesses are what turn into wrong-sized or wrongly printed boxes.

The free **3D Box Designer** replaces the guessing with a box you can see. You set the style, size and board, put your own artwork on it, fold it open and shut, and get an indicative price, all in your browser. There is nothing to install and no sign-up.

This guide walks through the whole tool, step by step, in the order most people use it. Keep it open in one tab and the designer in another.

## Who the designer is for

The same tool serves three quite different jobs:

| You are... | You want to... | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| **A brand or shop owner** buying boxes for the first time | Find the right style and size, see your logo on it, know roughly what it costs | "Find my box" (guided mode) or a template |
| **A wholesaler or trader** quoting for your own buyers | Get a clean spec fast, compare quantities, share options | The size and board panel, the estimate, a share link |
| **A box manufacturer** meeting a customer | Show the box on a laptop or tablet, then send a proof | Presentation mode, the spec sheet and the dieline |

If you only have a minute, do steps 1, 2 and 5 below. You will have a box of the right shape and size with your logo on it. Everything else is optional and stays tucked away until you open it.

## Step 1: Start from a style, a template or "Find my box"

When the designer opens you see a plain kraft carton in the middle of the screen. A short three-step tour points out how to rotate the box, how to select a face, and where the quote button is. You can skip it; it will not come back unless you clear your browser data.

There are three ways to begin:

1. **Pick a style.** The style picker shows a card for each box style with a short "best for" line: the regular slotted carton (RSC), half slotted (HSC), full overlap, sleeve, telescope lid and base, mailer, pizza box and tray. If you are not sure which one you need, read our guide to the [8 box styles in 3D](/blogs/box-styles-in-3d).
2. **Use a template.** Templates are ready-made starting points such as an e-commerce shipper, an apparel mailer, a pizza box, a bakery tray, a gift box, a shoe box or an export carton. Each comes with a sensible size, board and sample artwork that you then replace with your own.
3. **Answer "Find my box".** Guided mode asks four plain questions: what you are packing, how heavy it is, how fragile it is, and how it travels (courier, local delivery, retail shelf, pallet or export). It then recommends a style, an inside size, a ply and a flute, with a one-line reason for each. One tap applies the whole recommendation.

**Tip:** if you already have a box you like, start from the same style and copy its inside measurements in step 2. It is the fastest way to a reorder spec.

## Step 2: Set the size (in mm, cm or inches)

Open the size controls and enter **length, width and height**. These are the **inside** dimensions, the space your product actually gets. The tool adds the board thickness for you, so you never have to work out the outside size by hand.

- **Units:** switch between millimetres, centimetres and inches at any time. The box does not change, only the numbers you see. Factories in India mostly quote in mm or inches; e-commerce sellers often think in cm. Use whatever you measured in.
- **Sliders or typing:** drag the sliders for a quick feel, or type exact values. Steps follow the unit you chose (5 mm, 0.5 cm or a quarter inch).
- **Quick presets:** small, medium and large presets give you a realistic starting point if you have no numbers yet.

Not sure what size to enter? The **fit checker** works it out from your product. Enter the product's own length, width and height, how much padding you want around it and whether it is fragile, and it recommends an inside size you can apply with one tap. Our guide on [finding the right box size](/blogs/find-right-box-size) explains the logic in detail.

## Step 3: Choose the board (ply, flute and colour)

Next, pick what the box is made of:

- **Ply:** 3-ply (single wall) for light goods and most e-commerce, 5-ply (double wall) for heavier goods and stacking, 7-ply (triple wall) for industrial and bulk loads. If you want the background, read [single wall vs double wall vs triple wall](/blogs/corrugated-wall-differences).
- **Flute:** the wave inside the board. B and E flutes print well and are thinner; C flute gives more cushioning. Combinations such as B+C are offered for 5-ply. See the [flute types guide](/blogs/flute-types-guide).
- **Board colour:** natural kraft, white, or a printed (flood) colour. You can also choose a different **inside colour**, for example a white-lined inside on a kraft box, which looks good in unboxing photos.
- **Finish:** matte or gloss, previewed on the 3D model so you can see how light catches the surface.

As you change the ply, the board edge on the 3D model gets visibly thicker. That is not decoration: the inside size stays fixed and the outside grows, exactly as it would on the real box.

## Step 4: Look at it properly (rotate, fold, cut away)

This is where 3D earns its keep. Spend a minute here before you add artwork.

- **Rotate and zoom:** drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use "fit view" to re-centre. On a phone, one finger rotates and two fingers zoom.
- **Fold it:** the assembly slider takes the box from a **flat blank** through **open top** to **sealed**. Watching it fold is the quickest way to understand where each flap ends up, which matters for artwork in step 5.
- **Show inside:** the cut-away view hides the front wall so you can see the inside colour and how deep the box really is.
- **Auto-rotate and full screen:** handy when you are showing the box to someone else.

## Step 5: Add your logo and text

Click any face of the box (or choose it from the surface list) and add:

- **An image:** your logo or artwork as PNG, JPG or SVG. Drag it to position it, then scale and rotate it.
- **Text:** a tagline, "Handle with care", a website or a batch line, with font, size, alignment and colour.
- **Brand colours:** type your exact hex or RGB values, and reuse recent colours across faces. Save your logo and colours as a **brand kit** so the next design starts branded.

As you work, **print checks** flag the problems printers reject artwork for: an image that is too low-resolution for its printed size, artwork running over an edge or too close to a fold, and text that is too small or hard to read on the board colour. Click a warning and the tool selects the item so you can fix it. Our [artwork and logo guide](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes) goes through each check.

## Step 6: Check strength, price and delivery

Three panels turn a nice picture into a buying decision:

1. **Strength guidance.** Enter the weight of one packed box and the tool tells you, in plain words, whether the board is comfortable for it and roughly how many boxes high you can stack. If the board is too light, it suggests the next ply up. See [will my box stack?](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide)
2. **Instant estimate.** Pick a quantity (100, 500, 1,000 or 5,000, or type your own) and see an **indicative** price per box and for the order, shown as a range. Change the size, ply or print and watch the number move. Our [pricing guide](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide) explains what drives it.
3. **Delivery estimate.** Enter a city or pincode for an indicative number of days, combining production time for your kind of job with transit.

All three are labelled indicative because real figures depend on paper grades, current paper prices, and how and where the boxes travel. The final quote confirms them.

## Step 7: Save, share and export

When the box looks right, you have several ways to keep or pass it on:

- **My designs:** save versions in your browser and come back to them later. Nothing is uploaded unless you choose to send it.
- **Share link:** a link that opens your exact design, ready for a colleague, a customer or WhatsApp.
- **Images and video:** download a product shot with a choice of backgrounds and angles, or record a short 360° spin or fold animation. See [product shots without a photoshoot](/blogs/packaging-product-shots-360-video).
- **Dieline:** download the flat layout as SVG or PDF at 1:1 scale in millimetres, with cut and crease lines. Read [dielines explained](/blogs/dielines-explained).
- **Spec sheet / proof PDF:** one page with three views, dimensions in your chosen unit, board, finish, quantity, a dieline thumbnail and a sign-off block for customer approval.
- **3D model:** a GLB file for other 3D tools and, on iPhone and iPad, an AR preview to see the box on your table at real size.

## Tips that save time

- **Measure the product, not an old box.** Old boxes have board thickness and wear built in. Use the fit checker with the product's own size.
- **Decide the style before the size.** A mailer and an RSC with the same inside size use very different amounts of board.
- **Fold before you print.** Run the assembly slider once with your artwork on. Logos that look centred on a flat face can end up under a flap.
- **Use the share link for approvals.** It is quicker than screenshots and always shows the latest version.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the 3D Box Designer really free?
Yes. There is no sign-up, no trial period and no locked features. We built it because customers who can see their box order with more confidence.

### Do I need to install anything?
No. It runs in a modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox) on a computer, tablet or phone. If your device cannot show 3D, the tool tells you and still lets you send a spec.

### Are the dimensions inside or outside?
Inside. You enter the space the product needs; the tool adds the board thickness to show the outside size.

### How accurate is the price?
It is an **indicative range** based on the board area, ply, print colours, finish and quantity. The final price depends on paper grades and current paper rates, and is confirmed in the quote.

### Is my artwork uploaded anywhere?
Not while you design. Images stay in your browser. They are only sent if you choose to send the design to us or share it.

### Can I use the designer and order elsewhere?
Yes. The dieline, spec sheet and images are yours to use with any printer.
