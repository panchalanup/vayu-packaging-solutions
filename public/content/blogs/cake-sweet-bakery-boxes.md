# Cake, Sweet and Bakery Boxes: Window Cake Boxes, Mithai Boxes, Trays and Clamshells

For a bakery or a sweet shop the box is the last thing your customer sees before the food, and often the first thing their guests see. A birthday cake arrives in a box. A Diwali sweet box goes from hand to hand through a family. A box of cookies is a small gift. The packaging has to protect something fragile, look good in a photo and hold up in a car boot.

This guide helps home bakers, bakeries, mithai shops and dessert brands choose between the **window cake box**, the **telescope sweet box**, the **cupcake and cookie boxes** and the **bakery tray**, and shows how to design each in the free **3D Box Designer**. ## A careful note on food

Packaging that touches food needs extra care, and this article is **general guidance, not food-safety advice**.

- **Say exactly what touches the box:** cream cake, dry sweets, oily snacks, warm bread. Different foods need different board, liners or coatings.
- **Moist or greasy food** usually needs a suitable barrier, such as a lined or coated surface, a greaseproof liner or a separate cake board or tray under the food.
- **Print and inks** need particular attention near food. Many businesses keep print on the outside and leave the food-contact surface plain.
- **Rules apply** to packaging that contacts food. They depend on the food and the use; confirm them with your food-safety adviser and ask your supplier what documentation can be supplied.
- **The designer helps with size, style and print. It does not certify any board, ink or film as food safe.**

## Window cake boxes

A window cake box is a shallow, one-piece, die-cut box with a hinged lid that has a clear film window. It folds from flat, which saves storage, and the big lid is a clean place for your bakery name, phone number and social handle.

### Choosing the size

Bakers think in weight or inches. Start from the **cake board**, not the cake: the board is what sits in the box, and a decorated cake is wider than a bare one at the base. Add a little room for icing and decoration.

| Designer preset | Inside size (cm) | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| Half-kilo cake | 20 x 20 x 10 | Small celebration cake |
| One-kilo cake | 25 x 25 x 12 | Standard birthday cake |
| Pastry box | 22 x 15 x 8 | Pastries, brownies, slices |

### In the designer, step by step

1. Open the [cake box designer](/box-designer/cake-box). It loads a 25 x 25 x 12 cm box in white 3-ply E-flute, with the window on.
2. In the **Edit Box** tab, enter the inside length, width and height. Use the unit switch for inches.
3. In **Cut-outs**, use the **Window size** slider. A large window shows more cake but leaves less board to print on and less strength in the lid; about half to two thirds of the lid is a sensible range to try.
4. Choose white for a fresh look or kraft for a natural one, and try **gloss** or **matte** finish. Both print well in one colour.
5. In **Customize**, add your bakery name in text, your logo and, from the **Cutouts** panel, bakery graphics: cake slice, cupcake, cookie, bread, croissant and more. Badges such as "Hand made" or "Small batch" add warmth with plain words.
6. Move the fold slider: watch the box go from flat to open lid to closed.
7. In **Price & order**, compare 100, 500 and 1,000 boxes. In **Actions**, download the **dieline** and a share link.

## Mithai and sweet boxes

Sweet boxes are bought as gifts, so the opening matters. The **sweet box (mithai)** template uses a **telescope box** (lid and base) at 22 x 22 x 6 cm, with a red outside, a gold inside and a gloss finish, plus decorative dividers and a sparkle graphic. The lid lifts straight off, the base is shallow so sweets sit in one layer, and the lid carries your shop name in a serif font.

**Planning a sweet box**

- **Design around weight.** Sweet boxes are typically sold by weight; decide your most common pack sizes first and design one base size for each.
- **Plan a liner or tray** under the sweets. The designer does not model inserts or liners, so mention them in your enquiry.
- **Use the inside.** A gold or cream inside colour makes the reveal rich.
- **Festive colours, neutral symbols.** The cutout library has stars, sparklers, glowing stars, ribbons, dividers and patterns. Use pattern fill for a border or a repeating motif.
- **Order early** before festivals; our [festive gift guide](/blogs/festive-gift-hamper-boxes) covers planning.

## Cupcake, cookie and bakery tray options

- **Cupcake box.** A window box (24 x 16 x 9 cm in the template) for six cupcakes or muffins. Check the height with the frosting.
- **Cookie box.** A clamshell with a hinged lid (18 x 14 x 6 cm in the template) for cookies and biscuits, quick to close at the counter.
- **Bakery tray.** An open die-cut tray with double walls and no lid, for pastries or sweets on display. A tray can sit inside a sleeve or a larger box for transport. The [bakery tray designer](/box-designer/bakery-box) loads the default.
- **Slide box.** A tray and sleeve, handy for gifting cookies or chocolates.

## Branding without clutter

Food packaging looks best when it is simple.

- **One colour on kraft or white** is economical and looks natural.
- **A name, a handle and a phone number** on the lid; leave the sides plain.
- **Cutouts, used sparingly.** One graphic and one badge, not ten.
- **Pattern fill** for a gift look: a small repeating cutout in a tint that matches your brand colour. "Match brand colour" tints every cutout in one tap.
- **Words, not claims.** Use plain words ("Fresh", "Handmade"). Do not print labels such as organic or certified unless you hold them and can show proof; the designer's badges deliberately avoid them.

## Tips for bakeries and sweet shops

- **Measure on the cake board.** Include icing, toppers and candles in the height.
- **Keep the box clean.** Print outside; leave the inside plain unless your supplier confirms it is suitable.
- **Plan for heat.** In summer, boxes sit in cars and queues. Ask about board and options for warm or creamy products.
- **Order two or three sizes,** not ten. One base size per weight keeps stock simple.
- **Test with the real product.** Order a small sample, pack a real cake and carry it home.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size cake box do I need?

Measure the cake board and the cake's tallest point, including decorations. The presets start at 20 x 20 x 10 cm for a half-kilo cake and 25 x 25 x 12 cm for a kilo cake; adjust to your own board.

### Can I make the window bigger or smaller?

Yes. Use the window size slider in the Cut-outs section. The dieline shows the true cut line.

### Is the box safe for food?

Board, inks and window film for food contact are chosen with your order, depending on what you pack and whether it touches the board. The designer does not certify materials; mention the food when you ask for a quote.

### Can a cake box have a separate lid?

The window cake box has a hinged lid. For a separate lid, choose the telescope style, which has a lid and a base.

### Can I print inside the box?

Yes, but for food, many bakers keep the food-contact surface plain. Ask us which options suit your product.

### Can I order a small quantity to test?

Ask the quote team about minimum quantities. Many bakers order a small sample first.
