# 10 Packaging Mistakes Small Businesses Make (and Fixes)

Packaging is rarely the reason someone starts a business, so it is often the thing that gets decided last, in a hurry, over the phone. That is when expensive mistakes happen: boxes that do not fit, boxes that are far stronger (and pricier) than needed, or print that looks nothing like what you imagined.

The good news is that most packaging mistakes are predictable. Here are the ten we see most often, why they happen, and how to avoid each one, including how the free **3D Box Designer** can help you catch them before you order.

## Quick checklist

Before you order your next batch of boxes, check that you have:

1. Stated inside dimensions, and said so
2. Sized the box from the packed product
3. Chosen the ply for the real load, not "to be safe"
4. Picked a style that suits how the box is used
5. Thought about stacking and storage
6. Kept artwork away from folds and made text large enough
7. Confirmed colours on a proof
8. Compared quantities, not just one price
9. Got sign-off from everyone who needs to approve
10. Kept a spec on file for repeat orders

Now the detail.

---

## Mistake 1: Not saying whether dimensions are inside or outside

**What happens:** You ask for a 30 × 20 × 15 cm box. You meant the space inside; the supplier read it as the outside. The board thickness takes several millimetres off each side, and your product no longer fits.

**How to avoid it:** Always quote **inside dimensions** for a box you will pack, and say so clearly. In the designer, you enter inside dimensions, and the spec sheet labels them.

Read more in [how to measure corrugated boxes](/blogs/box-measurements-guide).

## Mistake 2: Sizing from the product, not the packed product

**What happens:** You measure the bare product. Then you add bubble wrap, an insert card, a pouch or tissue, and the box is too tight.

**How to avoid it:** Measure the product exactly as it will be packed. Use the designer's **fit checker**: enter the product size, a padding allowance and whether it is fragile, and it recommends an inside size.

## Mistake 3: Choosing a box that is far too big

**What happens:** A bigger box "to be safe" means the product moves around, you use more filler, and you pay more for board. Many couriers charge by volumetric weight, so you may also pay more to ship air.

**How to avoid it:** Keep the box as small as the packed product allows. Watch the 3D model as you adjust the size, and use a size reference (an A4 sheet or a 1-litre bottle silhouette) in the scene to judge real size.

---

## Mistake 4: Over-building (or under-building) the board

**What happens:** You order 7-ply for a light product because it sounds stronger, and pay for strength you do not need. Or you order 3-ply for a heavy product that will be stacked, and the bottom boxes crush.

**How to avoid it:** Choose the ply for the actual weight, the stack height and the journey. The designer's **strength guidance** gives an indicative, plain-language verdict for the size and ply you choose, so you can compare 3, 5 and 7-ply before you ask for a price.

For the theory, see [single, double and triple wall](/blogs/corrugated-wall-differences).

## Mistake 5: Picking the wrong style

**What happens:** A regular slotted carton is used for a premium gift, and it looks like a shipping box. Or a die-cut mailer is chosen for a heavy industrial part, and it is not the right structure.

**How to avoid it:** Match the style to the use:

| Use | Style to consider |
|---|---|
| Shipping, storage, bulk | Regular slotted carton (RSC) |
| Heavier goods, stronger base | Full overlap (FOL) |
| E-commerce, subscriptions | Mailer |
| Gifts, shoes, premium | Telescope (lid and base) |
| Pizza, flat food | Pizza style |
| Display, bakery | Tray or half slotted (HSC) |

The designer's style picker shows each style as a card with a "best for" note. The guided **"Find my box"** mode asks about your product, weight, fragility and shipping method and suggests a style, size and board.

## Mistake 6: Forgetting about stacking and storage

**What happens:** Boxes that are fine on their own collapse at the bottom of a stack in your store room or on a truck.

**How to avoid it:** Think about how many boxes will sit on top of each other, and for how long. The strength guidance includes an indicative stacking verdict, and the stacked view shows what a stack of your boxes looks like.

## Mistake 7: Artwork that does not print well

**What happens:** The logo looks sharp on your screen but blurry on the box. A line of text disappears into a fold. Fine print is too small to read on corrugated board.

**How to avoid it:** Use a high-resolution logo (vector if you have it), keep artwork away from folds and edges, and keep text large. The designer's **print-ready checks** flag low-resolution images, artwork near folds or over edges, and text that is too small, and let you click each warning to jump to the problem.

## Mistake 8: Expecting screen colours to match exactly

**What happens:** Your brand colour looks perfect on screen and noticeably different on brown kraft board.

**How to avoid it:** Screen colours are always approximate, and kraft board changes how inks look. Enter your exact brand colour in the designer to preview it, then **confirm it on a physical proof** before a large run. White board shows colours more faithfully than kraft.

---

## Mistake 9: Comparing one price, not the quantity curve

**What happens:** You order 500 boxes because that is what you need this month, then reorder three times, paying a higher per-box price each time. Or you order 5,000 to get a low price and run out of storage.

**How to avoid it:** Look at prices across quantities before deciding. The designer's **indicative estimate** shows per-box and total prices at several quantity tiers, so you can balance price against storage and cash flow. Remember these are estimates; confirm the final price before ordering.

![The estimate panel with prices at four quantity tiers](/blog-images/designer/designer-estimate-tiers.png)
*Indicative prices at several quantities help you choose an order size.*

## Mistake 10: Approving by phone, with nothing on record

**What happens:** You agree the box on a call. The boxes arrive and someone says "that is not what we discussed". There is nothing in writing to check against.

**How to avoid it:** Get a written spec and a visual proof approved before production. In the designer, share a link with everyone who needs to approve, then download the **spec sheet PDF**, a one-page proof with views, dimensions, board, finish, quantity and an approval block. Save the design in My designs so the next order is identical.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common packaging mistake?

Getting the size wrong, usually because inside and outside dimensions were mixed up, or the product was measured without its packing. Quote inside dimensions of the packed product.

### Is a stronger box always better?

No. A box much stronger than needed costs more and weighs more. Choose the board for the real weight, stack height and journey.

### How do I know which ply I need?

Consider the product weight, how high boxes will be stacked and how far they travel. The designer's strength guidance gives an indicative verdict; we can confirm it for your product.

### Is the price in the designer final?

No. It is an indicative estimate to help you compare options and quantities. Send your design for a firm quote.

### Can I send my design to another supplier?

Yes. Share the link or the spec sheet PDF with anyone. We would of course be glad to quote too.
