# Volumetric Weight Calculator for India: Formula and Savings

Volumetric weight is the space a parcel takes up in kilograms: length x width x height in cm, divided by a divisor such as 5000. Many couriers bill the larger of that and the real weight. A 30 x 20 x 15 cm box holding 1 kg works out at 1.8 kg at 5000; a smaller box lowers it.

## The formula, in one line

**Volumetric weight (kg) = length x width x height (cm) / divisor**

The divisor is the number of cubic centimetres that count as one kilogram. A bigger divisor is kinder to you, because the same box converts to fewer kilograms.

| Divisor | What it means | Same box, 9,000 cm3 |
|---|---|---|
| 4000 | 4,000 cm3 counts as 1 kg | 2.25 kg |
| 5000 | 5,000 cm3 counts as 1 kg | 1.80 kg |
| 6000 | 6,000 cm3 counts as 1 kg | 1.50 kg |

Which divisor applies depends on the courier and often on the service, for example air, express or surface. Some couriers publish 5000 for express parcels and 4000 for heavier surface freight, but we have not verified any courier's current rule, so ask for yours in writing.

## A worked example you can check

Take a box that is 30 cm long, 20 cm wide and 15 cm high, with a parcel inside that weighs 1 kg.

1. Volume: 30 x 20 x 15 = **9,000 cm3**.
2. At a divisor of 5000: 9,000 / 5,000 = **1.8 kg**.
3. The parcel really weighs 1 kg, so the larger figure is 1.8 kg.
4. That is 0.8 kg above the real weight.

If your rate is R rupees per kg, the extra cost is 0.8 x R on every parcel. We do not know your rate, so the calculator asks you to type it from your rate card.

## Which weight do you pay on?

Most rate cards bill the **chargeable weight**, which is the larger of the actual weight and the volumetric weight, often rounded up to a slab. Rounding varies, so round up the way your rate card does.

| Parcel | Box (cm) | Actual | Volumetric at 5000 | Billed on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cushion cover set | 40 x 30 x 25 | 0.8 kg | 30,000 / 5,000 = 6.0 kg | **6.0 kg** (volumetric) |
| Hardbound books | 30 x 25 x 20 | 10 kg | 15,000 / 5,000 = 3.0 kg | **10 kg** (actual) |
| Two kurtis in a shipper | 35 x 25 x 20 | 1.2 kg | 17,500 / 5,000 = 3.5 kg | **3.5 kg** (volumetric) |

The weights in this table are illustrative. Light and bulky goods (clothes, cushions, hampers, plastic items) are billed on volume. Dense goods (books, tools, liquids) are billed on weight, so a smaller box does not help them.

There is a break-even point for every parcel: **box volume = actual kg x divisor**. For a 1 kg parcel at 5000, any box up to 5,000 cm3 is billed on the real weight. A 25 x 20 x 10 cm box is exactly 5,000 cm3, so it lands at 1.0 kg, with nothing extra.

## Measure the packed parcel, not the inside size

Couriers measure the **outside** of the parcel as it is handed over. The 3D designer and most box makers work in **inside** sizes, so the outside is a little bigger: add the board thickness on each side.

For a 3-ply box with board about 3 mm thick, that is 0.3 cm per wall, so 0.6 cm on each dimension:

- Inside 30 x 20 x 15 cm gives outside 30.6 x 20.6 x 15.6 cm.
- Volume: 30.6 x 20.6 x 15.6 = 9,833.6 cm3.
- At 5000: 9,833.6 / 5,000 = **1.97 kg**, not 1.80 kg.

That is a 9% difference from one measuring habit. A 5-ply box is thicker, so the gap is larger. Board thickness is indicative here; see [inside versus outside size](/blogs/box-measurements-guide) and confirm the thickness of the board you order. If tape, a pouch or a label makes the parcel bulge, measure that too.

## What a smaller box saves

Keep the 1 kg parcel and shrink the box one dimension at a time, at a divisor of 5000. The saving only appears while the volumetric weight is above the real weight.

| Box (cm) | Volume (cm3) | Volumetric kg | Less than the 30 x 20 x 15 box |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 x 20 x 15 | 9,000 | 1.80 | none |
| 30 x 20 x 12 | 7,200 | 1.44 | 0.36 kg |
| 27 x 20 x 12 | 6,480 | 1.30 | 0.50 kg |
| 27 x 17 x 12 | 5,508 | 1.10 | 0.70 kg |

Taking only 3 cm off the height saves 0.36 kg on each parcel. If you ship 1,000 such parcels a month, that is 360 kg less billed weight, so about 360 x R rupees a month at R per kg. The saving is real only if the smaller box still protects the product. Use padding only where it is needed, and see our guide to [finding the right box size](/blogs/find-right-box-size) for the fit checker's defaults: 1 cm padding per side, 2.5 cm for fragile goods, and 0.3 cm of ease.

## Five ways to lower volumetric weight

1. **Size the box to the product.** One "universal" box for everything is the most common reason for empty air. Two or three sizes usually cover a catalogue.
2. **Choose a flat style for flat goods.** A mailer 6 cm high converts to far less than a shipper 15 cm high with the same footprint: 30 x 20 x 6 = 3,600 cm3 is 0.72 kg at 5000, against 1.8 kg. See [mailer box sizes for T-shirts, kurtis, sarees and jewellery](/blogs/mailer-box-sizes-tshirt-kurti-saree-jewellery).
3. **Trim the padding that does nothing.** Sturdy goods need less. Breakable goods need more, and a cracked item costs more than a few grams.
4. **Pack several items in one box.** Two shirts in one box can add up to less volume than two small boxes, because every box repeats its own air gap. Do the sum for your own sizes.
5. **Shave a centimetre or two.** Moving from a 30 x 22 x 15 to a 30 x 20 x 15 box drops 9,900 cm3 to 9,000 cm3, about 9%, for the cost of 2 cm of width.

## Questions to ask your courier

Do not rely on a blog, including this one, for the figure on your invoice. Ask these and keep the answers:

- Which divisor applies to my service, and does it change by weight or by zone?
- Are dimensions rounded up to the next cm before the sum?
- Is the chargeable weight rounded up to a slab, and how big is the slab?
- Is there a minimum chargeable weight?
- Do flyers, polybags or soft packs follow the same rule?
- How do I dispute a re-measured parcel?

If you ship on several platforms or couriers, build one small table of divisors and update it when rate cards change.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I calculate volumetric weight?

Multiply length x width x height in centimetres, then divide by the divisor. For a 30 x 20 x 15 cm box and a divisor of 5000, that is 9,000 / 5,000 = 1.8 kg. Compare it with the real weight; many couriers bill the larger one.

### Which divisor should I use, 4000, 5000 or 6000?

Use the one on your courier's rate card for the service you ship with. 5000 is a common starting point, 4000 and 6000 also appear, and a courier can use different divisors for different services. If you do not know, calculate all three and plan for the highest.

### Do I use the inside or the outside size of the box?

Use the outside size of the finished parcel, because that is what a courier measures. Box makers and the 3D designer use inside sizes, so add the board thickness on each side, about 0.6 cm in total per dimension for a thin 3-ply box.

### Does every courier bill on volumetric weight?

Not necessarily in the same way. Rules depend on the courier, the service and the contract, and some parcels are billed on slabs or flat rates. Read your rate card or ask your account manager rather than assuming.

### How can I reduce volumetric weight without risking damage?

Cut empty space, not protection: pick a box close to the product size, use a flat style for flat goods, and test a packed sample before you commit to a size. Our [box size guide](/blogs/find-right-box-size) explains padding and ease.
