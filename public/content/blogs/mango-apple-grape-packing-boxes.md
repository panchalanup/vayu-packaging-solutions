# Packing Mangoes, Apples and Grapes for Sale and Export: Box Sizes, Board and Design Ideas

Three fruits, three different packing problems. **Mangoes** are heavy, bruise at a touch and ripen on the journey. **Apples** are firm and robust but travel in layers that must not shift. **Grapes** are delicate, crush under their own weight and need airflow more than almost anything else. A box that suits one can quietly ruin another.

This guide helps fruit growers, packers, FPOs and exporters plan the box for each fruit, and shows how to try the idea in the free **3D Box Designer** before you place an order. It covers starting sizes, board, vents, branding and a pallet check. For the general science of vents and hand holes, read our companion article on [ventilated cartons, open trays and hand holes](/blogs/fruit-vegetable-ventilated-boxes) first or after.

## A word on what this article does not promise

Fruit packing for export is governed by buyer specifications, destination-market requirements and, for some crops, official rules on marking, treatment and inspection. **This article does not state any standard or regulation**, and none of the sizes below is a rule. Where a fact depends on your buyer, crop or market, we say so and mark it for review. Always confirm requirements with your buyer, your exporter or the relevant authority before you print or order.

## One box does not fit all three fruits

| | Mango | Apple | Grape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main risk** | Bruising, ripening heat | Layers shifting, scuffing | Crushing, moisture, heat |
| **Typical pack idea** | Single or double layer, each fruit often wrapped, spacers between | Layers on trays or dividers | One or two shallow layers, often with liner |
| **Box idea** | Ventilated carton with hand holes | Open ventilated tray or shallow carton | Vented shallow box, light and wide |
| **Board idea** | 5-ply for heavier packs and stacking | 3-ply or 5-ply depending on stack | 3-ply for 2 kg-style packs, 5-ply for bigger |
| **Design look** | Warm, natural, fruit graphic | Fresh green or red accent | Deeper colour, leaf graphic |

## Mangoes: heavy, delicate and warm

Mangoes are often packed in a single layer or two, each fruit wrapped in paper or placed in a net or a pulp tray, with spacers so fruit does not touch. They carry weight, so the box has to carry a stack without crushing the layer at the bottom. They also need air, so the box usually has vents, and a hand hole each side because a full carton is heavy.

**In the designer:**

1. Open **Templates**, filter to **Fruit & produce** and choose **Mango carton**.
2. In **Edit Box**, set the inside size from one wrapped fruit: length across a tray, number across, number of layers, plus the paper or pulp between them. Add a little headroom.
3. Check the **Cut-outs** section: hand holes and vents are on. Try switching vents off to see the plainer look, then back on. The strength guide warns that openings reduce stacking strength.
4. In **Customize**, put your farm name or brand on the wall that faces out. Add a mango graphic from the **Cutouts** panel (search "mango", or the Hindi word *aam*, or *keri*) and a "Hand picked" or "Fresh" badge.
5. In **Price & order**, enter the packed box weight and check the strength guide. If it recommends the next ply up, try it and compare the price.

## Apples: layers, trays and cartons

Apples are firm, but they scuff and bruise if layers slide. Orchards typically use trays or dividers that keep each fruit in its own cell, and they pack in layers. An **open ventilated tray** is a quick way to present apples in a shop or for local sale; for longer trips, a shallow ventilated carton with dividers (the dividers themselves are not designed in the tool, mention them when you enquire) protects more.

**In the designer:**

1. Open the **Apple tray** template. It uses the open produce tray style with vents.
2. Set the size from one layer of fruit on its tray, plus the height of the layer.
3. Compare with a carton: open the **style picker**, set **What are you packing?** to **Fruit & vegetables** and try the **Ventilated produce carton** on the same size.
4. Pick a fresh accent colour: green or red on kraft reads as apples at a glance.

## Grapes: air, light weight and no crushing

Grapes are the most delicate of the three. They crush if piled deep, they sweat if airflow is poor, and bunches are easily damaged by rough handling. Boxes tend to be shallow and wide, hold a small weight (a few kilograms), and carry a liner or paper. Because the box is light, 3-ply board is often enough; stacking height and the cold chain decide.

**In the designer:**

1. Open the **Grape box** template: a shallow vented carton in green.
2. Keep the height low, so the fruit is one or two layers deep. Watch the inside depth in the **Edit Box** panel.
3. Keep vents modest. The **Cut-outs** section also lets you switch hand holes off for a lighter, tidier look.
4. In **Customize**, add a grapes graphic from the **Cutouts** panel and your brand, with light text on the green board for contrast.

## Board: how to think about it

- **3-ply (single wall)** works for light packs in short stacks.
- **5-ply (double wall)** suits heavier fruit, taller stacks and pallets. Vents and hand holes weaken the wall, so the extra board helps. General literature reports that openings can reduce stacking strength by roughly 10-40%; see the [produce box guide](/blogs/fruit-vegetable-ventilated-boxes) for the context.
- **Moisture** matters. Cold, humid conditions lower corrugated board strength, so ask your supplier about options if boxes will spend time in a cold room.

## Selling locally vs exporting

**Local and wholesale.** A clean, branded carton raises the perceived value of the fruit, helps repeat buyers recognise you and makes your boxes easy to spot at a mandi or in a shop. Keep the print simple: your name, the fruit and variety, the net weight, and your contact. A cutout badge ("Hand picked", "Daily fresh") adds warmth without claims.

**Export.** The buyer, not the packer, usually sets the specification: pack size, box dimensions, marking and sometimes board strength. Ask for it in writing. Then use the designer to:

- build the box to the buyer's size and show a **3D preview and share link** before anything is printed,
- check how it sits on a pallet with the **Showcase** tab's **On a pallet** scene (the designer assumes a 1.2 x 1.0 m pallet; confirm the pallet size your buyer uses),
- download a **dieline** and a **spec sheet** to send to your supplier.

## Design tips for fruit boxes

- **Lead with the fruit.** One strong graphic and one short name beats a crowded wall.
- **Use the colour of the fruit family.** Warm kraft for mangoes, green for grapes, red or green accent for apples.
- **Keep print away from vents and hand holes** and away from folds. The print checks flag both.
- **Plan reorders.** Keep the same footprint across sizes so pallets stay stable. Use **My designs** or the spec sheet to keep the record.
- **Do not claim what you cannot show.** Use generic words (fresh, hand picked). Do not print certification marks or claims you do not hold; the cutout library deliberately has none.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size box is right for mangoes?

It depends on the variety, size and count per layer. The mango carton template starts at 36 x 26 x 14 cm inside, a 5 kg-style pack in the tool, but measure your own fruit and wrapping and adjust.

### Which board should I use for apples?

3-ply for short stacks, 5-ply for tall stacks or pallets. Vented walls are weaker, so be generous with the board and test a sample.

### Do grapes need hand holes?

Not necessarily. Hand holes help with carrying, but a small light box may not need them. Vents matter more. You can switch hand holes off and compare.

### Can I export in your boxes?

Export packing depends on your buyer's specification and the destination market. We can make boxes to a size and board you specify; we do not state which standards a box meets. Ask your buyer or exporter for their requirements.

### Are the template sizes standard pack sizes?

No. They are starting points taken from common pack ideas. Always size from your own fruit.

### Is the board food safe for fresh fruit?

Tell us what you are packing and whether the fruit touches the board. Suitable options are confirmed with your quote. The designer helps with style, size and print; it does not certify materials.
