# Box Specs for Traders: Spec, Price and Share in Minutes

As a packaging trader, you sit in the middle of two conversations. Your buyer wants a price *now*, usually based on a photo, a rough size and the words "same as last time but stronger". Your manufacturer wants a clean specification before they will commit to anything. Every hour spent translating between the two is an hour you are not selling.

This guide shows how to use the free **3D Box Designer** as your quoting desk: capture a spec quickly, check an indicative price at several quantities, and send something your buyer can open on their phone and your supplier can read without phoning you back.

## The trader's problem in one picture

A typical enquiry reaches you as a WhatsApp message: a photo of an old carton, "need 2,000 like this, 5-ply, urgent". Before you can quote, you need answers to questions the buyer often has not thought about:

- Are the dimensions inside or outside?
- Which style: regular slotted, half slotted, full overlap, a die-cut mailer?
- What is going inside, and how heavy is it?
- Plain, or printed? How many colours?
- Will the boxes be stacked in a godown, or on a truck for days?

Get any of these wrong and you either lose margin (you quoted the wrong board) or lose the customer (the boxes arrive and do not fit). The designer does not answer them for you, but it puts all of them on one screen, so you ask them in one go.

---

## When to reach for the designer

- **A new buyer** with no previous spec on file.
- **A "like this but different" request** where size, board or print is changing.
- **Several options to compare**, such as 3-ply versus 5-ply, or plain versus printed.
- **A buyer who needs approval from someone else** and will forward whatever you send.
- **Mixed units**: the buyer talks inches, the factory works in millimetres.

For a straight repeat order of a spec you already hold, your existing price list is faster. Use the designer when something is new or uncertain.

## Step by step: from enquiry to shareable quote

### 1. Choose the style

Pick the style the buyer is describing. If they sent a photo of a normal brown carton with four flaps top and bottom, that is a regular slotted container (RSC). If it has a hinged lid and folds out of one piece, it is probably a mailer. The style picker shows each option as a card with a "best for" line, which helps when you are not sure what the buyer means.

### 2. Enter the size in their unit

Switch between **mm, cm and inches** to match the buyer, then type the inside length, width and height. The model updates as you type. If the buyer only knows the product size, use the **fit checker**: enter the product's dimensions, a padding allowance and whether it is fragile, and the tool recommends an inside size you can apply with one tap.

![The fit checker recommending an inside box size from product dimensions and padding](/blog-images/designer/designer-fit-checker.png)
*The fit checker turns a product size plus padding into a recommended inside box size.*

### 3. Pick board and check strength

Choose 3, 5 or 7-ply. The strength guidance gives a plain-language, indicative verdict such as roughly how much weight each box can carry and how high it can stack. It is an estimate based on board caliper and box size, not a lab test, but it is enough to spot a buyer who is about to put 20 kg into a box that will not take it.

### 4. See the indicative price across quantity tiers

The estimate panel shows an **indicative** per-box and total price at quantity slabs (100, 500, 1,000 and 5,000). Seeing the tiers side by side helps you have the "if you take 1,000 instead of 500" conversation with real numbers in front of both of you.

![The estimate panel with per-box and total prices at four quantity tiers](/blog-images/designer/designer-estimate-tiers.png)
*Indicative prices at four quantity tiers. Labelled as an estimate, because final prices depend on board rates, print and freight on the day.*

These are estimates for comparison, not a final quote. Board prices move, and print set-up, freight and urgency all change the real number. Use the tiers to guide the buyer's choice of quantity, then confirm.

### 5. Add the buyer's logo if they want print

If the buyer is considering printed boxes, drop their logo on the right face. It takes a minute and often settles the "plain or printed" question by itself.

### 6. Share it

You now have three ways to send the spec:

- **Share link**: opens the exact design in the browser. Your buyer can rotate it and forward it.
- **Spec sheet PDF**: one page with views, dimensions in the chosen unit, board, finish, quantity and a dieline thumbnail. Good for buyers who need something to file or sign.
- **Send to Vayu**: if you want us to quote or make it, the "Send my design" form attaches the spec and a preview automatically.

## A checklist for every spec you send

Before you share a design with a buyer or a supplier, check that it states:

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Style (RSC, HSC, mailer, telescope...) | Changes the blank size, the die and the price |
| Inside dimensions and unit | The single most common source of wrong boxes |
| Ply (3, 5 or 7) | Drives strength and cost |
| Board colour and finish | Kraft, white or coloured; matte or gloss |
| Print: none, logo, how many faces | Print set-up is a real cost at low quantities |
| Quantity | Price per box falls as quantity rises |
| Delivery city or pincode | Freight and lead time |

The spec sheet covers most of this automatically. Add delivery details in your message.

## Tips for traders

- **Save each buyer's designs with a clear name.** "Sharma Traders, 12x10x8 in, 5-ply" is easier to find than "Design 7". Designs are saved in your browser under My designs.
- **Snapshot before you change.** When a buyer asks for a tweak, save a version first, so you can go back if they change their mind.
- **Build a small brand kit for repeat buyers.** Store their logo and colours once, apply them to every new box.
- **Quote two options, not five.** A good and a better option (for example 3-ply printed and 5-ply printed) is easier for a buyer to decide on.
- **Keep units consistent with your supplier.** Share in the buyer's unit, but check your supplier is reading millimetres if that is what their machines use. The spec sheet shows the unit clearly.
- **Remember the estimate is indicative.** Protect your margin by confirming prices before you commit to a buyer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use the designer to quote my own buyers?

Yes. The tool is free and needs no sign-up. You can use it to build specs and share links with your buyers. If you want Vayu to make the boxes, send the design to us for a firm price.

### Are the prices in the designer final?

No. They are indicative estimates based on board area, ply, print and quantity, and are labelled as such. Final prices depend on current paper rates, print, freight and lead time.

### Which units does the designer support?

Millimetres, centimetres and inches. Switching units changes the numbers shown, not the box.

### What does the spec sheet include?

Three views of the box, dimensions in your chosen unit, board, finish, quantity, a dieline thumbnail, the date and an approval block.
