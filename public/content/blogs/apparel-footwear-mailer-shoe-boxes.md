# Apparel and Footwear Packaging: Designing Garment Mailers and Shoe Boxes

Clothes and shoes are personal purchases. Customers try them on, post them, return them, and keep (or throw away) the box they came in. For an apparel or footwear brand, the box is part of the product: it says whether you are a premium label, a playful streetwear brand or a practical everyday basics store.

This guide shows how to design the two boxes most clothing and footwear brands need, a **garment mailer** and a **shoe box**, in the free **3D Box Designer**, and how to get the size, board and branding right before you order.

## The problem: soft products, hard choices

Packing clothes and shoes looks simple until you scale:

- **Garments get crushed** in poly bags, and arrive creased and less appealing.
- **Boxes are too big**, so the product slides, tissue is wasted and courier charges go up.
- **Shoe boxes collapse** when stacked in a store room or a warehouse rack.
- **Returns come back in poor shape** because the original box did not survive the first trip.
- **The brand looks generic**, even when the product is not.

A properly sized mailer and a sturdy shoe box solve most of these, and both are easy to design in 3D.

## When to use which box

| Product | Box to start with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirts, shirts, kurtas, knitwear | Garment mailer | One-piece, folds flat, tuck lid, great for unboxing |
| Jeans, jackets, heavier items | Deeper mailer or RSC | Extra height for thicker folds |
| Sarees, dupattas, gift sets | Mailer or telescope gift box | Presentation matters as much as protection |
| Shoes, sandals, sneakers | Shoe box (lid and base) | Lid lifts off, stacks in stores, reusable |
| Shoes shipped online | Shoe box inside a plain shipper | The shipper takes the courier knocks |

## Step by step: a garment mailer

### 1. Start from the apparel mailer template

Open the designer and choose the "Apparel mailer" template, or go straight to the [mailer box preset](/box-designer/mailer-box). The template comes with a garment-friendly shape: wide and flat.

![The apparel mailer template in the designer, wide and shallow with the lid open](/blog-images/designer/designer-apparel-mailer.png)
*The apparel mailer template: wide and shallow, sized for a folded garment.*

### 2. Size from the folded garment

Fold the garment the way you will pack it, wrapped in tissue if you use it, and measure length, width and thickness. Use the **fit checker** to add a small padding allowance. For clothes, a snug fit is usually better than a loose one: it keeps the fold crisp.

Tip: fold your three best-selling sizes and measure each. Often one box covers small to large, and a second covers XL and above or heavier fabrics.

### 3. Choose board and finish

Most garment mailers are 3-ply, in a fine flute for a crisp edge and good print. Choose kraft for a natural look, white for a clean premium look, or a coloured board for a bold brand.

### 4. Brand outside and inside

Put your logo on the lid, and use the **inside colour** and inside-lid artwork to create a moment: a thank-you note, a size-exchange instruction, a QR code to your lookbook. Use the fold animation to see what the customer sees as they open it.

### 5. Check, share and send

Run the print checks, export a product shot for your team and send the design to us for a price.

## Step by step: a shoe box

### 1. Open the shoe box preset

Go to the [shoe box designer](/box-designer/shoe-box). It loads a lid-and-base (telescope) style, the classic shoe box where the lid lifts straight off.

![The shoe box preset showing a base and a lifted lid](/blog-images/designer/designer-shoe-box.png)
*The shoe box preset: a telescope base with a separate lid. The fold animation lifts the lid off.*

### 2. Size it from your largest pair in each range

Place the pair as you will pack it (usually heel to toe, often with tissue) and measure the length, width and height of the pair together. Add clearance for tissue and any shoe bag. Shoe boxes vary widely by size range and style; for example, a men's sneaker box is noticeably bigger than a women's sandal box, so most brands use a few box sizes rather than one.

### 3. Set the lid depth

In the telescope style you can set the **lid depth**: a shallow lid (a few centimetres) is easy to lift and saves board; a full-depth lid covers the whole base and feels more premium and protective.

### 4. Choose a board that stacks

Shoe boxes often stack in store rooms and on shelves. Look at the **strength guidance** for an indicative stacking verdict. A stiffer board or a 5-ply base can help for heavy boots or tall stacks.

### 5. Design for the shelf and the end panel

In shops, the end panel faces outward on the shelf. Put your logo and space for a size label there. On the lid, keep it clean: logo and perhaps a short line.

### 6. Decide on a shipper

If you ship shoes online, the shoe box itself usually travels inside a plain outer carton. Design the outer as a simple [shipping carton](/box-designer/shipping-carton) sized around the shoe box, so the branded box arrives clean.

## Branding ideas for clothing and footwear

- **Contrast inside and out.** Kraft outside, brand colour inside. It costs little and is memorable.
- **Make returns easy.** Print short return instructions inside the lid, and size the box so the product goes back in easily.
- **Design for reuse.** Shoe boxes are often kept for storage. A clean, tasteful design survives on a shelf at home for years.
- **Leave label space.** Plan where the courier label or size sticker goes so it does not cover your logo.
- **Keep artwork away from folds.** The print checks warn when artwork is too close to a fold or edge.
- **Show it before you print it.** Export product shots for your website and social channels while you wait for the boxes.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best box for shipping clothes?

For most garments, a 3-ply corrugated mailer sized to the folded garment works well: it protects the fold, assembles without tape and makes a good unboxing. Heavier items may need a deeper box or 5-ply.

### How do I choose shoe box sizes?

Measure your largest pair in each size range with tissue, and add clearance. Most brands use a small number of box sizes across their range. Try each size in the designer first.

### What is a telescope shoe box?

It is a lid-and-base box: a base tray and a slightly larger lid that slides over it. In the designer this is the telescope style, and you can set how deep the lid is.

### Can I ship a shoe box directly by courier?

You can, but it may get scuffed or labelled across your design. Many brands put the shoe box inside a plain outer carton for shipping.

### Can I print inside a garment mailer?

Yes. Set an inside colour and add artwork to the inside faces in the designer. Inside print is quoted with the rest of the print.

### Do I need a separate design for each size?

Save each size as its own design in My designs. A brand kit keeps the logo and colours consistent across them.

### Will the box colour match my brand colour exactly?

Screen colours are approximate, and board colour affects ink. Confirm brand colours on a physical proof.
