# What Drives Cost per Box (and How to Lower It)

"How much will it cost?" is usually the first question about a custom box, and the hardest to answer without a spec. The price of a corrugated box is not one number from a list. It is built up from the board, the print, the finish, the set-up and the quantity, and changing any one of them moves the total.

The free **3D Box Designer** shows an **instant indicative estimate** that updates as you design. This guide explains what goes into that number, why it is shown as a range, and the practical levers that bring the price per box down without making the box worse.

## First, what "indicative" means

The estimate is a **guide, not a quote**. It uses typical rates for board, printing and conversion, and shows a **range** around a midpoint so nobody mistakes it for a fixed price. The final price depends on things the designer cannot know yet: the exact paper grades (GSM and bursting factor), current paper prices, which change through the year, and delivery to your location.

## The six things that drive the price per box

### 1. Board area: size and style together
Boxes are priced largely by how much board they use, and that is decided by the **flat blank**, the unfolded sheet the box is cut from, not by the inside volume. Two things set the blank:

- **Size.** A bigger box needs a bigger sheet. Height adds less than you might expect; length and width add more, because they also set the flap sizes.
- **Style.** For the same inside size, a mailer's blank is wider than an RSC's (double side walls, a lid and a tuck), and a telescope box needs two blanks. A full overlap carton has bigger flaps than an RSC.

You can see the blank for any design by downloading its **dieline**. See [dielines explained](/blogs/dielines-explained).

### 2. Ply (and board surface)
More plies means more paper per square metre: 5-ply costs noticeably more per square metre than 3-ply, and 7-ply more again. A **white top liner** costs a little more than natural kraft, and a printed (flood) colour adds a print colour. The [GSM guide](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength) explains how paper weight feeds into this.

### 3. Print colours
Flexo printing on corrugated board is priced **per colour**: each colour needs its own printing plate and a pass through the press. The designer counts colours from your design:

- one colour for each distinct **text colour**,
- an extra colour if the **board itself** is a printed colour,
- a **photo or full-colour logo** counts as full colour (four colours).

A one-colour logo in black or a brand colour is the classic economical choice.

### 4. Finish
A **gloss** varnish adds a little per square metre over matte. You can preview both on the 3D model.

### 5. One-time set-up
Two costs are paid once per job and **spread over the quantity**:

- **printing plates**, one per colour,
- a **cutting die** for die-cut styles (mailer, pizza box, tray).

On 100 boxes these set-up costs can be a large part of the price per box; on 5,000 they almost disappear. That is the single biggest reason small runs cost more per box.

### 6. Quantity
Larger runs spread set-up and machine time over more boxes, so the price per box falls as quantity rises. The designer shows four slabs, **100, 500, 1,000 and 5,000**, plus any quantity you type. Small runs carry a short-run premium; bigger slabs earn a volume rate.

## Reading the estimate panel

The estimate sits in the **Price & order** tab. The panel shows:

- **Price per box** as a range, with the midpoint highlighted,
- **Order total** at the chosen quantity,
- the **quantity chips** so you can compare slabs in one tap,
- a short **breakdown**: board, print, finish, conversion and set-up per box.

Try this exercise once; it teaches you more than any price list:

1. Set your size and style with plain kraft board and no print. Note the price at 1,000.
2. Add a one-colour logo. Note the change.
3. Replace it with a full-colour photo. Note the change again.
4. Switch 3-ply to 5-ply.
5. Tap 100 and then 5,000.

You will see exactly which decisions matter for **your** box.

## Ten ways to lower the price per box

1. **Right-size the box.** Every centimetre you take off length and width saves board on the walls and the flaps. Use the [fit checker](/blogs/find-right-box-size) instead of rounding up generously.
2. **Pick the leanest style that works.** For heavier goods and short runs, an RSC usually beats a mailer on cost. Use a mailer where the unboxing is worth it. See [which box style should you choose](/blogs/which-box-style-should-you-choose).
3. **Do not over-specify the board.** If the strength guidance says 3-ply is comfortable for your weight and stacking, 5-ply is money spent on nothing. See [will my box stack?](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide)
4. **Use fewer print colours.** A one or two-colour logo on kraft looks deliberate and premium, and avoids the cost of full colour.
5. **Print less of the box.** One printed face can do the job of four. Or print a **sleeve** and keep the boxes plain.
6. **Stay with kraft.** Natural kraft avoids the white liner premium and a flood colour.
7. **Choose matte unless gloss sells.** Gloss adds cost per square metre.
8. **Order in the next slab up** if you will use the boxes within a reasonable time: the quantity chips show how far the price per box falls from one slab to the next.
9. **Standardise sizes across products.** One box for three similar products means larger runs and one set of plates.
10. **Reuse plates and dies.** Keep the same artwork and size on reorders, and ask us whether stored plates and dies can be reused for your job, so set-up costs are not paid again.

## Wholesalers and traders: quoting faster

If you buy boxes to resell or to supply your own customers:

- Switch the unit to whatever your buyer uses (mm, cm or inches), so the spec you send matches their drawings.
- Use the **quantity chips** to show your buyer the price break between slabs.
- Send the **spec sheet PDF** and a **share link** together; the buyer sees the box and the numbers in one place.
- Use **compare-quote prefill** to send the exact spec through our [quote comparison form](/compare-quote) without retyping it.

Remember that the estimate is ex-GST and ex-delivery; add both before passing a figure on.

## What the estimate does not include

- **GST.**
- **Delivery.** The designer gives an indicative delivery time by city or pincode, but freight is quoted separately.
- **Inserts, partitions or special materials** such as food-grade or moisture-resistant board.
- **Special print processes** beyond flexo colours and a gloss/matte finish.

Ask for these in the note when you send your design.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does the estimate show a range instead of one price?
Because paper grades and paper prices vary. A range is honest; the quote fixes the number.

### Why is 100 boxes so much more per box than 5,000?
Set-up costs (plates and dies) and machine time are mostly fixed per job. Spread over 100 boxes they are a big share of each box; over 5,000 they are small.

### Is a printed box always more expensive?
Yes, but often by less than people expect when it is a one or two-colour logo on a run of a few thousand. Try it in the estimate panel.

### Does a bigger box always cost more?
Generally yes, because it uses more board. But a slightly bigger standard size you already order in volume can be cheaper than a small custom run. Compare both.

### Can I get the estimate as a document?
Download the spec sheet PDF, or send the design to us and we will reply with a quote.
