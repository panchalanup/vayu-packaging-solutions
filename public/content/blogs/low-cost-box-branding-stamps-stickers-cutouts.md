## Three low-cost ways to brand a plain box

| Route | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rubber or self-inking stamp** | A stamp made from your artwork, pressed on each box | One colour, simple marks, tiny runs | Uneven ink, smudging, slow if you have many boxes |
| **Sticker or label** | Labels printed on a roll or sheet and stuck on by hand | A logo in colour, a seal over the tape seam, a "Thank you" mark | Adhesive on rough or dusty board; labour to apply |
| **Printed tape** | Packing tape printed with your name or logo | Branding the seam on every parcel | Ask the supplier if it is offered and in what minimum |

All three work on a plain brown or white box you already buy. Each adds a small step per box, so the cost is mostly labour. When the labour is more than the extra cost of printing the box, it is time to move on ([printing on corrugated](/blogs/flexo-offset-digital-printing-corrugated-boxes)).

## Why preview first

A stamp or sticker is cheap, but a wrong one is wasted: too big for the face, a colour that vanishes on kraft, a logo too fine to stamp cleanly. Preview solves the problem before you buy:

- Place the mark on the real box size and see how much of the face it covers.
- Try your brand colour **on kraft and on white** side by side.
- Move it, resize it and compare three positions in a couple of minutes.
- Send the screenshot or the share link to your stamp maker or label printer so they know what you want.

## Preview it in five steps

1. **Pick a plain box.** Open the [designer](/box-designer) and choose the stock-style box you buy now, such as a [shipping carton](/box-designer/shipping-carton) or a [mailer](/box-designer/mailer-box). Set the inside size.
2. **Open Cutouts.** In the **Customize** tab, find the **Cutouts** section. Use the **Recommended for this box** row, the group chips or the search box ([full guide](/blogs/how-to-add-cutouts-box-design)).

3. **Tint it to one colour.** Select the cutout and choose a colour, or use **Match brand colour** to recolour every cutout at once. A one-colour tint looks like a one-colour stamp or label print.

![The Match brand colour swatches open above a box whose stickers all use the same green](/blog-images/designer/designer-cutouts-brand-colour.png)

4. **Place and size it.** Drag it onto the face where it will go. Keep it about 0.5 cm away from folds and edges, where the designer's print checks warn you.
5. **Fold the box.** Drag the assembly slider from flat to sealed. A mark that looks centred on a flat face can end up across a fold or under a flap.

Add your own logo as an image in the Customize tab if you want your real mark rather than a generic one. Then screenshot it or share the link.

## Which cutouts suit which job

| Job | Cutout group | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A seal over the tape seam | Shapes, frames, patterns | A circle or ribbon with your logo inside |
| A friendly note | Badges | Generic words such as Thank you, Handmade, Gift |
| Handling marks | Shipping marks | This way up, fragile, keep dry |
| A quiet pattern on the sides | Patterns | Dots, stripes, leaves, repeated at low opacity |
| Contact details | Contact icons | Phone, mail, web, location |
| A product hint | Fruits, bakery, clothing and others | A mango on a fruit box, a cupcake on a cake box |

The badges use plain generic words only. If you hold a certification mark and are allowed to use it, add it from your certifier's own file as an image. The designer makes no certification claims for you.

## Keep it simple so it stamps well

- **One colour, solid shapes.** Fine lines and tiny text fill in on rough board.
- **Large enough to read.** The designer warns about text under 8 pt, and board is coarser than paper.
- **Contrast with the box.** A dark green on dark kraft nearly disappears; try a lighter tint or white.
- **Leave clear board around the mark.** A thumb's width of space looks neat.
- **Place it where the packer can reach.** A stamp on the lid centre is quick; one near a fold is not.

## When each route makes sense

Think about the time and the total. As an illustration in cost units, not prices: if stamping takes a packer a little time per box, multiply by the number of boxes and the packer's hourly cost. Compare that with the extra cost of printed boxes from your supplier, which the designer's [estimate](/blogs/box-price-estimate-guide) gives as an indicative range. If the labour for 1,000 stamped boxes is more than the premium for 1,000 printed ones, print them.

| Your situation | A sensible route |
|---|---|
| Testing a product with a few dozen orders | Stamp or sticker on a plain box |
| A few hundred boxes a month, one colour | Stamp for now; ask for a one-colour printed quote |
| Boxes ship in thousands, same logo | Ask for one-colour printed boxes |
| Gift or premium look | Sticker or tissue first; then printed boxes if it earns its place |

Our current minimum for custom printed boxes is 500 ([minimum order quantity](/blogs/minimum-order-quantity-custom-boxes)). If you are below it, stamps and stickers are the practical bridge.

## A ten-minute test you can run today

1. Open your usual box in the designer and place your logo as an image.
2. Tint it to one colour. If it loses its meaning in one colour, it will not stamp well either; simplify it.
3. Try three positions: lid centre, a corner of the front, and across the seam.
4. Try two colours: your brand colour, and black or white for contrast on the board.
5. Pick the winner, share the link and send it to your stamp maker or label printer with the real size in cm.

## Common mistakes

- **A mark too big for the face.** It covers the whole panel and looks crowded; about a third to a half of the face width is usually enough.
- **Too many small cutouts.** Three bigger marks beat twelve small ones.
- **Forgetting the fold.** A label that crosses a flap edge wrinkles. Check the folded box.
- **Different marks on different boxes.** Pick one mark and use it everywhere, so packages look like they come from one brand.

## Before you buy the stamp or labels

- Preview the mark in the designer on your actual box size and share the screenshot.
- Ask the stamp maker or label printer what file type and minimum line weight they need.
- Test the stamp ink on your board. Ink may dry slowly on coated surfaces and show on kraft differently.
- Test the label adhesive on your board, including dust and low temperatures if boxes sit in a godown.
- Do not stamp or stick on a surface that touches food; ask for food-safe information.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I preview a rubber stamp in the designer?

You can preview its size, colour and position by placing a cutout or your logo and tinting it to one colour. The designer does not simulate ink texture, so the real stamp will look rougher.

### How many cutouts does the designer have?

512 cutouts in 17 groups, and you can place up to 80 on one design. They are original or openly licensed graphics, credited on the image credits page.

### Will a sticker stick to corrugated board?

Often, on a clean, dry surface, but board texture and dust vary. Test your label on the actual board and ask the label supplier about adhesive.

### When should I move to printed boxes?

When the labour to stamp or stick is more than the extra cost of printing, or when you need a finish a stamp cannot give. Ask for a one-colour printed quote at your quantity.

### Can I use the same artwork for a stamp and for a printed box?

Yes, if it is one colour and made of solid shapes. Keep the original file, ask the printer what format they need, and check the print checks in the designer first.
