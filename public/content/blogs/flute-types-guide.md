# Corrugated Flute Types: A, B, C, E and F Flute Compared

Two boxes can both be "3-ply" and behave very differently, because the wavy layer inside, the **flute**, comes in different sizes. Flute choice changes how well a box cushions, how it stacks and how sharply it prints.

This guide compares the five common flutes in plain terms and shows how to pick one.

**Next step:** you can select the flute for a 3, 5 or 7-ply box in the [3D Box Designer](/box-designer), or [ask us to recommend one](/quote).

## What a flute does

The flute is a row of paper arches between two liners. It does three jobs:

- **Cushions** the contents by absorbing shock.
- **Carries load.** The arches work like small columns when the box is stacked.
- **Adds stiffness** to the board, so walls and lids stay flat.

Taller flutes give more cushioning and thickness. Finer flutes give a flatter, smoother surface that prints better.

## The five flutes at a glance

The letters come from the order in which the sizes were introduced, not from size. | Flute | Height (approx.) | Flutes per metre (approx.) | Main strength | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 4.5 to 5 mm | 100 to 120 | Cushioning and stacking load | Thick, prints less sharply |

| **E** | 1.2 to 1.8 mm | 280 to 320 | Thin, flat, high-quality print | Little cushioning, lighter loads |
| **F** | about 0.8 mm | 400 or more | Very smooth, board-like feel | Minimal cushioning, premium price |

Note that flute counts are quoted per **metre** here. You will also see "per foot" counts in older tables, which are about three times smaller.

## How each flute is used

### A flute
The cushioning flute. It is thick and good for stacking load when the box is new, but the surface is wavier so print is less crisp. In practice it is used less often than B or C for printed boxes.

### B flute
Flat and tight, with good resistance to crushing from the face. That makes it a favourite for die-cut boxes, printed cartons and boxes that carry canned or jarred goods. Mailers and tuck-end cartons are often made in B or E.

### C flute
The general-purpose choice for shipping cartons: enough height for cushioning and stacking, and widely available. If you are not sure, C is a safe starting point for a plain shipping box.

### E flute
A fine flute for retail and e-commerce boxes where **looks matter**. The thin, flat surface prints well and folds into neat edges. It cushions less, so it suits lighter products.

### F flute
A micro-flute for premium packs: cosmetics, jewellery and electronics retail cartons. It is close to a thick paperboard in feel, with corrugated stiffness.

## Combining flutes in 5-ply and 7-ply

Double and triple wall boards combine flutes to balance print, cushioning and strength.

- **B + C** is a common double wall: the B flute faces out for better print, the C flute adds height and stacking strength.
- **C + B + C** is a typical triple wall.
- Other combinations (such as A + C or B + E) exist for special jobs. Ask your supplier what they run.

The designer defaults to B for 3-ply, B + C for 5-ply and C + B + C for 7-ply, and lets you change the flute.

## How to pick a flute

| Your priority | Start with |
|---|---|
| General shipping box | C |
| Printed or die-cut box, or heavy cans and jars | B |
| Premium retail look, light contents | E |
| Luxury pack with a board-like feel | F |
| Extra cushioning and a tall, stacked box | A or a double wall with C |

Then check three things:

1. **Weight and stacking.** Taller, stronger combinations carry more.
2. **Print.** Fine flutes and white liners show detail best: see [logo and artwork on boxes](/blogs/logo-artwork-on-boxes).
3. **Board thickness and inside size.** A thicker board makes the outside bigger, because [inside dimensions stay fixed](/blogs/box-measurements-guide).

## Common mistakes

- **Treating flute height as the only strength factor.** Paper grade (GSM and BF) matters just as much: see the [GSM guide](/blogs/gsm-calculation-strength).
- **Choosing a fine flute for heavy goods.** E and F are for light contents.
- **Choosing A for a printed box.** The wavy face makes fine print harder.
- **Not asking what you are getting.** Write the flute into the spec: "5-ply, B + C flute".

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common flute in India?
Most plain shipping cartons use B or C flute, and 5-ply boxes commonly combine them. Ask your supplier what they stock.

### Which flute prints best?
Fine flutes such as E and F have the flattest surfaces and print best. B is a good all-round printing flute.

### Does a thicker flute always mean a stronger box?
No. Edge crush strength also depends on paper grade and board construction. Taller flutes add cushioning and thickness, but a stack-loaded box is mostly about ECT and thickness: see [will my box stack?](/blogs/box-stacking-strength-guide).

### Can I mix flutes in one board?
Yes, in double and triple wall. B + C is the usual double wall.
